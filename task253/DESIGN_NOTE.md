## Stump sentence

A competent analyst fits the analysis standard's three-level model, reads each school's predicted underlying average and commissions 28 places for the schools whose prediction is below 480, where the system needs 42; the step that lands it there is counting point predictions, which the model pulls toward the system's mean and which are therefore less spread than the schools' underlying averages, instead of adding up each school's probability of an underlying average below 480.

## Three rules

Domain test: the call turns on school-effects estimation, education research's own decomposition of NAPLAN results into student, year-group and school levels and its use to say how a system's schools are spread on underlying attainment; with another field's nouns there is no year group sitting as one class whose luck turns over every year, no NAPLAN scale or participation codes, and the small-school noise that decides the count has no counterpart, so the task stops reading the same.
Analyst test: an analyst who parses the three sittings correctly, keeps the participating students, fits the filed three-level model and counts the schools whose predicted underlying average is below 480 commissions 28 places, and one who skips the model and counts three-year averages below 480 commissions 56; both have read every field and rule correctly and miss the 42 the reviews will find, because the number of schools below a floor is the sum of each school's probability of lying below it, not a count of estimates on either side of the line. `stump_class: analytical`.
Layer 1: three NAPLAN extracts whose layout changes across the sittings (coded legacy fields, a portal workbook with a title block and a sheet per year level, a platform export with renamed columns and spelled-out participation), participation codes under which only participating students carry a scale score, an August enrolment census by year level run from the live student system in November, after some schools had already moved to the 2027 school year, school codes held as integers with leading zeros dropped in the census and the distance table, and a facilities service directory whose servicing-hub field lags the hubs' 2027 visit plan, which the hub coordinators keep by school name and locality. None of it sits on the main call's rows in a way that can move the call (Closure table, axes 15, 16 and 19); the census, the service directory, the visit plan and the distances sit on the ask paths only.
Layer 2: the estimand is a count of schools by a latent quantity, and the easy methods count estimates (raw three-year averages, which over-count because sampling noise widens them; the model's predictions, which under-count because shrinkage narrows them; or schools confidently below, which under-count further), where the count has to be estimated as the sum of the schools' probabilities under the fitted model. A school-effects analyst recognises both easy counts as wrong for a population count; the literature on ensemble estimation of school or provider effects (posterior means are under-dispersed, so they misstate how many units lie past a threshold) is the field's own warning.

## Strongest-solver test

The most careful path a top solver takes: it parses the three sittings, fits the standard's model by REML, notices that 56 schools' raw averages but only 28 predictions sit below 480, recognises the predictions as shrunk, and then either counts the schools whose predicted average is below 480 (28), or the schools whose posterior probability of being below exceeds a half (28, the same set), or the schools confidently below (7 at a 90 per cent bound), each a classification of every school followed by a count of the classifications. It still files the wrong number because classifying each school and counting the classes estimates the number of predictions below the line, not the number of schools below it: only treating the count as a quantity to estimate, the sum over the 264 schools of each school's probability, gives 42. What the build cannot close: a solver who asks how many schools the reviews will actually find, and adds the probabilities, lands the call; the solver round measures how often. The hardening reserve is written down for that round (`## Stopping rule`): the year-group level left to the data rather than named by the standard, so that the two-level probability sum (45.5) and the two-level point count (36) become live misses for a solver who adds probabilities but pools the sittings, paid for with the estimator forks recorded in `## Tried and rejected`.

## Determinism

The model (stage 3 pack, REML): a NAPLAN Year 5 reading score is the system mean (496.5) plus the school's effect (SD 16.5) plus its year group's effect at that sitting (SD 9.3) plus the student's own deviation (SD 69.9). Given its three year groups' means and sizes, each school's underlying average has a normal distribution under the fitted model: its precision is one over the school variance plus, for each year group, one over (the year-group variance plus the student variance divided by the group's size); its mean is the system mean plus the precision-weighted deviations of the year-group means, divided by that precision. Its probability of lying below 480 is a normal tail probability, and the expected number of schools below 480 is the sum of the 264 tail probabilities. The estimator is unique once standard 3.2 fixes REML; stage 3 asserts that two algorithms (a profiled likelihood search and an EM or Fisher-scoring iteration) reach the same variance components to 1e-6.

## Literal reader
