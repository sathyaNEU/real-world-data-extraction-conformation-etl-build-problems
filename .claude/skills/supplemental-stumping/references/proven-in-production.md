## Part 1. The two arithmetic laws, both measured

Both decide whether the ask layer can clear the bar **before any device is designed**, and both were
paid for by a build that failed on arithmetic with a working trap.

### A1. Reachability, not count, decides the bar  **[M]**

**task58 v15.** A response that lands the main call banks

```
score  =  0.30  +  0.60 x c        c = share of ask weight reachable FROM the landed main call
```

With 13 of 24 asks reachable the first draft scored **62.5 per cent** and the bar was unreachable
however hard the trap was. **task58 v1 is the same finding from the other side:** roughly **75 per
cent of ask weight downstream of the main discovery**, so a landed call scored 75 to 95, and one good
draw in twelve fails the top-two bar. Count `c` on paper before the pack is cut.

### A2. The denominator is a lever the ladder is not  **[M]**

**task60 v7, against a real generated rubric.** Rubric at **87 weight points**, two responses at
**65 and 32**. Adding N asks both fail gives

```
average  =  97 / (2 x (87 + N))       under 50 per cent from N = 11
```

(The same pair averages 55.7 per cent at N = 0, so it needs N = 11 to get under the 50 per cent bar
and N = 35 to reach the build target of 40. On a build's own numbers, solve the same formula for
0.40.)

It cut the free block from roughly 27 of 45 criteria to roughly 20 of 57. task60 reached for it
because the main ladder was **measured out**: every basis in the register landed within 4.8 per cent
of the answer, so no method error could clear the separation floor. **This is the move when the
ladder cannot be deepened, and then it is the only move that works.**

## Part 2. Inheriting asks, as actually built

> SKILL.md does not build these (its Part 2 says why). Every block below is coupled to the main call,
> so the response that cracks the main trap banks it and the best response that missed it loses it,
> which costs the pair the block's full weight. Kept as the record of what coupling buys, and as the
> model for a narrow inheriting ask when the author asks for one.

**The reference instance, task74.** *"The size-scale grid is computed at pattern grain, so a response
holding the per-colourway reading assigns each short colourway to the smaller nested scale it matches
and returns a different grid entirely: different rows, different cells and a different ordering. That
is 16 separately graded figures plus the chart's own ordering and annotation moving together on one
analytical choice."* Sixteen criteria, one decision, no partial credit, and it never asks the same
question twice.

**The population walk as an inheriting block.** task74 and task80 both ship the ladder itself as a graded
ask: every intermediate population, in order, with the running figure named. task74's runs 6,200 ->
5,780 -> 11,350 -> 12,950 -> 9,928 across eleven lines with variant counts beside every one; task80's
runs 10,938 -> 3,655 -> less 608 -> plus 2,148 -> 5,195. **A solver at the wrong rung gets the whole
walk wrong, line by line**, so the block grades the ladder rather than the answer.

**task60, one rule deciding four graded columns**, each with a distinct natural wrong value:

| Graded column | Correct | Natural wrong construction |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrators with no remaining exposure | 4,789 counted once each | 5,231 counted once per cluster |
| Concentrators already holding an approved credential | 7,152 inside the certification | 7,851 counted once per district and cluster |

**Keyed-to-the-recommendation asks, all determinate.** task83 prices the plan at **150 and 250** sends
(23.3 and 29.9 stops) beside the committed 200, plus the unfunded-reader count and value, plus the
re-entry thresholds (*"B3 re-enters when its first-qualification return clears the last band funded,

fall to before the release drops a replicate. task78 spreads the committed figure across four quarters
and six regions. task72 drills the decisive allocation twice, by region and by site. **Every one of
these is a sensitivity or a decomposition of the committed call, and every one is fully forced.**

### The device class the doctrine does not name: deliberate, stated non-additivity  **[S]**

**Four builds ship it and it is the signature device of the ask layer.** A count that deliberately
does not sum, with the reason stated in the deliverable. A solver who forces every total to tie is
wrong, and getting it right requires understanding why it does not.

- **task60**: *"it makes the concentrator column of three deliverables fail to sum to its own total,
  because a student planned into two career clusters is one concentrator in each. Anyone who counts
  rows gets a self-consistent set of answers that are all wrong together."*
- **task81**: *"A firm with sites in both tiers stands in both rows, which is why the firm counts do
  not add to 471"*, and the `by_industry` sheet *"describes the register rather than the run and so
  does not reconcile to the payment schedule"*, and the region row does not equal the sum of the four
  districts because units are counted at the business number.
- **task80**: the district row's students-reached figure is *"distinct students rather than the column
  total."*
- **task64**: `reattributed_reports` is deliberately double-counted, *"counted with the account on each
  side of the move, 114,322, which is the record's 57,161 transferred reports carried once by the
  account they were billed against and once by the account holding the branch now."*

**Its power is that it inverts the solver's strongest habit.** A careful response checks that things
tie; here tying is the error, and the stated reason is what makes the correct answer forced rather
than arbitrary.

### Four more devices worth naming, each shipped

are removed, the withdrawn invoices excluded and the transferred branches put back against the account

**Disjointness asserted so a breakdown is order-independent.** task74's four usability tests:

rather than by wording, and it is what lets a 9-by-4 grid ship as 36 determinate criteria.

**Sentinels in five forms at once.** task74: `TBC`, `N/A`, a bare dash, an empty value and
`0000-00-00` all fail the usability test. A solver handling four of the five lands on a distinct wrong
count per supplier.

## Part 4. Separation, as actually proven

**Prove it where a reviewer can rerun it.** The write-up carries no separation argument, so the proof
lives in the generator's zero-counts and in the deliverable script's own control assertions, below.

**Assert it with zero-counts.** task64 v13: *"Every device in the shipped pack is inert on the main
call ... 64 branch transfers with 0 in 2027Q3 and 0 crossing a plan code, 26 account plan changes all
before 2027-01 with the register current."* Zero-counts on the main call's own window and grain are
the cheapest inertness proof there is and they are checkable by inspection.

**Ship the audit inside the deliverable.** task80's script carries seven named control assertions:
uniqueness of the enrolment of record, site figures adding to the district figure, no site holding
more determinations than students, at most one criterion year per site, every open year inside a
recovered designation, any referral year inside a term traceable to a late-settled notice, and
attendance short for at most one provider. **Those are the separation and fairness proofs made
visible to the reviewer at no cost to difficulty**, because each one is true on the correct path only.

**The cost, stated.** task64 v13 also records the consequence: *"any fresh rung requires recutting the
pack."* A perfectly separated device layer cannot later be mined for main-ladder difficulty. That is
the trade and it is the right one.

## Part 6. Twelve checks this corpus says to run

1. **Count reachability first** (A1). Ask weight reachable from the main call above about a third puts the bar out of reach whatever the devices do.
2. **Score the ceiling against the pack as cut**, then grep the whole bundle for every device's own vocabulary. 76 v1 and 65 v1 both had good devices and both died here.
3. **Run the habitual hygiene battery on every wrong path** and assert it comes back clean: no orphans, no duplicate keys, no count mismatch, no null joins. 73 v2 died on this.
4. **Give every device a stated wrong value or a per cent.** 65's 24,247.8 and 5,715.9 against 19,081.6, and its 22.4 per cent, are law 4 discharged rather than claimed.
5. **Make at least one device break on the hygiene repair as hard as on the carelessness**, like 65's reissued codes.
6. **Name one referee and only one** per device or per set: 63's four per-device manifests, 69's digest.
7. **Make a single device move a whole block**, the way 74's sixteen-cell grid moved on one choice, but on a device rather than on the main ladder: 74's grid was an inheriting block, which SKILL.md does not build.
8. **Ship a population walk as a graded block only off the main ladder**, a walk through a device's populations rather than 74's and 80's walks down the main ladder, which are inheriting blocks SKILL.md does not build.
9. **Use deliberate, stated non-additivity.** 60, 64, 80 and 81 all do. It inverts the solver's habit of making things tie.
10. **Engineer one reconciliation column that reads zero only on the correct path**, like 64's `meter_to_billed_gap`.
11. **Buy density inside three files**, not with a fourth: a code deliverable printing numbered figures (64's 36, 80's seven assertions), a multi-sheet workbook with one sheet that deliberately does not reconcile (81), a one-choice grid (74). Twelve builds in this corpus used four to eight files and cannot be copied as they stand.
12. **Assert disjointness wherever a breakdown could be counted two ways**, like 74's four tests, so the grid is order-independent and every cell is determinate.
