## Default path

The pipeline a competent toxicovigilance analyst builds without thinking, with the field's own method for the deciding
quantity in it (exposures per units sold, the base poison-center literature uses for product comparisons):

1. Read CRP-3: the effect is the twelve months' accidental under-six exposures against the number the product's
   packs would have produced with the previous packaging, the largest effect at 1.20 or more is referred; take the
   eight changes and their first-retail-sale dates from the register.
2. Count each product's accidental exposures of children under six by calendar month of the recorded call from the
   case export (reason Unintentional - general, patient_age_years under 6, the product's product_code).
3. Total each product's units by month from the revised point-of-sale release (UPCs and channels summed).
4. Take the expected count as the exposures per pack sold in the twelve months before the change month times the
   packs sold in the twelve months after; the effect is exposures over expected, the after-rate over the before-rate.
5. Refer the largest effect at 1.20 or more.

- **Ends on:** Dozinox Nighttime Sleep-Aid Doxylamine Succinate Tablets referred at 1.5 (Feverkind Junior Strength
  fourth at 1.1).
- **Golden:** Feverkind Junior Strength Acetaminophen Oral Suspension referred at 1.3, runner-up Embercalm Nicotine
  Polacrilex Lozenge Mint at 1.1, gap 0.2.

## Three rules

- **Task type:** safety-signal-detection in Pharmacology, Pharmacoepidemiology & Safety, which serves Anomaly
  Detection & Diagnostics: separating a real change in the risk a product carries after a product event from movement
  the exposure base explains.
- **Domain test:** the deciding quantity is each product's observed-to-expected exposure ratio after its packaging
  change, the toxicovigilance signal statistic, whose expectation the field takes from an exposure base, by convention
  the units sold over the same months.
- **Swap:** "refer the road whose resurfacing raised crashes per vehicle-mile the most": the ratio survives the swap but
  the mechanism does not, because a vehicle-mile is consumed in the month it is counted while a pack sold sits in a home
  for months before a child can reach it, and that lag between the base the field counts and the base at risk is what
  decides here.
- **Layer 1:** the network's move to one case system on 10 July 2023 (two case-number formats in one export); call
  times on each member center's local clock across four time zones and daylight saving; four-week periods against
  calendar months, a revised history beside a preliminary latest period; one line per substance with a blank product
  code on second substances; ages in years with months for under-twos; UPCs by pack size, flavor and channel. Each is how
  the producing system writes its records, none is placed to be caught, and no reading of any of them moves the call
  (SF1 to SF10, the R2 checks).
- **Layer 2:** estimation_fit: the expected count needs the exposure base actually at risk, the packs still in homes,
  built from each product's daily purchases with a drain fitted to its own pre-change record and validated on its
  held-out year, where the field's per-units-sold base assumes the packs at risk move with the packs sold.
- **Decisive step:** recognising that a child can only reach packs already in the home, so the expected count must
  come from the packs in use that each product's past purchases left there, drained at the product's own pace, rather
  than from the packs sold in the same months.
- **Not semantics:** every field, code, unit and definition read correctly (the procedure's population, window, month
  and ratio, the provider's periods and releases, the export's reasons, ages and clocks) still leaves the
  per-units-sold expectation on Dozinox at 1.5; no file names packs in homes, a drain, a use-up time or a sales basis,
  and the ten fork readings each give the same call.
- **Not rule application:** the procedure files the counterfactual only in words ("the number the product's packs would
  have produced in those months had they kept their previous packaging") and no filed rule says how it is built; the
  enumerated family (the 40 flow-based routes in `analysis.cheap_routes`: rates per pack sold over six base windows,
  Poisson models with sales as offset or covariate, trend and month of year over three spans, raw counts, trailing-sales
  totals) spreads Feverkind's effect from 0.82 to 1.46 and refers Dozinox on 20 routes and Embercalm on 20, never
  Feverkind; the standing family the record leaves (the packs-in-use construction under every drain kernel the record
  does not refute, the drain at either end of its 95 per cent interval, monthly or quarterly grain) spans 1.298 to 1.308
  and refers Feverkind; what does the ruling out is each product's own held-out pre-change year, which per pack sold
  predicts 7.6 to 56 times worse in deviance than the construction.
- **Analyst test:** a strong analyst who reads every field and rule correctly divides exposures by units sold, the
  field's base, before and after, and refers Dozinox Nighttime Sleep-Aid at 1.5; one who fits the field's interrupted
  time series with sales and trend instead refers Embercalm at 2.0.
- **Estimand:** CRP-3 issue 4 (`packaging_review/crp_referral_procedure_issue4.pdf`) sections 3.1 to 3.3 and 4: accidental
  (unintentional general) exposures of children under six to the product recorded by the member centers in the twelve
  calendar months beginning the first day of the month after the changed pack was first sold at retail (the register's
  date), divided by the number the product's packs would have produced in those months had they kept their previous
  packaging; the largest effect of at least 1.20 is referred, else none.
- **No decoy:** nothing is planted to be caught: no shipped table carries an effect, rate or ranking, no memo, briefing
  or stakeholder figure argues a product, no rule is withheld; the procedure and the call letter name no watched
  product (R3c), and Dozinox's and Embercalm's pull on the easy routes comes from their own sales records (a blister pack
  that sold fewer packs, a pocket pack that sold more).
- **No certified replay:** no certified or published figure exists for any product's effect, expected count, rate or
  drain; the construction is checked against each product's own raw monthly exposures before its change, which are
  observations to predict, not figures to reproduce.
- **Lesson:** an exposure count follows the stock at risk, not the flow that fills it; when the flow steps at the very
  event being judged, a rate per unit of flow mis-states the event in proportion to the step and to how long the units
  stay at risk, and the stock is rebuilt from the flow with a residence time the record fits; no build on file reads a
  count against a stock its own past inflows built (the 35 spent AD drivers above).

## Survivor anatomy

- **Actor:** the deciding property is each product's packs in use in each month, which no file stores: the case export
  holds exposures, the provider holds packs sold by four-week period, the register holds dates; the stock is a relation
  between many earlier months' purchase rows and the product's use-up pace, and no column holds either.
- **Link:** a month's exposures are read against the purchases of many earlier months, across two files that share
  only the product (the export's product code, the register's code-to-UPC rows, the provider's UPCs) and two calendars
  (calendar months against four-week periods); no key ties a case to a purchase and no sentence invites the link.
- **Reflex:** the field's own base, exposures per units sold before and after (rung 0), refers Dozinox at 1.5; the
  field's interrupted time series with sales and trend refers Embercalm at 2.0; raw counts refer Embercalm at 2.8.
- **Back-test:** this build departs from the anatomy line on purpose. Lesson 12 requires the imputed counterfactual to
  be back-tested, so the record refutes per pack sold at the monthly grain (7.6 to 56 times the construction's held-out
  deviance), and what holds the call is the count of cheap routes reaching it: none of the 40 flow-based routes an
  analyst reaches for after per pack sold fails refers Feverkind (Gate F checks in `checks.py`).
- **Second use:** every file the decisive step reads has its own use: the case export carries the counts every route
  needs, the provider's sales are the field's own denominator in rung 0, and the register gives the dates every route
  needs; the antidote records and the hospital list carry the asks.
- **Shock:** the movement the step recovers reads as ordinary: Feverkind's rise in exposures after its change (37 per

- **Pins:** the WHAT is filed in the Office's referral procedure (population, outcome, window, month, ratio, threshold)
  and no filed sentence names packs in homes, a drain, a use-up time, a sales base or a model; deleting any filed
