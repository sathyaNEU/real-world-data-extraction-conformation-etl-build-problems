## 1. Final Recommendation

**Building permits will add 13,000 net new homes on the lots in the Ridgemont Line transit-oriented area order over 2027 to 2031: 2,800 in 2027, 2,700 in 2028, 2,600 in 2029, 2,500 in 2030 and 2,400 in 2031.**
Each of the 3,701 houses, commercial sites and rental buildings still standing on the order's lots goes only when the building that pays best on it is worth more than what stands there, and then at the pace its kind of lot has kept once rebuilding paid (houses 3.0%, commercial sites 8.5%, rental buildings 2.0% a year), each lot once; at all four stations that building is the six-storey wood frame at 3.0 FAR, because a concrete tower costs more to build than it sells for there, so the 5.0 and 4.0 FAR bands yield no more than the 3.0 band. Not the order's zoned capacity at the take-up the corridors have shown since their rezoning, which counts floor area no one will build and carries the corridors' commercial pace onto the station areas' houses. Not a redevelopment model on the lots' area, assessed values, allowed FAR and use, which reads the order's higher FAR as a reason to rebuild. Not one rate for every feasible lot, which gives the houses the commercial sites' pace.

## 2. Critical Components

1. At the order's densities the building that pays best on every one of the **3,701** standing lots is the six-storey wood frame at **3.0** FAR, and it outbids the assessed land and improvements on all of them.
2. Feasible lots were rebuilt at **3.0%** a year for houses, **8.5%** for commercial sites and **2.0%** for rental buildings over 2014 to 2026.
3. Rebuilt, the standing lots would add **85,906** net new homes.

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

1. Carried the folios in tod_order_schedule_a.xlsx to their current folios through folio_history_2014-2026.csv and kept the houses, commercial sites and purpose-built rental buildings standing on the 2026 roll in roll_extract_2019-2026.csv that hold no counted permit for new construction in building_permits_2014-2026.csv (methodology section 2): 3,701 lots.
2. Priced every building type each standing lot's zone permitted in each roll year 2014 to 2026 (both roll extracts, acres converted; zoning_schedule_2014-2026.csv; the building types, sale values and costs in housing_reference_tables_2026.xlsx) as lot area times its floor area ratio times the net to gross times the sale value, less lot area times its floor area ratio times the all-in cost, and called the lot feasible that year when the best of them exceeded its assessed land and improvements.
3. Counted each feasible lot's first counted permit for new construction as its rebuild (cancelled and expired permits out, a revision once, per the methodology's section 2): houses 490 in 16,349 feasible lot-years, 3.0%; commercial sites 2,016 in 23,723, 8.5%; rental buildings 209 in 10,487, 2.0%.
4. Priced the standing lots at the order's floor area ratio for their band (5.0, 4.0 or 3.0, or the zone's where higher) with the 2026 values and costs: the six-storey wood frame at 3.0 pays best on all 3,701, since concrete costs more than it sells for at all four station areas, and it outbids every lot's assessed value.
5. Took each lot's homes as its net floor area at 3.0 FAR (0.85 of gross) over the 820 square foot average home, rounded down per the reference tables, less the dwelling units on the 2026 roll: 85,906 net new homes.
6. Applied each kind's rate to the lots still standing at the start of each year, each lot rebuilt once: 2,805.4, 2,698.1, 2,596.2, 2,499.1 and 2,406.6 net new homes for 2027 to 2031, 13,005.4 in all.
7. Rounded the years to the nearest hundred and the total to the nearest five hundred: 13,000 net new homes, 2,800, 2,700, 2,600, 2,500 and 2,400.
