# The 2027 engagement dashboard should report three sub-scales

To: Alexander Copeland, Head of Employee Listening
From: People Analytics
Date: 12 November 2026
Re: sub-scales for the 2027 dashboard, from the 2026 survey

The 30 items of the 2026 survey measure three distinct dimensions of employees' views, so the dashboard should carry three sub-scales, one per dimension, with every item reporting under one of them. The item list and loadings are in engagement_subscales_2027.xlsx; the eigenvalues are charted in engagement_dimensions_2026.png.

**Resources (11 items).** Q02 My workload is manageable on a normal shift; Q04 In the past four weeks, was your scanner or till working whenever you needed it; Q07 Enough people are scheduled on my shifts to get the work done; Q09 In the past four weeks, was your schedule posted at least two weeks ahead; Q13 I often have to skip or cut short my breaks; Q14 Do you have the safety gear your job requires; Q19 Were your last three pays correct and on time; Q20 My schedule gives me enough rest between shifts; Q24 Can you get into the scheduling and pay app when you need to; Q27 In the past four weeks, were you able to leave on time at the end of every shift; Q29 Do you know who to call when equipment in your department breaks down.

**Manager and growth (11 items).** Q01 My manager treats me with respect; Q06 Do you have a written development plan agreed with your manager; Q08 My manager keeps me informed about changes that affect my work; Q11 In the past twelve months, have you been offered training beyond what your role requires; Q15 My manager is hard to approach about problems; Q17 In the past twelve months, have you been put forward for a promotion or a lead role; Q21 My manager gives me useful feedback on my work; Q22 In the past twelve months, have you shadowed or covered a role in another department to learn it; Q26 In the past twelve months, has someone outside your store or site talked with you about your career; Q28 In the past four weeks, has your manager talked with you one to one about how you are doing; Q30 Have you been invited to apply to the Brackenmere Leaders programme.

**Connection (8 items).** Q03 I feel part of a team at work; Q05 I get credit for the work I do; Q10 I sometimes feel like an outsider where I work; Q12 Good work in my department usually goes unnoticed; Q16 People here treat each other fairly whatever their background; Q18 The people I work with appreciate what I do; Q23 In the past four weeks, has a coworker helped you without being asked; Q25 In the past four weeks, has anyone thanked you for your work.

## How the three were found

13,717 employees answered at least one item before their site's close. Each answer is a coarse cut through a continuous view: a yes or no question cuts it once, an agreement statement four times. That matters here because the tools questions (Q04, Q09, Q14, Q19, Q24, Q29) are answered yes by 93.7 to 95.0 per cent of respondents and the growth questions (Q06, Q11, Q17, Q22, Q26, Q30) by 4.5 to 6.7 per cent. Correlated as scores, two cuts that sit far to one side can only correlate strongly with cuts on the same side, so the tools questions correlate mostly with each other and the growth questions with each other, whatever they measure. We therefore estimated the correlation of the views behind each pair of items (tetrachoric for two yes or no questions, polychoric otherwise; two-step maximum likelihood, pairwise, the four negatively worded statements reversed, does-not-apply left out).

The eigenvalues of those correlations are 9.75, 6.01 and 2.21, then 0.78, 0.76 and 0.75. Random data of the same size (13,717 respondents by 30 items, 200 samples) gives a 95th percentile of about 1.07 at the fourth dimension, so the first three stand above it and the fourth does not; eigenvalues above one, Velicer's MAP and the scree all give three as well. A three-factor solution (principal-axis factoring, promax rotation) puts every item under one factor, and minres or maximum-likelihood extraction with varimax, oblimin or geomin rotation gives the same placements.

## Why not five

Run on the answers as scores (Pearson correlations), the same test keeps five dimensions: the eigenvalues are 5.59, 3.93, 2.15, 1.49 and 1.34, the fourth and fifth above random data's 1.07 and 1.06. The two extra dimensions are the tools questions and the growth questions on their own, split from the workload and manager statements they move with. They are the lopsided splits, not separate views: on the latent correlations the tools questions load with workload and the growth questions with the manager statements, and nothing is left over for a fourth or fifth dimension. Five sub-scales would have managers write action plans for two scores that mostly record where the yes line sits.

Employees are counted once, under the district of the store they were assigned to in HR on 14 September. Printed forms count by the date their store sealed the envelope (every envelope was sealed by 27 September), not the date Kestrava keyed them; an employee's kiosk sessions are taken together; a does-not-apply answer counts as an answer. Answers given after a site's 23:59 close but before Kestrava's links stopped at 06:00 UTC on 28 September are left out under section 3.1 of the reporting standard. Distribution centres and head office are outside the 12 districts.
