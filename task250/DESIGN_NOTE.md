## Default path

1. Read QS-HS section 6 (the 85 in 100 line over responding households of two or more adults, the next birthday
   after the date on the first letter), the selection protocol (the instruction's wording on each letter and on the
   paper cover, the household grid's sex and year of birth for each member aged 16 or over, the confirmation item)
   and the returns (household_returns_2026.parquet, one kept return per household).
2. Take each survey and mode's responding households of two or more adults and read the confirmation item: the
   respondents who said they were the next-birthday adult (94 to 99 per cent in every stream on the prototype).
3. Check it against the household grid the way the field checks a selection rule without a full roster: under the
   rule every adult in a household is equally likely to be chosen, because birthdays are unrelated to sex and age, so
   the respondents' share of women and their mean age should match their households' adults; move every stream
   whose respondents depart from their households by sex or age beyond chance.

Ends on: move to the grid every stream whose respondents skew from their households by sex or age (ten of the twelve
on the prototype, every survey's web and paper stream but Cultural Participation's), keeping Cultural Participation's
two; the generator measures the set and writes it here.

Golden: move to the grid the streams whose share of households with the next-birthday adult answering is below 85,
read from the respondents' days between their household's first letter and their own next birthday against the order
statistics of the adults' seasonal birthday distribution at that letter's date for a household of their size (seven
on the prototype, among them Cultural Participation on paper, and not Health and Wellbeing on the web, Safety and
Victimisation on paper, Time Use on paper or Working Life on paper).

## Stump sentence

A competent analyst judges each survey and mode by its respondents set against the adults on their own household
grids and by the confirmation item, and moves to the grid every stream whose respondents skew from their households
by sex or age; whom a household substitutes follows the survey's topic, not how often it substitutes, so that set
moves streams that seldom substitute a sharply profiled adult and keeps the Cultural Participation paper stream, which
often substitutes whoever opens the letter, and only the respondents' days from the letter to their own next birthday,
read against the order statistics of the adults' seasonal birthday distribution at that letter's date for a household
of their size, finds the streams below the line.

## Three rules

Task type: nonresponse-calibration-measurement-error-adjustment (Survey Research & Official Statistics), the client domain the house domain demographic-social-science sits in, a task type the taxonomy tags for Data Quality Monitoring & Alerting; a substituted respondent is nonresponse by the selected adult with a substitute taken.
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each collection stream's within-household selection accuracy, the share of its responding households of two or more adults whose respondent was the adult with the next birthday after the letter date, estimated the way survey methodology tests a quasi-random selection rule, from where the selected adult sits in the rule's own ordering against that ordering's distribution for a household of that size.
Swap: "which hospital wards' discharge surveys were answered by the patient the protocol selects" keeps a response share but loses the next-birthday rule, the household size that sets the order statistic and the seasonal birthday distribution it is read against, so nothing ties an answer to the rule's ordering and the call stops reading the same.
Layer 1: the institute's systems produce it: web and paper exports writing dates and codes their own way, a reserve release posted three weeks after a survey's main letter, households returning in both modes with the first received kept, the grid's year of birth beside the respondent's full date of birth, postal returns logged in the post room's register outside the case management system, case dispositions in two code lists across the system's July upgrade; each is neutral on the call (to be asserted) and none is placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimation_fit: the respondent of record is not the adult the rule selects, and the rule's footprint is the respondent's place in the household's birthday order; the methodological choice is which reference distribution that place is read against, an even spread of birthdays (which holds pooled over the year) or the adults' own seasonal distribution at the household's letter date.
Decisive step: reading each respondent's days from the household's first letter to their own next birthday against the order statistics of the adults' seasonal birthday distribution at that letter's date for a household of their size, so that each stream's share of next-birthday respondents is the mixture weight the record supports, while the respondents' traits against their household grid are left as evidence of whom a household substituted.
Not semantics: every field read right (the instruction's date, the household size, the grid's members, the confirmation item, both modes' date formats) still leaves the analyst with the confirmation share or the respondents' traits against their grid: no field of any return says whether its respondent was the next-birthday adult.
Not rule application: QS-HS files the estimand (the share of the stream's responding households of two or more adults whose respondent was the adult with the next birthday after the letter date, against 85 in 100) and nothing filed says how a return shows it; the enumerated family (the confirmation share, the grid departure by sex and age, the male share in mixed couples, the birthday order statistic against an even spread, against the adults' own distribution) moves from keeping every stream to moving ten, with the even-spread order statistic moving the January and May streams across the line both ways; the standing family after the data are read (the birthday distribution is not even; the substitutes' traits follow the topic) is the order statistic against the adults' own distribution, its spread across likelihood and moment estimators and the pooled or the stream's own single-adult null measured on the pack.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who cleans the returns and reads QS-HS and the protocol correctly takes the confirmation item checked against the household grid and moves every stream whose respondents skew from their households by sex or age (ten of twelve on the prototype); on the generator's clean truth the same route files the same wrong set and only the birthday order statistic against the adults' own distribution at the letter date files the golden (checks.py R-block, to be written).
Estimand: QS-HS section 6 (qs_hs_household_surveys_issue3.pdf): a survey and mode keeps the next-birthday instruction for the following year where the respondent was the adult whose birthday came next after the date on the household's first letter in at least 85 in 100 of its responding households of two or more adults aged 16 or over.
No decoy: nothing is planted: the confirmation item is the programme's ordinary log-in and cover question and is answered as respondents answer it, the household grid is the programme's weighting grid, substitution is what households do with a letter addressed to one of them, and no memo, earlier review, draft or stakeholder figure rates or ranks a stream.
No certified replay: the method is not recovered by reproducing a published figure: no file states a stream's accuracy, and nothing filed is reproduced to recover the reference distribution.
Lesson: a quasi-random selection rule's accuracy is read from where the chosen member sits in the rule's own ordering, and the reference for that place is the ordering's distribution at the group's own reference date, never an even spread that holds only pooled over the year nor the chosen members' traits, which record whom was substituted and not how often; no build on file reads a selection rule's footprint.
