Since spring the share of our mobile page views that miss the service line has climbed on all three titles. Five changes shipped in that stretch: the header-bidding ad stack, Bølge's new front end, the image pipeline, the consent banner and the brand fonts. The quarterly service review on 17 September gives the operations squad its Q4, and the squad fixes one of them. Ove Schmidt blames the banner. I run digital operations, so the call is mine.

Tell me which change the squad fixes and how many mobile views a month that fix brings back under the line, to the nearest ten thousand, with the closest other change and its figure on the same footing.

That call goes to the review as q4_squad_call.pptx, stated on the first slide. Then one chart: a horizontal bar per change for the views its fix brings back, largest first, the chosen change and the runner-up labelled with their figures, a line at the most any fix the squad has already closed brought back, and a title naming the change we fix.

The workings behind the number go in q4_squad_call_cohort_effects.xlsx, in views a month to the nearest thousand. For each of Bølge's six template groups, what fixing the ad stack and fixing the front end would each bring back there. For the banner, the image pipeline and the fonts, what each fix brings back on each title. The ad-revenue committee will ask about page weight, so every change on every title with the kilobytes it added to the average crawled mobile page, to one decimal. And for each month from March to August, the image requests and image kilobytes our CDN served per mobile page view, also to one decimal.
