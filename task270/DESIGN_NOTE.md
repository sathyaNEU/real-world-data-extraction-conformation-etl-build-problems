## Stump sentence

A competent analyst correlates each change's measured effect on each candidate with its measured effect on retained_m6 across the 489 changes, disattenuates for each effect's sampling error, and adopts sessions in days 1 to 14 (0.89 disattenuated, 0.39 raw); the step that lands them there is treating each change's two sampling errors as independent, when both effects are measured on the same users and sessions and active days follow retention user by user (0.46 and 0.54 among the 2025 holdout's users), so the errors' shared covariance (65 per cent of sessions' covariance across the changes) is counted as shared change; with it taken out, first purchase within 14 days leads at 0.54 against sessions' 0.31.

## Three rules

Task type: Product Analytics, `metric-definition-measurement-science`: a metric's validity as a proxy for the outcome it stands in for, judged across a corpus of randomized experiments (trial-level surrogacy); it serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis (the joint distribution of the changes' effects on the candidate and on the outcome).
Domain test: the deciding quantity is the association between each candidate's and six-month retention's effects across randomized experiments, the field's own criterion for choosing a proxy metric, estimated from per-arm scorecards in which each experiment measures both effects on the same users, so their sampling errors covary by the users' own correlation over the arm sizes; with another field's nouns there is no corpus of hundreds of randomized experiments each reading a short-term metric and a long-term outcome on the same users.
Swap: with hospitals for the app and readmissions for retention the sentence still reads, but nothing there runs 399 randomized tests on one population with per-arm scorecards of a two-week metric and a six-month outcome; the decisive covariance is an artefact of that measurement design, so the identification needs online experimentation's own records.
Layer 1: the platform's scorecards computed again on 15 September 2025 for 23 experiments after delayed iOS events, both computations in the export; metric keys renamed on 3 March 2025 (677 rows under former keys); 11 closed experiments that failed the sample-ratio check; 15 stopped early; 122 three-arm experiments read arm by arm against a shared control; arm labels as each team named them; 100 experiments whose six-month read is still open; each written by the system named, none placed to be caught, each mishandled leaving the call where it is (R2 rows in `checks.py`).
Layer 2: `estimation_fit`, own:proxy effects correlated with outcome effects after each experiment's shared sampling covariance is taken out: each change's two effects are measured on the same users, so the correlation of the measured effects mixes the changes' co-movement with the errors' co-movement; disattenuating for each effect's own error variance (R1) removes the variances and keeps the covariance, which no scorecard column shows.
Decisive step: take each change's sampling covariance between its effect on the candidate and its effect on retained_m6 (the 2025 holdout's user-level correlation times each arm's two standard deviations over its users, summed over the arms) out of the covariance of the effects across the changes, with the sampling variances out of the variances.
Not semantics: every field, key and definition read correctly (the standard's population, each treatment arm a change counted once, the latest computation, the former keys, the registry's control arm, retained_m6) leaves the raw and the disattenuated correlations on sessions; no definition says that a change's two effects share their errors.
Not rule application: the standard files the estimand (the correlation of the changes' effects, an effect being the difference a change makes to a metric's mean across all new users, each change once) and nothing on estimating it; executing it on the measured effects files sessions, and what rules that out is a covariance no document states, reached only by setting the holdout's user-level correlations against the scorecards' arm sizes.
Analyst test: A strong analyst who cleans every record and applies every filed rule, correlates the changes' measured effects and disattenuates each correlation for the effects' sampling error, adopts sessions in days 1 to 14 (0.89, against first purchase's 0.60); executed on the generator's clean truth in `checks.py` (R3a ANALYST), where taking out the shared covariance alone files first purchase (R3b DECISIVE).
Estimand: standard 2.2 to 2.4 of proxy_metric_standard_2027.pdf: the correlation, across the onboarding changes tested since 1 January 2024 whose six-month read had closed by the scorecard export of 23 October 2026 (each treatment arm against its control one change, each counted once, sample-ratio failures out, early stops read on their users), between the changes' effects themselves on the candidate and on retained_m6, an effect being the difference the change makes to the metric's mean across all new users, which an experiment measures on the users it enrols (2.3); the highest correlation is the proxy.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: no memo, earlier review, dashboard or stakeholder figure ranks the candidates or argues for one; the catalogue gives definitions only, and the 2026 reading guide the standard replaces is not in the pack.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure carries a candidate's correlation, an effect's reliability or a covariance, so the method cannot be recovered by reproducing one.
Lesson: when two outcomes are measured on the same units in each of many trials, their estimated effects co-move by the units' own correlation, so take that covariance out before reading the effects' co-movement across trials; otherwise the metric that tracks the outcome unit by unit is mistaken for the one that tracks it change by change.

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the standard files the correlation across the past changes between the changes' effects themselves on the candidate and on retained_m6, an effect being the difference the change makes to the metric's mean across all new users, which an experiment measures on the users it enrols (2.3). The raw correlation of the scorecards' differences is therefore the correlation of the measurements, not of the effects (SF14); the records show how far apart they are: each effect's sampling variance is 53 to 71 per cent of its spread across changes, and because a change's two effects are measured on the same users, the holdout's user-level correlations (sessions 0.46, active days 0.54) set against the arm sizes put a shared covariance at 65 per cent of sessions' measured covariance with retention. The correlation of the effects is the correlation of the measured effects with both sampling moments out; a correction of the variances alone keeps the shared covariance, and a correction of the covariance alone divides by the measurements' spread, so neither is the filed quantity. Every equal-weight member computes the same five figures.
