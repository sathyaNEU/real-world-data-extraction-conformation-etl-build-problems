## Three rules

- Task type: fertility-mortality-life-table-estimation in sociology-demography, which serves descriptive-distribution: how old-age survival under 2024 to 2026 death rates was distributed over a network's twelve local government areas.
- Domain test: the deciding quantity is each area's period life expectancy at 85, the expected further years at 85 under the area's death rates at each age; demography estimates it from age-specific death rates, deaths over the population at risk at each age, and here the population at each age inside a group published as one total is the estimate that decides it.
- Swap: "which product lines' policies reaching their tenth year could expect fewer than seven more years in force" with policies for residents: a policy register records every policy's duration to the day, so there is no population published as one total above an age, no earlier full count to follow forward and no registered exits to follow it with, and the census, the estimated resident population and the death registrations the measure runs on stop making sense.
- Layer 1: census night (10 August 2021) against 30 June estimates; deaths by date against year of registration (late-2026 deaths registered in 2027); usual residence coded to SA2, with the registry's own LGA field coded at registration and the 2023 boundary adjustment moving Thistlebank from Merriwold to Wolfram Ranges; residents' deaths registered in other states in a separate ABS file; the 2026 estimates preliminary against revised 2022 to 2025; TableBuilder's randomly adjusted cells and a census night undercount; a missing date of birth on a few registrations with the age at death kept. Each is how the registry, the ABS and TableBuilder release their data, none is placed to be caught, and none moves the call (SF2, SF3, SF6, SF8, SF9, SF12 asserted).
- Layer 2: field_method, each area's population at each age inside the 85-and-over group rebuilt from the 2021 census by following each census age forward with its own deaths since census night and bringing each year's total to the published estimate, rather than splitting the estimate by a census or state age distribution of another time or place.
- Decisive step: rebuilding each area's single-year exposures for 2024 to 2026 by following each 2021 census age forward with the deaths of its own birth window, so the rates run on the group's own age mix in those years rather than the census night mix the split assumes.
- Not semantics: every field, code, date, unit and definition read correctly still leaves the census split, because the estimated resident population is published for the 85-and-over group as one total and no file holds any area's population at a single age above 85 in 2024 to 2026; the population at each age has to be estimated, and reading a field differently does not estimate it.
- Not rule application: the guidelines file what the life table is (single years 85 to 94 and 95 and over, the deaths by date, the estimated resident population at 30 June as the base) and nothing says how a single-year population inside the published group is estimated. Enumerated family (R0 to R3 on the pack): five constructions, five distinct named sets, every construction but the rebuild missing (R1 names two areas, R2 three, R2s one, R0 three with Cobbleton). Standing family (the rebuild's own conventions: the census-night birthday split or none, persons or each sex, a death without a date of birth placed or dropped, a negative remainder kept or floored, half a year or 0.45 lived in the year of death): six members, one named set and every area in the same one-decimal cell, the widest spread 0.04 years. Loudness of the ruling-out: no clause rules the splits out; only the data do (the coast's census 80 to 84 counts 2.5 to 2.6 times its 85 to 89, Spraymouth's 85-and-over estimates falling 3.0 per cent a year from 2021 to 2026).
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who takes every 2024 to 2026 death of a resident aged 85 and over by date and usual residence, interstate registrations included, and splits each area's 85-and-over estimated resident population into single years by its own 2021 census counts names Coldwater Plains, Spraymouth and Windermoor, when the life table on exposures rebuilt from the census and each birth window's deaths names Coldwater Plains, Dunesford, Saltwick Shoals and Windermoor.
- Estimand: each area's life expectancy at 85, the average further years a person reaching 85 would live if the area's 2024 to 2026 death rate at each age held, the rates by single year from 85 to 94 and for 95 and over, each the deaths of the area's usual residents at that age that occurred in 2024, 2025 or 2026, wherever registered, over the sum of its estimated resident population of that age at 30 June 2024, 2025 and 2026, named below seven years on the unrounded figure; filed in the program guidelines, sections 3.1 to 3.4.
- No decoy: nothing is planted: the retirement arrivals, the cohorts that left young and the earlier arrivals now in their nineties are each area's ordinary history as the census and the registry record it, and no document states a life expectancy, ranks the areas or describes how the measure was computed before.
- No certified replay: no published or certified life expectancy, life table or single-year population above 85 ships for any area, so nothing recovers the method by reproducing a figure.
- Lesson: a population published as one total above an age has its own age mix, and splitting it by a census or state distribution assumes the mix of another night or place; where the cohorts reaching the group's lower bound changed in size since the census, each age's population has to be rebuilt by following the census ages forward with their own deaths. task228's oldest-old screen rebuilt a register's over-coverage from extinct cohorts and task173's screen sized cohort-size exposure bias on planning totals, and no build on file estimates the single-year population inside a published open group.

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the prompt and the guidelines give the golden: the life table runs by single year from 85 to 94 with 95 and over as the last group, a person who dies at an age living half of that year and the last group's years its survivors over its rate (3.2); each rate is the deaths of the area's usual residents at that age that occurred in 2024 to 2026, wherever registered, over the sum of the area's estimated resident population of that age at the three 30 Junes (3.3); the ABS publishes that population for the 85-and-over group as one total, so each age's share has to be estimated, and the record estimates it by following each 2021 census age forward with the deaths of its birth window, each year's total brought to the published estimate; life expectancy at 85 below seven on the unrounded figure names Coldwater Plains, Dunesford, Saltwick Shoals and Windermoor (3.1, 3.4). The census and state splits read the same sentences and estimate each age's population from a mix the record shows has moved: the census counts the cohorts that cross 85 by 2026 at 80 to 84 and the deaths since census night take out of each census age only its own birth window's dead. The call is retrospective by design: the program is allotted on the latest complete death rates and acts on them from 2028.
