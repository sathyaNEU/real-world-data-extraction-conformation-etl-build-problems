## Stump sentence

A competent solver fits each clinic's decline as the slope of its patients' ACE-III scores on time, per patient or in a random-slopes mixed model, and files Thurlwick and Penwardine as the two fastest-declining clinics, Penwardine in place of Quarndale, because every patient's second completed sitting is lifted by having sat the test before, which flattens a slope most where patients have sat it only two or three times; Quarndale opened in April 2024, nearly all of its patients have sat the test twice, and its decline, the fastest of the eight, reads as the slowest. Only separating the second sitting's one-time gain from each patient's trend puts Quarndale in the pair.

## Three rules

Task type: longitudinal-growth-curve-modeling (Psychology & Behavioral Science), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis in the taxonomy.
Domain test: the quantity that decides the call is each clinic's rate of cognitive decline, which neuropsychology estimates from serial testing net of the practice gain a repeated test carries at its first retest; the trial needs patients whose disease will move the endpoint, and the gain is the test's, not the disease's.
Swap: "which two substations' transformers lose insulation strength fastest, read from yearly dielectric tests": a dielectric test learns nothing from being taken, a second reading carries no gain, nothing ties a step to the test's number rather than to time, and the separation the call turns on stops making sense.
Layer 1: the memory service's move from CIS to the EPR on 8 January 2024 (two ACE-III exports in different layouts, one form a row against one item a row, dates day-first against ISO with a UTC offset, the hospital patient number against the NHS number, CIS clinic codes against EPR site codes); forms abandoned and sets stopped part-way, recorded with a partial total; sets entered on the wrong record or with a slip and marked entered in error; patients registered twice on CIS, merged in the master index; NHS numbers written with spaces in the index; diagnoses revised over time (a working diagnosis confirmed, mild cognitive impairment become dementia) and the current diagnosis copied onto the EPR at migration; patients whose care moved between clinics when they moved house; Quarndale's catchment taken from Corvale and Mosswick. Each is produced by the trust's own systems, nothing is placed to be caught, and each mishandled keeps the call (R2, asserted).
Layer 2: estimation_fit: a growth rate separated from a one-time measurement gain tied to the sitting's number, identified because the second sitting falls at different times for different patients and some patients' first sittings fall before the window.
Decisive step: estimate the second completed sitting's gain from all patients' sittings (a step at the second completed sitting of the patient's whole record beside each patient's own trend) and read each patient's decline net of it, so a clinic whose patients have sat the test only twice is no longer flattened.
Not semantics: every field read correctly (the sitting's date, total and domain scores, completion, the diagnosis on the record, the clinic, the NHS number across the two systems) still leaves a slope through a patient's scores as the natural estimate of their decline; no field records a gain, and no document says the second sitting is lifted.
Not rule application: the paper fixes whose decline counts, over which window, in what unit and how the clinics compare, and no filed rule or procedure separates a practice gain; executing every stated rule files the slope through every sitting.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who links each patient's sittings across the two systems, keeps the completed ones, reads the diagnosis and the window correctly and fits each clinic's decline as the slope of its patients' scores on time, per patient or in a random-slopes mixed model, files Thurlwick and Penwardine; only separating the second sitting's gain from the trend places Quarndale, whose patients have nearly all sat the test twice, in the pair.
Estimand: paper RC/26/11 (a filed PDF), section 3: "Each clinic's figure is the average, over its patients, of the rate at which each patient's cognition declined between 1 January 2022 and 30 September 2026, in ACE-III points a year, each patient's own rate counting once"; 3.2 the population and the clinic, 3.3 the comparison (the two fastest, compared unrounded, reported to the nearest half point).
No decoy: no memo, draft, briefing or stakeholder figure argues for a pair; the service's 2024-25 audit reports referrals, waits, missed appointments, new diagnoses and caseloads by clinic, another question, and nothing in the pack ranks the clinics on decline.
No certified replay: no published or certified decline is shipped to be reproduced, and the method is not recovered from controls.
Lesson: a repeated test is not a fresh measurement: the second sitting carries a one-time gain from having met the test, so a decline read off a few sittings is flattened, most for the patients newest to the test, and the gain is separated as a step tied to the sitting's number beside a trend tied to time. task262 and task253 teach noise around a persistent level; no build on file teaches a systematic gain tied to a measurement's own repetition.

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the paper files each clinic's figure as the average, over its patients, of the rate at which each patient's cognition declined in the period, in ACE-III points a year, each patient's own rate counting once. A patient's cognition is not recorded directly: each completed sitting's total measures it with the test's own error and, from the patient's second completed sitting on, with the gain the patient carries from having sat the test before, which the records show within patients (2.63 points, every licensed estimator between 2.47 and 2.68) and which no disease produces. The literal quantity is the trend in cognition, so each patient's own rate is the slope of their totals net of that gain; the slope of the totals themselves is the rate of the recorded scores, which for a patient with two sittings is the decline plus the whole gain spread over the interval. A random-effects clinic slope is not the literal unit either: it weights each patient by the precision of their own record and pulls short records toward the clinic, where the paper counts each patient's own rate once.

## Oracles

None. No shipped figure states a decline, a slope or a gain by clinic or for the service, ranks the clinics, or counts sittings by their number. The paper defines the measure; the questionnaire defines its own two figures; the 2024-25 audit reports referrals, waits, missed appointments, new dementia diagnoses and caseloads, and the planning team's file reports residents by district.

**Ask A. For each clinic, the patients with Alzheimer's disease or MCI under its follow-up on 30 September 2026 and how many of them will be 85 or under on 1 January 2027 (8 clinics, 16 figures).**
- H18 home: the questionnaire's B1 and B2, the trial's recruitable pool at each site under its upper age limit.
- Use: the workbook's questionnaire B sheet.
- Path: the EPR episodes (start, end, site), the outpatient appointments (outcome, date), the master patient index (date of birth, date of death, record status), the problem list and the CIS diagnoses (the latest diagnosis), the clinic register: six files, about fourteen columns.
- Primary device (D8, the clock against the cut-off): the EPR ends an episode when the discharge or handover letter goes, a few days to some weeks after the review, so a patient discharged at a review in the weeks before 30 September, or handed to the care home liaison team, still has an open episode in the extract of 2 October.
