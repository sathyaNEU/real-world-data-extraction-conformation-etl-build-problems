# task270 design note

Architecture 3, the slot's last, re-rooted 2026-10-19 (wave 4, Descriptive lane, sathay3012) after architecture 2's sixth stage-4 reader landed the call. Hollinsmere Partnership NHS Foundation Trust runs its Memory Assessment Service from eight clinics. Its research and development unit opens two of them as recruiting sites for a 2027 trial in early Alzheimer's disease, and the trial's sponsor asks for the sites whose patients' cognition declines fastest with their disease, because the trial's 18-month endpoint needs patients who will decline on placebo. The trust's research committee names the two clinics on the decline its patients showed on the ACE-III over 2022 to 2026.

    - 1.2a: the filed quantity is unobserved: cognition is measured only through sittings that carry the gain, so a patient's decline is a latent rate, and the slope through every sitting, a random-slopes mixed model and a slope with the second sitting's gain separated are estimators of it.

In the prototype Quarndale and Thurlwick sit within 0.1 points a year of each other once the gain is separated, so the build widens Quarndale's lead until the pair survives the gain's estimate moving by half a point (to be asserted at stage 3).

## Default path

1. Read the trust's site selection paper: the two recruiting sites are the clinics whose patients' cognition declines fastest with their disease, each clinic's figure the average over its Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment patients with two or more completed ACE-III sittings between 1 January 2022 and 30 September 2026 of each patient's decline in ACE-III points a year.
2. Join each patient's sittings across the two record systems (the old patient record to 2023, the electronic record from 2024) through the NHS number, keep the completed sittings, and place each patient in the clinic that assessed them.
3. Fit each patient's decline as the slope of their ACE-III totals on time across their sittings in the window (or fit a mixed model with random intercepts and slopes per clinic), and average over each clinic's patients.
4. Rank the eight clinics; the two fastest recruit.

Ends on: Thurlwick and Penwardine, with Quarndale's decline among the slowest.
Golden: Quarndale and Thurlwick, with Penwardine the first clinic below.

## Stump sentence

A competent solver fits each clinic's decline as the slope of its patients' ACE-III scores on time, per patient or in a random-slopes mixed model, and files Thurlwick and Penwardine as the two fastest-declining clinics, Penwardine in place of Quarndale, because every patient's second sitting is lifted by having sat the test before, which flattens a slope most where patients have sat it only two or three times; Quarndale opened in 2024, its patients have sat the test at most three times, and its decline, the fastest of the eight, reads as one of the slowest. Only separating the second sitting's one-time gain from each patient's trend puts Quarndale in the pair.

## Three rules

Task type: longitudinal-growth-curve-modeling (Psychology & Behavioral Science), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis in the taxonomy.
Domain test: the quantity that decides the call is each clinic's rate of cognitive decline, which neuropsychology estimates from serial testing net of the practice gain a repeated test carries at its first retest; the trial needs patients whose disease will move the endpoint, and the gain is the test's, not the disease's.
Swap: "which two substations' transformers lose insulation strength fastest, read from yearly dielectric tests": a dielectric test learns nothing from being taken, a second reading carries no gain, nothing ties a step to the test's number rather than to time, and the separation the call turns on stops making sense.
Layer 1: the memory service's move from the old patient record to the electronic record in 2024 (two ACE-III exports in different layouts, dates in day-month-year against ISO, a local patient number against the NHS number, clinics coded differently); sittings abandoned part-way and recorded with a partial total; diagnoses revised over time (mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's disease, a working diagnosis replaced); patients whose assessments moved between clinics when they moved house; appointments not attended and rebooked. Each is produced by the trust's own systems, and nothing is placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimation_fit: a growth rate separated from a one-time measurement gain tied to the sitting's number, identified because the second sitting falls at different times for different patients.
Decisive step: estimate the second sitting's practice gain from all patients' sittings (a step at the second sitting beside each patient's trend) and read each patient's decline net of it, so a clinic whose patients have sat the test only two or three times is no longer flattened.
Not semantics: every field read correctly (the sitting's date, total and domain scores, completion, the diagnosis on the day, the clinic, the NHS number across the two systems) still leaves a slope through a patient's scores as the natural estimate of their decline; no field records a gain, and no document says the second sitting is lifted.
Not rule application: the site selection paper fixes whose decline counts, over which window and in what unit, and no filed rule or procedure separates a practice gain; executing every stated rule files the slope through every sitting.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who links each patient's sittings across the two systems, keeps the completed ones, reads the diagnosis and the window correctly and fits each clinic's decline as the slope of its patients' scores on time, per patient or in a random-slopes mixed model, files Thurlwick and Penwardine; only separating the second sitting's gain from the trend places Quarndale, whose patients have sat the test at most three times, in the pair.
Estimand: the trust's site selection paper (a filed PDF) fixes the population (each clinic's patients with an Alzheimer's disease or mild cognitive impairment diagnosis who completed two or more ACE-III sittings between 1 January 2022 and 30 September 2026), the outcome (how fast each patient's cognition declines with their disease, in ACE-III points a year), the window, the unit (the patient, averaged over the clinic's patients) and the comparison (the two fastest of the eight clinics recruit); the sentence is quoted under `## Literal reader` at stage 2.
No decoy: no memo, draft, briefing or stakeholder figure argues for a pair; the service's own audit reports waiting times and diagnosis rates by clinic, another question, and nothing in the pack ranks the clinics on decline.
No certified replay: no published or certified decline is shipped to be reproduced, and the method is not recovered from controls.
Lesson: a repeated test is not a fresh measurement: the second sitting carries a one-time gain from having met the test, so a decline read off a few sittings is flattened, most for the patients newest to the test, and the gain is separated as a step tied to the sitting's number beside a trend tied to time. task262 and task253 teach noise around a persistent level; no build on file teaches a systematic gain tied to a measurement's own repetition.
