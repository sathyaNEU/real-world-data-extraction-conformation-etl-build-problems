## Three rules

Task type: psychology-behavioral-science / experience-sampling-intensive-longitudinal-analysis, which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis.
Domain test: emotional instability is experience sampling's own estimand, the root mean square of the change in momentary negative affect between adjacent prompts, and it measures change over the interval the prompt schedule sets, so the field reads an instability threshold only at the spacing it was set on.
Swap: "the teams where 30 per cent of patients' negative affect changes by a root mean square of 2.0 between the collaborative's three-hourly prompts" becomes "the buildings where 30 per cent of rooms' temperature changes by 2.0 degrees between three-hourly readings": the prompt schedule, the answering window, the affect items and the clinical cut point stop making sense, and no field sets a cut point on a room's drift at a sampling interval.
Layer 1: the app's 15 June 2026 version change (0 to 100 sliders to 0 to 10 buttons, local time to UTC, a new layout, 30 fortnights split across the two files), patients who re-enrolled on a new phone holding two app codes (15), the two Haddersley teams merged on 1 October 2026, the register's clinician-entered fortnight status against the paper's completion rule (23 fortnights marked Completed short of half the prompts), version 4's offline sync resending answers (630 repeated rows), and the clock change inside 44 fortnights; each is what the trust's own systems produce and none is placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimation fit, estimating each patient's change over the collaborative's three-hour interval from ratings taken at shorter and uneven intervals; reading the change between adjacent 2026 ratings instead takes three teams out of the call.
Decisive step: reading each patient's emotional instability at the three hours between the collaborative app's prompts, from the 2026 ratings about three hours apart or from the patient's fitted curve of mean squared change against the time between ratings, not between adjacent 2026 prompts.
Not semantics: every field, scale, code and the standard's definition read correctly still leaves the easy route computing the change between adjacent 2026 prompts and funding Dunnerby and Marrowcombe alone; no field states how far apart a patient's ratings are against the standard's interval or that the change depends on it, which only the ratings' own change by interval shows.
Not rule application: the standard defines instability on its own app's prompts and says nothing on estimating it from ratings taken on another schedule, so executing every filed rule on the 2026 ratings funds Dunnerby and Marrowcombe; measured on the cut pack, the enumerated family (adjacent prompts, successive answered prompts, successive ratings across the fortnight, a linear stretch to three hours, ratings 2.5 to 3.5 or 2.75 to 3.25 hours apart, ratings two blocks apart, each patient's exponential curve, one time constant per team) runs from two teams to five, while the standing family (the five estimators of the change at three hours the ratings' own curve supports) names the same five in every member, spreading 9.4, 10.2 and 5.4 points of share on Drystow, Cressbeck and Haddersley with all three at 40.8 or more; nothing filed rules out the adjacent-prompt reading, only the ratings' own change by interval does.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads the collaborative's standard and both app exports correctly computes each patient's root mean square change between adjacent answered prompts of the 2026 fortnight, applies 2.0 and funds Dunnerby and Marrowcombe alone, because the trust app's adjacent prompts sat about 90 minutes apart while the collaborative's sit three hours apart and negative affect moves further in three hours.
Estimand: the board paper's section 2 with the collaborative's standard, sections 2 and 3: each team's share of the patients whose latest completed 2026 assessment fortnight began while the team held their care (teams as they stand from 1 October 2026), a patient counted when their emotional instability, as the collaborative's app will measure it, is 2.0 or more; a group wherever the share is 30 per cent or more.
No decoy: nothing is planted; no memo, briefing, draft or colleague argues the adjacent-prompt reading, no file comments on the two apps' schedules side by side, and the trust app's schedule is simply what the app did.
No certified replay: the method is not recovered by reproducing any certified or published figure; the pack holds no instability figure for the trust's patients from any source.
Lesson: an index built from the change between successive readings measures change over the interval the schedule sets, so a threshold set on one schedule is read at that spacing; no build on file reads an index at its own sampling interval (the nearest, task272 v2, scales a level across a seasonal calendar).
