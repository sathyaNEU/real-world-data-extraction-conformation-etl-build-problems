## 1. Final Recommendation

**Fund a DBT skills group in 2027-28 in Dunnerby, Marrowcombe, Drystow, Haddersley and Cressbeck, and in none of the other six teams.**
Read over the three hours between the collaborative app's prompts, 30 per cent or more of each of these teams' 2026 assessed patients reach 2.0. Not Dunnerby and Marrowcombe alone, which the change between the trust app's adjacent prompts gives: those prompts sat about 90 minutes apart and negative affect moves further in three hours, so that reading leaves Drystow, Haddersley and Cressbeck under the line.

## 2. Critical Components

1. Patients counted: **578**, each once on their latest completed 2026 fortnight, in the team holding their care on its first day, Haddersley North and South as one team.
2. The trust app's adjacent prompts sat about **90 minutes** apart against the collaborative's three hours, and the change in negative affect between two same-day ratings grows with the time between them.
3. Each patient's emotional instability is read at three hours, the interval between the collaborative app's prompts, before the **2.0** line is applied.

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

1. Read both app exports on one 0 to 10 scale (version 3's sliders divided by ten, version 4's UTC times put in UK time, its resent rows dropped), joined app codes to patients through mood_app_enrolments_2025-2026.csv, and took each patient's latest 2026 fortnight in assessment_fortnights_register_2026.xlsx with at least 56 of its 112 prompts answered (the committee paper, section 2), in the team cmht_care_episodes_2025-2026.csv shows on its first day: 578 patients.
2. Took negative affect at each answered prompt as the mean of its four items (the collaborative's standard, 3.1).
3. Measured each patient's emotional instability over the three hours between the collaborative app's prompts (standard 2.1 and 3.2) as the root mean square change in negative affect between their same-day ratings 2.5 to 3.5 hours apart.
4. Counted each team's patients at 2.0 or more (standard 3.3): Dunnerby, Marrowcombe, Drystow, Haddersley and Cressbeck reach 30 per cent or more.
5. Counted each team's clinicians whose learning record, under their own or their previous employee number, holds PTD-301, EXT-0412 or PTD-114 completed before 1 April 2025 (the four-day course that code carried until the catalogue rebuild, as the 2024-25 annual report lists it), with a non-administrative assignment in the team of 0.4 WTE or more on 1 April 2027 and no leaving date on or before 16 September 2027 (DBT programme operating standard 4.2), and summed those assignments' whole-time equivalent.
6. Took each patient on the DBT waiting list on 31 December 2026 in cen_referrals_module_export_2027-01-08.csv (accepted for DBT, added by then and not removed by then), dated each referral by the service's acknowledgement letter in cen_correspondence_log_2023-04_to_2027-01.csv (DBT programme operating standard 2.2), the latest for the patient on or before the module's received date through patient_index_extract_2027-01-06.csv, which replaces the date the module stamped on the referrals keyed in from the service's database from 22 June 2026, and averaged the days to 31 December over seven by the team holding the patient's care on 31 December.
7. The committee funds a DBT skills group in Dunnerby, Marrowcombe, Drystow, Haddersley and Cressbeck.
