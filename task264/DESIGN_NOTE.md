## Default path

1. Read the members' resolution: the 2027-28 levy is apportioned in proportion to each member council's residents aged 16 and over who visited at least one of the parks in the 2025-26 survey year, each counted once.
2. Stack the survey's two deliveries; add each respondent's twelve-month visits to the six parks (Q9's six answers) with the codebook; place each home town or village in a council through the Authority's settlement gazetteer; keep the residents of the nine member councils.
3. Join the contractor's expansion weights and sum them by council: the adult visits each council's residents made in the survey year.
4. Turn each council's visits into residents by dividing them by its respondents' average 12-month visit count (the expansion-weighted mean).
5. Apportion the levy in proportion to those residents.

Ends on: the three valley councils pay 25.0 per cent of the levy between them.
Golden: the three valley councils pay 50.7 per cent of the levy between them.

## Stump sentence

The default path charges the three valley councils 25.0 per cent of the levy instead of 50.7 because it turns each council's expanded visits into residents with its respondents' average 12-month visit count, and in an exit survey that average is visit-weighted (a resident who comes two hundred times is two hundred times as likely to be interviewed), so it overstates how often the valley councils' residents come and undercounts them most; the survey's weight over each respondent's visits to the six parks counts each resident once and gives the golden split (over the visits to the park of interview alone it counts a two-park walker twice, 55.5).

## Three rules

Task type: sample-design-allocation-weighting (Survey Research & Official Statistics), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis in the taxonomy; the setting is a regional park authority's funding formula.
Domain test: the deciding quantity is a population total, each member council's adult residents who visited at least once in 2025-26, estimated from a probability sample of visits by the design-based estimator that weights every interview by the inverse of its person's inclusion probability, which an exit survey makes proportional to that person's visits; replacing it with the visit total, or with visits over a visit-weighted average frequency, moves every council's levy.
Swap: "how should a hospital trust's patient-experience budget be split among its nine clinics by the patients each saw" keeps an exit survey and a split, but loses the intercept design that draws each adult in proportion to how often they come, the expansion strata of park, season and day type and the 12-month visit count that set each person's chance of selection, so the estimator the call turns on has nothing to attach to and the task no longer reads the same.
Layer 1: the survey in two contractor deliveries (a spring and summer workbook and an autumn and winter CSV from the contractor's new tablet system) with renamed columns, parks as names in one and site codes in the other, dates as Excel dates and as DD/MM/YYYY text, recoded age bands and interview numbers written three ways (DV25-1-0001, DV25/2/0001 and the weights file's 1-0001); the contractor's expansion weights in a separate file; home areas coded by the contractor, with NK where no home was given or it could not be placed; twelve-month visit counts asked park by park and heaped at round numbers as people give them; and, off the main path, pay-machine payments and counter hours stamped in UTC across British Summer Time, the parking app's statements by month and tariff, and the finance system's cash book booked by posting date beside the bank's report of the same credits by value date. All of it is how the contractor's and the Authority's systems hold their records, and nothing is placed to be caught.
Layer 2: the estimation_fit family; a person total estimated from a sample of visits needs each sampled person weighted by the inverse of their own inclusion probability (the design weight over their visit count), because the exit survey's selection is proportional to how often each person comes; and since the survey intercepts at all six parks, a person's chance of interview runs over their visits to all six (a multiplicity across sites), not only the park where they were met.
Decisive step: weighting each interview by its expansion weight divided by the respondent's twelve-month visits to the six parks, so each resident counts once, instead of apportioning by expanded visits, dividing each council's visits by its respondents' average visit count, or dividing by the visits to the park of interview alone.
Not semantics: every field and document read correctly (the resolution's residents counted once, the weights' adult visits, the visit-count question, the gazetteer) still leaves the analyst choosing how to turn visits into residents, and the natural conversion, visits over the respondents' average visits, is a size-biased average; no field, code or definition says how a sample of visits becomes a count of people.
Not rule application: the resolution fixes what is counted (residents aged 16 and over of each member council who visited at least once in the survey year, each once) and the contractor's note fixes how interviews were drawn and weighted; no filed rule says how to estimate a count of people from them, so executing every stated rule still files the default path's split.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who codes every answer, places every respondent, reads the resolution correctly and expands the interviews by the contractor's weights divides each council's visits by its respondents' average visit count and charges the valley councils 25.0 per cent (Ends on: the three valley councils pay 25.0 per cent of the levy between them); weighting each interview by its weight over its visits to the six parks charges them 50.7. The analyst who sees the size bias and divides each weight by the visits to the park where the interview took place charges them 55.5 per cent, counting a resident who walks in two parks once at each.
Estimand: the members' resolution of July 2026 (in the Authority's levy papers) fixes the population (residents aged 16 and over of each of the nine member councils who visited at least one of the Authority's six parks between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026, whether or not the survey met them), the outcome (the number of such residents, each counted once), the unit (the member council) and the comparison (each council's levy in proportion to its number).
No decoy: nothing is planted; no memo, draft, briefing or stakeholder figure argues a split, the old population basis is the 2026-27 levy as invoiced, and the survey is the Authority's routine visitor survey.
No certified replay: the method is not recovered by reproducing published or certified figures; no file publishes a resident count, a share or a split on the new basis.
Lesson: an on-site intercept survey samples visits, so each person's chance of selection rises with how often they come, and a count of people weights each respondent by the inverse of their own visits to every site the survey covers; the respondents' average visit count is a visit-weighted average that overstates frequency, so dividing visits by it undercounts the most frequent visitors; no build on file teaches frequency-proportional selection.
