## Default path

1. Read the complaint log the district's complaint system exports (`odor_complaints_2026.csv`) for May to September 2026 and form odor events under the district's odor complaint procedure (one household's complaints within two hours of the time the odor was noticed are one event).
2. Average the district's 5-minute wind record at the Harlow Falls station (`hfs_met_5min_2026.csv`, Pacific standard time) into hourly vector means and take each event's hour, converted to standard time.
3. For each event and each facility in the district's odor source list (`odor_sources_permits_2026.xlsx`), compute the bearing from the household to the facility; the facility lies upwind when the hour's wind blows from within 30 degrees of that bearing, and no facility does in a calm hour (the procedure's upwind check).
4. Count, for each facility, the season's odor events it lay upwind of.
5. Take the facility upwind of the most events as the source.

Ends on: the Pine Gulch landfill, upwind of the most odor events of the season (the figure is frozen at stage 3).
Golden: Ridgeway Organics' compost facility.

## Three rules

Domain test: odor source attribution from complaint geometry and the local wind record (the conditional probability of a source lying upwind, and its time-stratified form for event data) is the air agency's own analysis, and the quantity that decides is how much more often a facility lies upwind of complaint hours than of the same hours on other days; with another field's nouns there is no plume bearing from each household, no valley wind turning with the clock and no drainage flow putting one source upwind every night, and the task stops reading the same.
Analyst test: an analyst who cleans the complaint log into the district's odor events, reads the noticed time, vector-averages the 5-minute wind into hours, converts the clocks and applies the 30-degree upwind check correctly still files the landfill by counting upwind events, or the rendering plant by dividing by each facility's upwind frequency over all hours, because both comparisons let the valley's daily wind cycle and the residents' evening reporting stand in for the source.
Layer 1: a complaint log merged from phone intake, the web form and the mobile app (received and noticed times, double submissions, app resubmissions under fresh ids, households filing under more than one contact, entries from outside the district); a 5-minute meteorological export kept in Pacific standard time all year, wind direction to be vector-averaged across north, calms with no direction, a sensor outage with flagged gaps; operators' reports each in its own idiom (a landfill's daily tonnage, a compost facility's monthly report in cubic yards and tons, a treatment plant's biosolids log); household locations geocoded at parcel and at street-segment precision.
Layer 2: the analytical pitfall is confounding by the shared daily cycle: complaint times and wind directions both follow the clock, so the evidence that a facility was upwind during complaints has to be set against the same hours on other days at each household's own bearing; the count of upwind events and the all-hours conditional probability each name a facility the clock favours.
