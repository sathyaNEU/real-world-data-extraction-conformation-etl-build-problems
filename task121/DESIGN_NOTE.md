## Default path

1. Read the trust's request and the published Progress 8 table: Halwick's Progress 8 over the three cohorts is the lowest of the trust's eight academies, about 0.15 below the trust average; Progress 8 compares each pupil's Attainment 8 with pupils nationally who had the same key stage 2 results, so the intake's starting point is already netted.
2. Take the pupils of the trust's eight academies from the borough's transfer file with their attendance (sessions missed against sessions possible) and the disadvantaged flag, and fit the school-effects model the field uses for value added: each pupil's Progress 8 on their attendance and disadvantage with an effect for each secondary (fixed effects, or random effects with the same terms).
3. Walk the gap: attendance and disadvantage each account for their effect times Halwick's difference from the trust average in them, a few hundredths at most; the academy's own effect is Halwick's school effect against the trust's pupil-weighted average, nearly all of the gap; the remainder is nil, because the model's residuals average to zero within each school.
4. Report that the academy's own effect accounts for most of the gap.

Ends on: yes, the academy's own effect, about 0.15 of the 0.15 gap.

Golden: no, the remainder (the legacy of seven primaries), with the academy's own effect near nil.

## Stump sentence

The default path takes Progress 8 at its word that the key stage 2 baseline nets the intake, fits the field's school-effects model of pupils within secondaries with attendance and disadvantage, and ends on the academy's own effect, about 0.15 of the 0.15 gap, because most of Halwick's pupils come from seven primaries whose pupils make less progress than their key stage 2 results predict at every secondary they go to, and a model with one classification books that shortfall to the only school it sees. The borough file follows every pupil from primary to secondary, and a cross-classified model of all fourteen secondaries and the primaries that feed them (fixed or random effects) measures the seven primaries' carried effect on their pupils at the other secondaries, books the remainder of the gap to it and leaves the academy's own effect near nil.

## Three rules

Task type: Education & Learning Sciences, `multilevel-classroom-school-research`: a secondary's gap on a value-added measure split between the school and the pupils it takes in, with pupils classified by both the secondary and the primary they came from; it serves Root-Cause Analysis (whether the academy's own effect caused most of the gap, and what did).
Domain test: the deciding quantity is the academy's own effect on its pupils' progress, how far their Progress 8 differs from what the same pupils would have made at the trust's average secondary, estimated by the field's cross-classified multilevel model of pupils in secondary and primary schools (Goldstein and Sammons 1997; Leckie 2009) against its default, the school-effects model Progress 8 implies, whose precondition is that the key stage 2 result carries everything a pupil brings from primary school.
Swap: rewritten with a hospital's nouns ("whether the hospital's own care accounts for most of its readmission gap, given the practices its patients are referred from"), the sentence survives only as an analogy; the call turns on a value-added baseline taken at a transfer between two phases of schooling, an attainment test sat at the earlier school, and the published Progress 8 method, none of which a hospital's case mix carries.
Layer 1: the borough's transfer file keyed by each school's URN, with primaries that converted to academies mid-window under a new URN and the predecessor only in the borough's establishment register; pupils without key stage 2 results (arrived from outside England, or absent from the tests) carrying Attainment 8 but no Progress 8; the 2022 cohort graded on the transition year's boundaries, so Attainment 8 levels move between cohorts while Progress 8 re-centres nationally each year; attendance as sessions by reason code; disadvantage as the Ever-6 free school meals and care flags; pupils who moved secondary in years 10 and 11 counted at the school on roll in the January of year 11; each written by the system named, none placed to be caught, none deciding the call.
Layer 2: `field_method`, own:primary-legacy-in-secondary-progress-cross-classified: the academy's own effect is estimated with the pupils classified by their primary school as well as their secondary, because pupils carry their primary's effect beyond their key stage 2 result; the two-level model of pupils within secondaries, and Progress 8 read alone, book that carried effect to the academy.
Decisive step: recognise that the key stage 2 baseline does not carry everything a pupil brings from primary school, measure the primaries' carried effect on their pupils at the borough's other secondaries (a cross-classified model of secondary by primary), and take the academy's own effect net of it.
Not semantics: every field, code, unit and definition read correctly (Progress 8's baseline and elements, the attendance codes, the Ever-6 flag, the URNs and their predecessors, the January census rule) still leaves the analyst choosing which classifications a pupil's progress is modelled on, and the transfer file's primary school is a field like any other, defined correctly and used by neither the Progress 8 method nor the school-effects model; the wrong answer reads every field correctly.
Not rule application: the trust's request files the estimand (the academy's own effect as the same pupils' progress at the trust's average secondary, the trust average pupil-weighted over the eight academies, the three cohorts, the walk's four items) and nothing files how a school's effect is estimated or on which classifications. Measured on the prototype world (seed 0, 10,062 pupils): the enumerated family (Progress 8 read alone; two-level fixed effects; two-level random effects; cross-classified fixed and random effects) spans the academy's own effect from -0.149 to +0.037 of a -0.144 gap; the standing family (the cross-classified constructions, the only ones the primaries' variance component admits) spans +0.007 to +0.037, both filing "not the academy's own effect"; what rules the two-level family out is the primaries' effect on their pupils at other secondaries, which no filed clause names and which a reader sees only by classifying the borough's pupils by primary as well as secondary.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every rule, takes Progress 8 as netting the intake and fits the field's school-effects model of pupils within secondaries with attendance and disadvantage files yes, the academy's own effect, about 0.15 of the 0.15 gap; executed in `checks.py` at stage 3, where the cross-classified family files the golden.
Estimand: the trust's request (filed at stage 2 in the director's paper to the trustees): the academy's own effect is how far the Progress 8 of Halwick's pupils in the 2022 to 2024 cohorts differs from what the same pupils, with the same attendance, would have made at the trust's average secondary (pupil-weighted over the eight academies); the walk's other items are attendance and disadvantage, each its effect times Halwick's difference from the trust average, and the remainder.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: the published Progress 8 table is the Department's own and ranks no cause; no memo, head's report, consultant's diagnosis or stakeholder figure names the academy's own effect or the primaries' share; the feeder primaries' legacy is a real effect generated for every secondary they feed, not a defect in Halwick's records; the distractors (an inspection-grade list, a staffing return) rank nothing on the decision.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure carries a school effect, a primary's effect or a component of the gap, so no method is recovered by reproducing one; the published Progress 8 scores are the gap itself.
Lesson: a school's value-added gap is split between the school and its intake only after the pupils are classified by the school they came from as well as the one they are in, because a single attainment test at the transfer does not carry everything the earlier school left them with; no build on file splits a unit's outcome between its own effect and an earlier unit's carried effect through a cross-classified model.
