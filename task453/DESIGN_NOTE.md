## Default path

1. Conform the old system's long export (weighted persons in thousands by labour force status, region under the pre-2025 codes until the review) and the new system's wide export (persons) into one cell table of month, region (2025 codes through the concordance), rotation group, questionnaire, sex, age band, labour force and unemployed, and check that each month's cells reproduce the release workbook's published regional rates.
2. Measure the redesign's effect in each phase-in month, January to May 2026, as the unemployment rate of the rotation groups answering the redesigned questionnaire less that of the groups still on the old one (with region, sex and age band held fixed or not, the same), and average the five months: about +0.9 points.
3. Restate the trigger by the Act's clause: 6.0 plus 0.9, 6.9 per cent.
4. Restate each region's monthly rates for 2024 and 2025 by the effect and the old-questionnaire part of January to May 2026 by its share, average each region's twelve months to December 2026, and designate the regions at or above 6.9 per cent.

Ends on: a restated trigger of 6.9 per cent (a break of about +0.9 points).
Golden: a restated trigger of 6.3 per cent (a break of about +0.3 points).

## Stump sentence

The default path restates the trigger at 6.9 per cent because step 2 reads the redesign's effect as the gap between the rotation groups on the redesigned questionnaire and those still on the old one in January to May 2026, about +0.9 points: the redesign went only to dwellings entering the sample, so that gap sets mostly first interviews against last ones and carries the survey's first-interview bias, and the redesign's dependent interviewing cannot act at a first visit; read within month in sample against the same month in sample before January 2026 and averaged over the six interviews the published rate averages, the break is about +0.3 points and the trigger 6.3 per cent.

## Three rules

- Task type: Survey Research & Official Statistics, seasonal-adjustment-official-time-series-production (official time-series production: carrying a published series across a change in its measurement), which serves Data Extraction & Conformation.
- Domain test: the quantity that decides the call is the redesigned questionnaire's effect on the published unemployment rate, estimated the way a statistics office reads a questionnaire change phased in through a rotating panel: each month-in-sample group on the new questionnaire against the same month in sample before the change, rotation-group bias and the second-interview start of dependent interviewing built in, averaged over the six rotation groups the published estimate averages.
- Swap: "restate the trigger by the points the redesigned questionnaire adds to the published unemployment rate" with hospital nouns reads "restate the readmission threshold by the points the new discharge form adds to the published readmission rate", and the phase-in through incoming rotation groups, the first-interview bias, the months in sample and dependent interviewing have nothing to attach to, so the comparison and its correction stop making sense.
- Layer 1: the office's two collection systems export different layouts and units (the old system's long file of weighted persons in thousands by status, the new system's wide file in persons), the 2025 classification review renumbered the region codes (the office's concordance joins them), the 2022 to 2024 cells exist in their original weights and in the 2025 reweighting to the new population base, rotation groups carry letters while a dwelling's month in sample follows only from the rotation schedule in the methodology note, and the release workbook carries header blocks, footnotes and phase-in markers; each is what the office's own systems produce, and none is placed to be caught.
- Layer 2: estimand_identification, the right comparison group: the break read within month in sample against the same month in sample before the phase-in, and the estimand's average over the six interviews, because the redesign's effect differs at the first interview.
- Decisive step: separating the redesign's effect from rotation-group bias by comparing like interviews (each month in sample on the new questionnaire against the same month in sample on the old one, month and region held fixed) and averaging the six month-in-sample effects as the published rate averages the six rotation groups.
- Not semantics: with every field, code, unit and the rotation schedule read correctly the easy route still files the arm difference (+0.9, trigger 6.9) or one effect with month in sample held fixed (+0.2, trigger 6.2); no field or definition names the role of months in sample or the redesign's effect at a first visit, and the error lies in which comparison estimates the effect.
- Not rule application: the Act fixes what is restated (the points the redesign adds to the published rate, to one decimal place, added to 6.0) and no filed rule or procedure says how the points are estimated; executing the Act's clause on the arm difference files 6.9 and on one effect 6.2.
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who conforms both systems' exports and the rotation schedule correctly and reads the Act's restatement clause takes the redesign's effect as the gap between the rotation groups on the redesigned questionnaire and those still on the old one in January to May 2026, about +0.9 points, and restates the 6.0 per cent trigger at 6.9 (the default path's Ends on); holding month in sample fixed with one effect gives about +0.2 and 6.2, and neither is the 6.3 the six month-in-sample effects average to.
- Estimand: the Act's restatement clause (`regional_employment_support_act_extract.pdf`, section 9): the number of percentage points by which the redesigned questionnaire raises or lowers the published unemployment rate once it is in full use, to one decimal place, added to the 6.0 per cent trigger; the population is the survey's published rate (all six rotation groups), the outcome the unemployment rate, the window the 2027 designation round, the unit percentage points, the comparison the redesigned against the old questionnaire for the same dwellings and months.
- No decoy: nothing is planted: no memo, briefing, draft or stakeholder figure states a break or a trigger; the release workbook publishes the phase-in months as measured, and the fieldwork report and the seasonally adjusted national series are relevant distractors carrying no break.
- No certified replay: no certified or published figure reproduces only under one method; the release workbook's rates reproduce from the cells on every route, and the method is left to the design.
- Lesson: a model learns that a change phased in through a rotating panel's incoming groups compares first interviews with last ones, so the change is read within month in sample and averaged over the interviews the published figure averages; no build on file teaches rotation-group bias or a phase-in comparison (task125's intake filter and task450's common-item link are differentiated on the card).
