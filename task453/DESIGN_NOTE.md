## Default path

## Three rules

- Task type: emissions-inventory (Physical & Environmental Sciences), which serves Data Extraction & Conformation: the inventory conforms the activity records (the province's cremation permits and the Fund's commissioning register) and the source's factor (the national adult oral health survey's three waves) onto each emitting event, one 2026 cremation.
- Domain test: the deciding quantity is the mercury released at each crematorium in 2026, the cremation source term an emissions inventory builds from activity data and an emission factor; the factor here is each decedent's teeth restored with amalgam, which the field estimates from a national oral health survey and which follows the person's birth cohort (the generation born from the mid-1940s to the late 1950s carries the most). Swap in hospital waste or landfill gas and there is no amalgam carried by a birth cohort, no survey wave and no ageing within a cohort: the share and the designations stop parsing.
- Swap: "which unabated landfill sites must fit gas capture so that 55 per cent of the province's methane is captured" keeps the arithmetic of the share but loses the quantity: nothing about a landfill's methane is carried by its waste's birth cohort and measured only in ten-yearly cross-sections of the living, so the step that decides here has no counterpart.
- Layer 1: the survey's three report PDFs in their own layouts (1996 one table with sex and age as rows and standard errors, 2006 men and women side by side with confidence intervals, 2016 separate tables per sex and for all adults, top bands 80 and over in 1996 and 90 and over after), produced by the survey's publisher; the agency's permit extract keyed by its own facility numbers with Wexley Regional renumbered on 2 March 2026 when the regional district took it over, infant ages in months, out-of-province residents, a window by return date; two abatement plants commissioned during the year; three receivables layouts, a controller export in Eastern time and a plant historian in UTC for the asks. Nothing is placed to be caught.
- Layer 2: estimand identification (age, period and cohort): the survey's age profile mixes cohorts other than the decedents' at each age, and the comparison that identifies how a cohort's amalgam changes is that cohort against itself one wave later, never one wave's neighbouring age bands; the decedent's cohort read at its 2016 mean and carried by its own change since 2006 flips the designations.
- Decisive step: re-key each 2026 decedent onto their birth cohort (ten years younger at the 2016 wave, twenty at the 2006 wave) and carry the cohort's 2016 mean to 2026 by the cohort's own change between the two waves, rather than reading or ageing along any one wave's age profile.
- Not semantics: every field can be read correctly (age at examination, per-adult means with the edentulous counted as none, the facility crosswalk, the commissioning dates, nil under 18) and the latest survey applied at age at death still files Tallis Creek and Dunmore Falls; the difference is which comparison carries the property to 2026, not what any field means.
- Not rule application: the Regulation fixes the population, the factor of 0.55 grams a tooth, the source (the survey), the share and the designation order, and says nothing of ages, waves or cohorts, so executing every filed rule leaves the estimator open; the enumerated family spans the age profile at death (41.8 per cent, Tallis Creek and Dunmore Falls), the 2006 profile (43.7, the same two), the age bands' own trends (39.6 to 40.5, the same two), the cohort's 2016 mean unaged (46.3, Ashgrove and Corbett Narrows) and the cohort carried along its own change in ten additive forms (44.0 to 45.0, Ashgrove and Tallis Creek, order margin 6.0 to 10.9 per cent, steps 52.3 at most and 58.6 at least); the standing family, the forms the three waves leave defensible, is the ten additive forms, spread 1.0 point of share and none in the set, with a quadratic through each cohort's three waves (46.3, Ashgrove and Corbett Narrows) ruled out because three waves cannot test its curvature and show none (SF23), and with the scaling forms the waves refute (the within-cohort change does not grow with the level: slope -0.008 against the -0.19 a proportional change needs) and the off-by-one cohort lags also landing the set; what rules the others out is loud in the tables (at a fixed age the 2006 to 2016 change runs from -3.7 to +2.4 teeth, while each cohort's own change runs from -1.5 to -0.6, mean -0.96, at every level).
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who cleans the permits, keys Wexley Regional's old number, dates the abated cremations right and reads every survey note applies the 2016 survey's amalgam at each decedent's age at death (or ages each cohort along that profile, which returns the same figures) and files a share of 41.8 per cent with Tallis Creek Crematorium and Dunmore Falls Memorial Gardens designated.
- Estimand: section 2 of the Regulation (in `crematoria_mercury_regulation_2023_consolidated.pdf`) files the population (the cremations performed at crematoria in the province in the scheme year), the outcome (the mercury in the dental amalgam in the teeth of the person cremated, 0.55 grams a tooth, nil under 18), the unit (each cremation, summed by crematorium), the window (the 2026 calendar year) and the comparison (the share released in abated cremations against 55 per cent, then the designation order by each crematorium's mercury), with the survey named as the source.
- No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught; no copied block, hidden rule, memo, draft, briefing, earlier inventory or stakeholder figure carries the age-profile answer, and no shipped file computes a crematorium's mercury at all.
- No certified replay: no certified or published figure exists for any crematorium's 2026 mercury, so the method cannot be recovered by reproducing one; the survey's tables are the raw material, not a target to match.
- Lesson: a property a person carries from their birth cohort and measured only in cross-sections of the living is read for a later population by carrying each cohort along its own change between waves; one cross-section's age profile, applied at the later age or used as the ageing path, returns earlier cohorts' values. No build on file re-keys survey cross-sections onto the cohorts of a later population.

## Survivor anatomy

- Actor: each decedent's teeth restored with amalgam has no column anywhere; the permits carry age, sex and dates, the survey carries means by age band and sex at three dates, and the property is a relation between a permit row and two survey cells ten and twenty years earlier that no file stores.
- Link: the link runs from a 2026 permit to the 2016 band ten years younger and the 2006 band twenty years younger, a comparison across files and waves that no key offers (the survey has no cohort column, the permits no survey reference) and no sentence invites (the Regulation names the survey, never a wave or a cohort).
- Reflex: the field's method, the latest survey's amalgam by age applied at age at death, sits at rung 1 and files Tallis Creek and Dunmore Falls; the careful correction of ageing the cohort along the cross-section returns rung 1 exactly.
- Back-test: the easy route validates on everything a solver would check: the 2016 profile is the latest and most precise wave (its confidence intervals about half a tooth wide), the share falls short under every route so the verdict is never in doubt, and the designated crematoria are the ones whose dead died youngest, where the cross-section shows the most amalgam.
- Second use: the permits and the register are the inventory's activity data for any method, the 2016 wave is the easy route's own source, and the 1996 and 2006 waves ship because the survey is a published series the Regulation names whole (a source an analyst downloads as its reports), carrying the edentulism trend and each wave's own layout; deleting the decisive step leaves every file in the pack.
- Shock: the cohort shift reads as ordinary explained movement: no crematorium, wave or band is an anomaly, the designated pair is neither the largest by cremations (Corbett Narrows) nor the youngest (Dunmore Falls), and on no shipped statistic (cremations, median age at death, place of death, the survey's means) are Ashgrove and Tallis Creek the top two. The one ordering that does put them first and second is the count of decedents in the ages the survey's peak cohorts have reached by 2026 (about 65 to 84), which is the decisive step's own insight and needs the cohort read first.
- Pins: the WHAT is filed in the Regulation in its own genre (population, outcome, factor, nil under 18, window, share and designation order, the survey as source), and no filed sentence names the age at which the survey is read, a wave, a cohort or an ageing path; deleting any filed sentence leaves the step unforced, and none of them hands it over.
