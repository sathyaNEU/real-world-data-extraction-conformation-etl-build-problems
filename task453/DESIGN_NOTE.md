## Three rules

- Task type: Survey Research & Official Statistics, seasonal-adjustment-official-time-series-production (official time-series production: carrying a published series across a change in its measurement), which serves Data Extraction & Conformation.
- Domain test: the quantity that decides the call is the redesigned questionnaire's effect on the published unemployment rate, estimated the way a statistics office reads a questionnaire change phased in through a rotating panel: each month-in-sample group on the new questionnaire against the same month in sample before the change, rotation-group bias and the second-interview start of dependent interviewing built in, averaged over the six rotation groups the published estimate averages.
- Swap: "restate the trigger by the points the redesigned questionnaire moves the published unemployment rate" with hospital nouns reads "restate the readmission threshold by the points the new discharge form moves the published readmission rate", and the phase-in through incoming rotation groups, the first-interview bias, month in sample and dependent interviewing have nothing to attach to, so the comparison and its correction stop making sense.
- Layer 1: the office's two collection systems export different layouts and units (CAI 2014's long file of weighted persons in thousands by status code, ICS's wide file of weighted persons), regions carry the 2019 codes in CAI 2014's months to December 2024 and the 2025 codes after (the 2025 review renumbered them; the concordance joins them), sex and age are coded differently in each export, rotation groups carry letters while a dwelling's month in sample follows only from the replacement schedule in the methodology note, and the release workbook carries header blocks, footnotes and phase-in markers; each is what the office's own systems produce, and none is placed to be caught.
- Layer 2: estimand_identification, the right comparison group: the change read within month in sample against the same month in sample before the phase-in, and the estimand's average over the six interviews, because the redesign's effect differs at the first interview.
- Decisive step: separating the redesign's effect from rotation-group bias by comparing like interviews (each month in sample on the redesigned questionnaire against the same month in sample on the previous one, month and region held fixed) and averaging the six month-in-sample effects as the published rate averages the six rotation groups.
- Not semantics: with every field, code, unit and the rotation schedule read correctly the easy route still files the arm gap (+1.2, trigger 7.2) or one effect with month in sample held fixed (+0.3 to +0.4, trigger 6.3 or 6.4); no field or definition names the role of month in sample or the redesign's effect at a first visit, and the error lies in which comparison estimates the effect.
- Not rule application: the Act fixes what is restated (the points the redesign moves the published national rate in full use against the previous survey for the same months, rounded to one decimal place, added to 6.0) and no filed rule or procedure says how the points are estimated; executing section 9 on the arm gap files 7.2 and on one effect 6.3 or 6.4.
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who conforms both systems' exports and the rotation schedule correctly and reads the Act's section 9 takes the redesign's effect as the gap between the rotation groups on the redesigned questionnaire and those still on the previous one in July to November 2025, about +1.2 points, and restates the 6.0 per cent trigger at 7.2 per cent (the default path's Ends on); holding month in sample fixed with one effect gives +0.3 to +0.4 and 6.3 or 6.4, and neither is the 6.5 the six month-in-sample effects average to.
- Estimand: the Act's section 9(2) (`regional_employment_support_act_2021_extract.pdf`): the trigger in force plus the number of percentage points by which the national unemployment rate published from the changed survey, once the change is in full use, differs from the rate the survey before the change would have published for the same months, rounded to one decimal place; the population is the survey's published national rate (all six rotation groups, the note's section 1), the outcome the unemployment rate, the window the months the survey ran on both questionnaires and after, the unit percentage points, the comparison the redesigned against the previous questionnaire for the same months.
- No decoy: nothing is planted: no memo, briefing, draft or stakeholder figure states a change, a trigger or a designation; the release publishes July to November 2025 as measured, and the response report, the population benchmarks and the projected seasonal factors are relevant distractors carrying no change.
- No certified replay: no certified or published figure reproduces only under one method; the release's rates reproduce from the cells on every route, and the method is left to the design.
- Lesson: a model learns that a change phased in through a rotating panel's incoming groups compares first interviews with last ones, so the change is read within month in sample and averaged over the interviews the published figure averages; no build on file teaches rotation-group bias or a phase-in comparison (task125's intake filter and task450's common-item link are differentiated on the card).

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the Act fixes one quantity: the number of percentage points by which the national unemployment rate the survey publishes once every rotation group answers the redesigned questionnaire differs from the rate the previous questionnaire would have published for the same months, rounded to one decimal place and added to 6.0; the designation then compares each region's mean of its twelve published 2026 rates with that sum. The counterfactual "for the same months" rules out a comparison of periods, and "once the change is in full use" makes the target the published estimate over all six rotation groups. How to estimate it is left to the analysis, and the records force it: the previous questionnaire's rotation groups differ by interview number by up to 1.9 points before July 2025, and the redesign's effect at a first interview differs in sign from its effect at the later ones, so only the six effects read within month in sample and averaged reproduce the change, and every such implementation rounds to 0.5. Every literal reading gives 6.5 and the four regions; the wrong triggers come from easy methods on the same literal quantity, never from another reading.
