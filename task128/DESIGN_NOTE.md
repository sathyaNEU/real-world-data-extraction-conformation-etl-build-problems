## Default path

1. Read the 2027 renewals of dogs aged 8 and over from the policy extract, book by book, with each policy's expiring premium, and the pricing model's indicated 2027 increase for each book.
2. Apply each book's indicated increase to its renewing premium.
3. Take each book's claims cost per dog from its 2026 policy years and the expense loading the pricing manual files, as the cost of the renewed year.
4. Each increase adds its share of the renewing premium at the book's margin, so every book adds; re-rate all five.

Ends on: all five books take their indicated increase, adding about SEK 31 million (prototype figures; frozen at stage 3).
Golden: the retriever, brachycephalic and giant-breed books only, adding about SEK 9 million (prototype figures; frozen at stage 3).

## Stump sentence

The default path re-rates all five books for about SEK 31 million, where the golden re-rates the retriever, brachycephalic and giant-breed books for about SEK 9 million; the step that lands it there is pricing the dogs an increase sheds at the book's average claims cost, or not at all, when the owners who lapse after an increase are those whose dogs have no ongoing condition and cost a fraction of the average while the dogs under treatment stay, so in the mixed-breed and terrier books, where many cheap dogs leave, the increase takes more off the result than it adds, and the first correction, a lapse response measured book by book from the past renewals, still prices the leavers at the average and keeps all five.

## Three rules

Domain test: pricing actuarial work decides the call: the result a renewal rate change buys depends on the renewal response and on anti-selection, the claims cost of the policyholders who leave against those who stay, which pet insurance makes acute because any new insurer excludes a condition the dog already has; with another field's nouns there is no renewal, no claims cost and no exclusion that ties a member to the cover, and the task stops reading the same.
Analyst test: an analyst who links each dog's policy years across re-issued policy numbers by microchip, separates lapses from deaths, transfers and non-payment cancellations, measures each book's lapse response to every past renewal increase and applies the 2027 indicated increases exactly as the pricing model states still prices the extra leavers at the book's average cost (or at the cost of dogs of the same age and sex) and re-rates all five books, because only rebuilding which dogs carry an ongoing condition from their claims history, or measuring what the dogs that left after past increases had cost, shows that the leavers are the cheap dogs.
Layer 1: a policy administration extract keyed by policy numbers that are re-issued when a policy is reinstated or the dog changes owner, the microchip number the stable key and written with and without spaces and the country prefix; one renewal-outcome field set by different systems for lapses, deaths, owner transfers and non-payment cancellations; claims keyed to the policy number in force at treatment, with re-opened claims and duplicates from the clinics' direct-settlement feed; diagnosis codes in two versions either side of a 2023 coding change; premiums with and without the 2024 premium-tax change, and monthly instalments against annual payment; a tariff change log by book and effective date, each renewal taking the change in force at its anniversary.
Layer 2: the analytical pitfall is anti-selection in a rate change: the increase's result has to be projected on the book that renews, which an increase re-mixes toward the dogs under treatment, so the extra leavers are priced at their own expected cost; applying the increase to a static book, or a lapse response priced at the book's average cost, re-rates books whose increase loses money.
