## Three rules

Task type: Physical & Environmental Sciences, emissions-inventory, which serves ETL in the taxonomy (an annual inventory of sulphur dioxide from abated coal boilers, conformed from the suppliers' delivery analyses, the operators' quarterly returns, the quarries' delivery tickets, the performance test reports and the permits' scrubber schedules).
Domain test: the quantity that decides is each boiler's sulphur dioxide leaving the stack over 2026; the field estimates an abated source without continuous monitoring by closing its sulphur balance (sulphur fired, less sulphur kept in the ash, less sulphur the absorber removed), and for a wet limestone scrubber the sulphur removed over the year is set by the calcium carbonate it consumed at its operating stoichiometry.
Swap: "which of 18 hospitals' surgical-site infections reached 250 in 2026" (hospitals for boilers, infections for sulphur dioxide) leaves no balance to close: no reagent is consumed in a fixed ratio to what a ward prevents, so a ward's year cannot be read from what it used and the task stops making sense.
Layer 1: four suppliers' statements collated into one file, a row per truck or wagon under its lot's certificate, Ostmouth writing dates day first, the collation reaching from December 2025 dispatches to January 2027 receipts, and corrected certificates re-issued with a revision letter; quarterly returns in a template whose layout changed at mid-year, the coal yard reported once on a two-boiler site's first row, stocks surveyed to the nearest 10 tonnes and weigh feeders that never quite agree with the weighbridges; quarry tickets keyed to the quarries' accounts and delivery points through a cross-reference to permit numbers, with certificates by ISO week; test reports as PDF tables from two laboratories at different reference oxygen contents. Each is how those systems issue their records; none is placed to be caught, and none moves a boiler across the line once read correctly.
Layer 2: field_method, the inventory's mass balance done right: the abatement term of an unmonitored abated source is the reagent-fixed removal over the year, not a removal rate observed while the absorber runs; the choice that flips the call is closing each scrubber's year on the limestone it consumed.
Decisive step: recognising that a scrubber's tested or design removal holds only for the hours it runs, and taking the year's sulphur removed from the limestone each scrubber consumed at its guaranteed calcium-to-sulphur ratio, so the sulphur dioxide left is the sulphur fired less the ash's share less that.
Not semantics: with every field read correctly (the receipt dates, the stock changes, the quarry accounts against permit numbers, the reference oxygen of each report, the guarantee schedule's ratio per mole removed), the tested-removal route still lists the wrong boilers, because a test run on a running scrubber measures that scrubber correctly and says nothing of the days it stood on bypass.
Not rule application: no filed rule takes the year's removal from the reagent; the Directorate's guidance files the estimand and the sulphur conventions, and its abatement clause asks only for the sulphur dioxide the scrubber removed over the year. Enumerated family on the generator: no removal (all 18 boilers listed), design removal (2: Quarholt Board Mills boiler 1 and Candrigg Soda Works), tested removal (1: Candrigg Soda Works), the test day's stack rate times the year's firing hours (2: Pridwold Starch and Candrigg Soda Works), the limestone balance (6), so the enumerated spread runs to twelve boilers on the call and five between the balance and the nearest test-based route; the standing family, the constructions that hold the conservation of sulphur and calcium (the balance with each ticket at its week's certified purity or at the year's mean purity, the silo stocks netted), spreads 0 boilers on the call and at most 3.4 per cent on any boiler's tonnage; the ruling-out is quiet, because only the balance itself shows a tested removal to be more than the limestone could have done.
Analyst test: a strong analyst conforms each boiler's 2026 coal burned from the four quarterly returns and its sulphur as received from the 2026 receipts, takes off the permit's ash retention, applies the removal its scrubber showed at its 2026 performance test to the whole year and lists the boilers at or over 250 tonnes, filing Candrigg Soda Works alone at 340 tonnes; it is wrong because five scrubbers stood on bypass for days at a time while their boilers fired, which no return records and no announced test sees, and the limestone each consumed fixes the sulphur it removed.
Estimand: each permitted coal boiler's sulphur dioxide leaving the stack from 1 January to 31 December 2026, in tonnes, against 250 tonnes; filed in the Order's draft schedule note and the Directorate's 2026 inventory guidance (sulphur on the as-received basis, coal and limestone as consumed, ash retention from the permit, molar masses stated).
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: the tests measure their days correctly, the design removals are the vendors' true guarantees, the returns record what their template asks, the limestone tickets record every delivery, and no memo, draft, briefing or stakeholder figure states or ranks any boiler's emissions.
No certified replay: the method is not recovered by reproducing certified or published figures; no shipped document states a boiler's 2026 tonnage, and the Directorate's earlier annual tables are not shipped.
Lesson: an abated source's year is what its reagent bounds; a removal rate observed while the absorber runs, at its design or its announced test, carries over days it stood idle, and no build on file closes a removal device's year on the reagent it consumed (task482 v7 read a device through its outlet floor, task478 v6 through hours since renewal).

## Literal reader

Executing the prompt and every filed sentence word for word: the Order lists each permitted coal boiler whose sulphur
dioxide leaving the stack over calendar 2026 was 250 tonnes or more, and the Directorate's guidance defines that
quantity as the sulphur dioxide formed from the sulphur fired, less the sulphur retained in the ash, less the sulphur
dioxide the scrubber removed over the year. Coal burned, the sulphur of the receipts and the ash retention are filed
conventions. No file states what any scrubber removed over the year: each laboratory report states the removal on the
day of its test, the guarantee schedules state the design removal, and the only records covering every day of the
year are the limestone each scrubber received and held, which the guarantees' stoichiometry converts to the sulphur
dioxide it removed. A literal reader therefore takes the year's removal from the limestone; carrying a test day's
removal to the year assumes the scrubber ran whenever the boiler fired, which nothing states and the limestone
contradicts for five scrubbers. The golden is what those records give.
