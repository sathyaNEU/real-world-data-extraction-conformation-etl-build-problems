## Default path

1. Read the maintenance policy: every pole of the cooperative's own distribution plant whose year of setting is 1977 or earlier is in the 2027 full inspection; the telephone company's poles and streetlight-only poles are not.
2. Read the register: about 236,600 of the cooperative's poles in service; parse each brand into its year, treat the 1900 placeholder as no year; about 32,200 poles carry no year, all set before the 1987 conversion.
3. Spread the poles with no year over the pre-1987 years like the pre-1987 poles whose brand was read.
4. Count the poles set in 1977 or earlier.

Ends on: about 27,000 poles 50 or older (spread like the read brands). The careful variants land elsewhere: spreading the poles with no year in proportion to each year's additions files about 38,600; the accounts' own oldest-first convention files about 20,100; a survivor curve fitted to the poles set since 1987 and carried past the ages they have reached files 35,200 to 39,800 (three- or two-parameter).

Golden: about 33,000 poles 50 or older: each pre-1987 vintage's survivors rebuilt as its additions times the survivor curve the pre-conversion poles' yearly retirements fit, less that vintage's read brands, the poles with no year spread over the remainder.

## Stump sentence

A competent analyst who parses every brand and reads every account spreads the poles with no year of setting like the pre-1987 poles whose brand was read and files about 27,000 poles 50 or older (or, spreading them by each year's additions, about 38,600); brands fade with age, so the poles with no year are the oldest, and each vintage's survivors are its additions thinned by decades of retirements that climb after forty years, so only a survivor curve fitted to the yearly retirements of the poles set before the conversion, applied to each vintage's additions, says how many poles of each age still stand (about 33,000). Data-native: the plant accounts' additions by year and the pre-conversion poles' yearly retirements fit the curve, and the read brands, thinning toward the oldest years against those additions, show the poles with no year are the old ones.

## Three rules

- Task type: missing-data-censoring-measurement-error (General Statistics & Data Science), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis: the call is a count read off the poles' age distribution, with the age missing not at random for one pole in seven.
- Domain test: the quantity that decides the call is the number of the cooperative's poles in service set in 1977 or earlier; the field's estimator for a distribution whose values are missing not at random while the population's inflows and outflows are recorded in aggregate is a stock-flow reconstruction, each vintage's survivors its additions times a survivor curve fitted by least squares to the yearly retirements of the group whose values are missing, the missing values spread over what that leaves; the recorded ages' own distribution in its place moves the count by thousands.
- Swap: "how many of the bank's savings accounts opened in 1977 or earlier are still open" keeps the words and loses the step: accounts close at a rate that does not climb with age, so each year's openings in proportion already give the survivors' shape; the step exists because wood decays at the groundline and poles retire ever faster after forty years, which only the pre-conversion poles' own retirement record shows.
- Layer 1: a register built at the 1987 conversion (years from work orders since, brands typed as they read before, a treatment code and two digits, blanks and a 1900 placeholder for the rest); plant accounts in two systems (additions by year 1938 to 1986 transcribed from the ledgers, the property system's aged records since, the pre-conversion poles' retirements as one count a year); retirements booked when the work order closes, so register and accounts differ by the open orders at year end; the telephone company's and streetlight-only poles in the register; two districts merged in 2019 with old codes on untouched poles. All of it is the cooperative's own record keeping; nothing is placed to be caught.
- Layer 2: estimation_fit (survivorship): the poles still standing from each year are a survivorship product, and the choice that flips the count is fitting the survivor curve to the pre-conversion poles' yearly retirements and spreading the poles with no year by what each vintage's additions leave, rather than like the read brands, by additions, oldest first, or by a curve fitted to the young poles.
- Decisive step: rebuild the surviving poles of each pre-conversion vintage as its additions times the survivor curve the pre-conversion group's yearly retirements fit (the depreciation profession's simulated plant-record method), and spread the poles with no year over that remainder after the read brands.
- Not semantics: reading every field right (each brand parsed, the 1900 placeholder taken as no year, the telephone company's and streetlight poles left out, the open work orders reconciled) still leaves about 32,200 poles with no year and no field that says how old they are; spreading them like the read brands is a correct reading that files about 27,000.
- Not rule application: no filed rule says how to date a pole with no year; the policy fixes the population, the date and the age (2027 less the year of setting) and nothing else. Enumerated family (seven routes): 12,100 to 39,800; standing family, survivor curves fitted to the pre-conversion retirements in two forms measured on the prototype (a three-parameter Makeham-Weibull curve fitted to the balances and to the retirements), the piecewise hazard and the post-1987 experience to be added at stage 2: 32,700 to 33,000 against a truth of 33,100, one count at the nearest thousand; the ruling-out is quiet, since nothing says the brands fade or that the oldest poles lack years, and the read brands against the additions show it only to an analyst who sets them side by side.
- Analyst test: the easy route, the poles with no year spread like the pre-1987 poles whose brand was read, files about 27,000 poles 50 or older against the golden about 33,000; spreading them by additions files about 38,600.
- Estimand: the number of the cooperative's own distribution poles on the register at 30 September 2026 whose year of setting is 1977 or earlier (50 or older in 2027), filed in the board's pole maintenance policy and the 2027 work plan memo.
- No decoy: nothing is planted: the contractor's close-out reports the cycle's findings, not ages; the plant accounts are the cooperative's own depreciation records; no memo, draft or staff figure argues a count.
- No certified replay: no published or certified figure reproduces the reconstruction; the accounts publish no age profile and no average age, and the method is not recovered by matching a filed number.
- Lesson: when the oldest members of a population have lost their entry dates, the age distribution is rebuilt from the entries by year through a survival curve fitted to the lost group's own exits; no build on file rebuilds lost entry years (task133 counts a takeover's exposure, task243 measures exit recording, task265 corrects a left-truncated register).

## Tried and rejected

- The seed's own mechanism (take-up conditioned on how each customer is reached) and its nearest honest variant, take-up read by distance below the cut in force from two pilot counties at different cuts: both are conditioned yield, G14, which the window held (task273), and the seed's architecture is the withheld-rule-with-decoy form the client bans.
- Enrollment ramping through Year 1 against the pilot's year-end take-up (credit-months against ever-enrolled): one ratio computed straight from the pilot's records, so a reader who reads "credits a month" lands it; and the call becomes a forecast.
- Survey income against verified income (a reporting gap measured on the panel's pilot-county applicants): the noise-variance-from-a-subsample insight task262 spent in this lane, and the verified subsample is selected on the outcome.
- Item imputation for missing income, a bracket-response imputation, raking to billing auxiliaries, small-area shrinkage: each screened on file as a template or rule application (task253's Tried and rejected), and the panel's income is asked in dollars here.
- Heaped incomes at round amounts, spread over their rounding interval: the spread's form forks the count at a whole-step cut.
- The affordability panel carried to the account base as stayers plus in-movers (prototyped, the first draft of this note): in a steady-state base the 2025 cross-section is the 2026 cross-section in distribution, so the panel weighted to today's accounts landed the golden's cut (recent movers 25.3 per cent of the frame, new accounts 25.2 per cent of today's base, panel and golden on one cut); only a turnover shift the world would have to be built to carry moves it, and carrying a survey to a later date is task259's report-ageing ground in the same subdomain.
- The affordability world as a whole: its deciding quantity is a survey-weighted income share whose remaining pitfalls are nonresponse within classes (task253 architecture 1), measurement noise from a subsample (task262), seasonal reference income (task272) or reflex calibration on visible auxiliaries; redrawn.
- The same reconstruction in a housing association's tenancies (start dates lost at a system migration, rebuilt from lettings by year through a curve fitted to the legacy endings), prototyped at 44,000 homes: tenancy endings rise only gently late in life, so spreading by lettings landed within 2 per cent of the truth at middle cutoffs while the golden's own fitted forms spread 3 to 6 per cent at the oldest; the poles' wear-out separates where tenancies do not.
- A coincident peak of a transformer's homes (diversity): task196's driver. Death registration completeness by growth balance: task243's driver. Reach and frequency by a beta-binomial and claim counts by a negative binomial: the latent rate from short windows, task253's ground.
