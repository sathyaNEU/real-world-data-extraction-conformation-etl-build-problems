9. The dispersion question. Away from the band the deposit amounts are far smoother than counts can be. Pooled 100-dollar bins just either side of the band read 187, 187, 188, 186, 187, 187, 184, 187, 184, 186 (Poisson sd at that level is about 13.7); per segment 200-dollar bins read 15, 15, 14, 15, 15 (clubs) and 54, 55, 54, 54, 54 (fuel). A Poisson GLM with a cubic or quintic in the amount, band excluded, gives chi-square per degree of freedom of 0.029 for the bank on 100-wide bins across 1,000-25,500 (6.6 on 231 df, tail probability about 1e-131), 0.065 on 5,000-15,000, and 0.004 to 0.026 for the eight segments (every segment tail probability below 1e-26). A real bank's deposit record has a Poisson floor of 1 and in practice more (customer clumping, repeat amounts, month and branch effects); a value of 0.03 cannot occur by chance and is the signature of amounts generated by evenly spaced quantiles of a fixed curve. That is also why the method fork in finding 3 is so tight: the pack contains no sampling noise, so the estimate carries no standard error. In real data the bank excess would carry roughly plus or minus 50 and transport roughly plus or minus 15 on the same method, so nearest 10 would not be a meaningful grain. Treat this as the one finding that must be fixed, and note that fixing it changes the graded figures.
