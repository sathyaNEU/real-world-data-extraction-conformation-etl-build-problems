## Stump sentence

The default path files a 12.1 per cent fall and releases only the first year of phase two, because step 4 corrects every year with one false-listing rate over all the drug overdose deaths no opioid caused: the review's 64 false listings are all methamphetamine and cocaine deaths in which the coroner listed fentanyl that played no part (half of the 128 stimulant deaths, none of the 64 other drug deaths), and the stimulant deaths grew from about 56 in 2021 to about 149 in 2026 while the other drug deaths fell from about 83 to about 61, so the pooled rate takes too many false listings out of the baseline and too few out of 2026; with each year's opioid, stimulant and other deaths solved from its three certificate categories through the review's error rates by drug class, 2026 has 190 deaths an opioid caused or contributed to against a 2021 to 2023 average of 253, 24.9 per cent fewer, and phase two is released.

## Three rules

- Task type: Survey Research & Official Statistics, census-register-administrative-data-quality (a register's classification error measured against a gold-standard review of one year and corrected in every year's count), which serves Data Extraction & Conformation.
- Domain test: the quantity that decides is each year's number of resident deaths an opioid caused or contributed to, estimated as official statistics corrects an administrative register for classification error: the probability that a unit of each true class is filed in each register category, measured on a full gold-standard review, carries to other years where predictive values and an error rate pooled over a changing mix of classes do not, so each year's true classes are solved from that year's register categories; the certified count, a net ratio, the predictive values, the two-by-two Rogan-Gladen correction and every rate conditioned on the certificate's drug pattern each file another fall.
- Swap: with retail nouns (returns coded as defects, one year audited) there is no certifier who lists every drug toxicology finds, no class of deaths in which an opioid is present without causing death, no medical examiner whose finding is the standard and no settlement outcome counted in deaths; the task needs a cause-of-death register whose false entries come from one neighbouring cause that is growing, which is vital registration's own misclassification problem.
- Layer 1: the state death file's record versions (a cause pending investigation amended when toxicology returns, later corrections to other fields, duplicate registrations voided), residents who died in other counties and in other states beside non-residents who died in the county, NCHS resident status, the legacy system's codes without the decimal point beside the new system's, the twenty record-axis positions, all-cause deaths around the overdose deaths, the medical examiner's review keyed by state file number with the drugs it found contributing and present as text, the EMS system's one report per responding unit, the fire districts' ages by year of birth beside the ambulances' from the date of birth, an out-of-state death the sending state sent twice under two of this office's numbers; each produced by the vital records, exchange, medical examiner and EMS systems, none placed to be caught.
- Layer 2: estimation_fit with the field's misclassification correction: classification probabilities by true class measured on the gold-standard year and inverted for each year's register categories, the false-entry rate conditioned on the class that generates it because the non-opioid deaths are a shifting mix of a class that produces false listings and one that does not.
- Decisive step: measuring the certificates' error by the medical examiner's drug class (opioid, stimulant, other) and solving each year's opioid deaths from its three certificate categories (an opioid listed, a stimulant without an opioid, neither), rather than carrying one false-listing rate over every non-opioid overdose death, a net ratio or a predictive value.
- Not semantics: every code, record version, residence field, review column and sentence of the agreement read correctly still leaves the analyst choosing how to carry the 2025 error to other years, and the two-by-two correction with the review's sensitivity and specificity is the well-read standard estimator; nothing in a field or a definition says the false listings depend on the stimulant deaths' share, which shows only when the review's false listings are set against the drug class of the deaths they come from and that class's growth over the years.
- Not rule application: Schedule B files the outcome (residents' deaths an opioid caused or contributed to, as a full medical examiner review would find), the baseline, the comparison, the as-of extract and the duty to correct every year's certificates by the error the 2025 review measured, never how; executing every filed rule with the standard two-by-two correction files the 12.1 per cent fall.
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who conforms the death file, reads Schedule B and the review correctly and corrects each year with the review's sensitivity and one specificity over all non-opioid drug overdose deaths (the Rogan-Gladen correction, the default path) files a 12.1 per cent fall and the first year of phase two only; the certified count, the net ratio and the predictive values file 6.2 to 7.3 per cent, and a false-listing rate per certificate that also names a stimulant files 13.2 per cent.
- Estimand: Schedule B of the grant agreement (`ori_grant_agreement_2024.pdf`), B1 to B5: the number of Wendmoor County residents who died in a calendar year of a drug overdose that an opioid caused or contributed to as a full medical examiner review of the case would find, whether or not the certificate names the opioid, estimated for 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2026 from the state death file as it stands at the department's extract, every year's certificates corrected by the classification error the 2025 review measured; 2026 compared with the 2021 to 2023 average, phase two released at 20 per cent or more below it.
- No decoy: nothing is planted: no memo, prior certification, coroner's report or stakeholder figure argues a fall or ranks the options; the false listings are the coroner's practice of listing every drug toxicology finds, the missed opioid deaths are certificates signed without a toxicology result, the stimulant deaths' growth is the county's drug supply, and the review measures the certificates as the agreement planned.
- No certified replay: no certified or published figure is reproduced to recover a method; the review gives one year's truth case by case and every correction reproduces 2025 alike.
- Lesson: a model learns that a register's false entries are corrected with the error rate of the class that produces them, measured against the gold standard and inverted year by year, because a rate pooled over a changing mix of classes moves with the mix; no build on file corrects a two-way register misclassification whose false entries come from one growing neighbouring class (task446 corrects with the pooled two-by-two, a wrong rung here).

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, Schedule B counts residents who died of a drug overdose that an opioid caused or contributed to as a full medical examiner review would find, whether or not the certificate names the opioid, and tells the department to correct every year's certificates by the error the 2025 review measured; it names no estimator. The literal estimand is the number of opioid-caused deaths, not the certified count, and the 2025 review is the only measure of the error. The pack supports one estimate: the review shows the certificates' error by the deaths' drug class (an opioid death certified with no drug one time in eight; a stimulant death listed with an opioid one time in two; another drug death never listed with one), each year's three certificate categories are a mix of the three classes through those rates, and the solve returns each year's classes; the same rates hold in every age band and sex, so solving within them sums to the same counts. The two-by-two correction is not another reading of a term: it is an estimate the data refutes, since its single false-listing rate over the non-opioid deaths is a weighted average of 1 in 2 and 0 whose weight, the stimulant share, the certificates themselves show rising year by year.
