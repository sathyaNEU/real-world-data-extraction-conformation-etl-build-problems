## Three rules

Task type: General Statistics & Data Science, `exploratory-data-analysis-distribution-assessment`: each unit's coming-year weekly backfill-need distribution rebuilt from the last closed year's records and read at an upper cut (the fifth-heaviest of 52 weeks), the cover set against it estimated for the population that will provide it; it serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis (the distribution of weekly need and where its fifth-heaviest week sits against a cover).
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each unit's ratio of the fifth-heaviest FY2028 week of backfill need to the hours one relief position will cover on post in an average FY2028 week; the field's own analysis is an order statistic of a rebuilt weekly distribution set against a capacity estimated for the population that will hold the positions, the July 2027 graduates in their first year on post, read from the cohort that started the same way two years earlier.
Swap: in General Statistics & Data Science the swap is of the method: replace the fifth-heaviest week by the annual mean (the relief factor of Budget Instruction 2016-03) and the call becomes 38 positions with 22 to spare; replace the holders' cohort by the title's average officer and it becomes 63, three short; the deciding object is an upper cut of a distribution over a cohort-specific capacity, and a generic summary changes the answer.
Layer 1: two timekeeping systems (RosterPro everywhere, Thornmere on ShiftLink from 5 January 2026 with its own status codes and, in payroll, its own site number SL-0410); fiscal weeks from Tuesday 1 July 2025 against payroll's biweekly periods ending on Saturdays; lines vacated by separation carried as roster rows with no holder until a class posted; three February days the RosterPro export re-ran and appended (315 rows); a payroll extract cut by check date, so June 2025 and July 2026 work sits in it; overtime keyed to the officer's home timekeeper, with the units' offer log recording the post an officer borrowed from another unit worked; timesheets approved after their period closed and paid on a later check, and some sent again when a supervisor reopened them and paid twice; the 2026 settlement's retroactive overtime posted on the April check against earlier service periods; HR's title history rows for the four officers who changed title after the year; resignations rehired under new employee numbers with their seniority restored, one of them three days later; the forensic security aides outside the Protective Services MOU, their compensatory time credited hour for hour; each written by the system named, none placed to be caught, and none moves the call (R2 rows in `checks.py`).
Layer 2: `estimand_identification`, own:forward-week-distribution-cut: the cover a relief position provides is a property of the people who will hold it, so its estimate has to come from the comparison cohort that matches them (an entry class in its first year on post), not from the unit's officers; with the standard's rebuild of the year planned for, that choice flips the placement from 63 to 59.
Decisive step: estimate the relief positions' hours on post in an average FY2028 week from the July 2025 entry classes' first year on post (FY2026), by title, because the positions are filled from the July 2027 entry classes; and read the new lines' holders after their first shift the same way.
Not semantics: every field, code, unit and definition read correctly (the status table's categories, training away from the post as off post, the position control's vacant lines and first shifts, fiscal weeks, the fifth-heaviest week, the whole number that reaches it) still leaves the unit's officers' off-post share on the relief position, because no field or definition says who holds a relief position or how much they will be off post; the academy_class column describes a person, not a relief position.
Not rule application: the standard files the outcome (the hours its holder will work on post in an average week of the year planned for) and the memo the population (the July 2027 entry classes); nothing files how to estimate the holders' off-post share. The enumerated family of that estimate (the unit's officers, the title's officers, 40 hours flat, officers hired since 2020, every FY2026 first-year holder, the July 2025 classes by unit, by title or as one class, pooled or member by member) places 52 to 64 positions, up to two apart on a unit; the standing family (the seven constructions that read the holders as new entrants: the five first-year cohort variants, the officers hired since 2020 and the title-pooled class with the new lines' holders read three ways) places one set, every unit at the same count (spread zero, every ratio 0.18 or more from a whole position); what rules the easy route out is a memo sentence about recruitment that mentions neither hours nor leave, joined to the HR extract's academy_class column by the reader.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every rule, rebuilds the FY2028 weeks with the position control's vacancies and FY2026's separations left out, and rates a relief position by title at the title's officers as the HR extract names them files 63 positions, three short of the 60; executed in `checks.py` (R3a), where the first-year cohort alone files the golden 59 (R3b) and each lower rung's single correction stays off it.
Estimand: Budget Instruction 2024-07, 2.1 to 2.5 and 3.1 to 3.4, with the memo of 9 October 2026: for each unit, the smallest whole number of relief positions whose hours on post in an average FY2028 week (40 scheduled, less the hours their holders, the July 2027 entry classes' graduates, will be off post; by title) reach the unit's backfill need in its fifth-heaviest FY2028 fiscal week (off-post hours of the same FY2026 week, plus the FY2028 position control's vacant lines until their first shift on post); positions not needed return to the reserve, a shortfall is reported.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: no memo, prior allowance, workbook, relief factor table or stakeholder figure states a unit's need, availability or complement; the Comptroller's overtime table (a distractor) totals agency programs and ties to nothing; the replaced relief factor is named only as the method the standard supersedes.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure carries an availability, a weekly need or a complement, so the method cannot be recovered by reproducing one; the payroll lines the asks read do not touch the placement.
Lesson: a capacity added by new people is rated at the availability of people like them, read from the cohort that started the same way, and a coming year's weekly need is rebuilt with that year's own planned vacancies before its upper cut is read; no build on file rates new capacity at a comparison cohort's availability inside an upper cut of a rebuilt weekly distribution.

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the standard asks for each unit's smallest whole number of relief positions whose hours on post in an average FY2028 week reach the unit's need in its fifth-heaviest FY2028 week, the need being FY2026's off-post hours by the same fiscal week plus the position control's vacant lines until their first shift on post. Everything in that sentence is computed from the records except one input: the hours a relief position's holder will work on post, which 3.3 defines as 40 less the holder's off-post hours and which no record states, because the holders do not exist yet. The memo says who they will be: graduates of the July 2027 entry classes, in their first year on post through FY2028. The records hold a cohort that started the same way two years earlier, the July 2025 classes, over their whole first year on post (FY2026), off post 7.8 per cent of their scheduled hours against the units' 14.5 per cent; any reading of that cohort (by title, by unit, as one class, pooled or member by member, or widened to every FY2026 first-year holder or every officer hired since 2020) places the same 59 positions, and the generator's world places them too. The unit's average officer is a reading of a population that will not hold the positions.
