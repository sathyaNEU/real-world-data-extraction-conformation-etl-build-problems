## Stump sentence

A competent analyst differences the operators' daily log into each day's well production, takes the largest day (4.15 million gallons on 21 August 2026) as the maximum-day demand and, against the firm capacity of 4.20 million gallons a day, files that the campus needs no added well capacity, where the golden is about 180 gpm (the campus used 4.47 million gallons on 20 August); the step that lands it there is reading the wells' production as the campus's use, when on the peak days the tanks gave up water the wells could not supply and the default's own maximum day is the day the wells refilled them.

## Three rules

Domain test: the call turns on maximum-day demand, the water-system engineering quantity a source capacity evaluation is scored on: the water the system's users took in a day, production adjusted for what storage gave up or gained; with another field's nouns there is no well field rated with its largest well out, no elevated storage riding through a peak, no pressure zone fed through a booster, and the task stops reading the same.
Analyst test: an analyst who reads the daily log, the SCADA export, the well test records and the state's definitions correctly (local calendar days, tested capacities, the largest well out) and takes each day's production as that day's demand files no shortfall; the reflexes on top of it (average day times a textbook peaking factor: none; production plus the booster transfer to the hill zone, which counts the hill zone's water twice: about 380 gpm; the peak hour's rate over a day: about 900 gpm) land on other wrong figures, and only production adjusted for each day's storage change gives about 180 gpm. `stump_class: analytical`.
Layer 1: an operators' daily log kept by hand in a workbook with a sheet per month (cumulative totalizers, one register rolling over, run hours, midnight tank levels in feet); an hourly SCADA historian export in long format with UTC timestamps, quality codes and a tag list; pump-test records against nameplate; level-volume tables for two elevated tanks with bowl geometry beside the cylindrical tanks' dimensions; monthly building meter reads on cycle days with register multipliers; distractors (the town utility's consumer confidence report, the grounds department's irrigation controller schedule). None of it sits on the main call's rows in a way that can move the call.
Layer 2: the estimand is the most water the campus used in a day, and the easy construction reads the wells' production as use, where use is production less the water the tanks gained; a water engineer recognises production as supply, which equals demand only over periods in which storage ends where it started.
