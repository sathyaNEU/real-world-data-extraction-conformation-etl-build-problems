## Default path

1. Read last winter's pathway-1 discharges at the five hospitals (December 2025 to February 2026) from the trusts' discharge records (`inpatient_discharges_2024-12_2026-09.csv`) or the national weekly return, with each patient's medically optimised date and discharge date.
2. Keep the patients the fund covers: registered with a Marchshire GP practice (the practice list maps each practice to its board) and whose home care had not started when they were medically optimised (the brokerage's care start dates, `brokerage_referrals_2024-12_2026-09.csv`).
3. Value a package at each hospital by its covered patients' average days between medical optimisation and discharge (the delay on record), or by their average wait for home care counted from medical optimisation (the care start less the optimised date).
4. Fill the 1,200 packages down the hospitals in that order, each hospital up to its covered patients last winter.

Ends on: Ashby Vale Hospital taking the most packages, filled to all of its covered patients.
Golden: Holmford Infirmary and Royal Marchford Hospital filled first, Ashby Vale Hospital with few or none.

## Stump sentence

The default path fills Ashby Vale Hospital first and to all of its covered patients, where the golden fills Holmford Infirmary and Royal Marchford first and leaves Ashby Vale with few or none; the step that lands it there is valuing a package at the patient's delay, or at the home-care wait counted from medical optimisation, when at Ashby Vale most covered patients also wait days for community equipment to reach their home, a wait that runs alongside the care wait and that a package does not remove, so a package there shortens a stay by only the care wait's excess over the equipment wait.

## Three rules

Domain test: the bed-days a discharge pathway frees is patient-flow analysis, the counterfactual length of stay when one discharge dependency is removed and the others left in place; with another field's nouns there is no pathway-1 patient waiting at once for home care and for community equipment, no national delayed-discharge return fusing them, and the task stops reading the same.
Analyst test: an analyst who cleans the discharge records, joins the brokerage's start dates, keeps the patients the fund covers and caps each hospital at them still values a package at the delay or at the care wait counted from medical optimisation, because both look like the days a package saves, and so fills Ashby Vale first, whose equipment waits hide most of its care wait.
Layer 1: five sources for one picture (the national weekly return, the trusts' discharge records, the council's brokerage, the equipment service's deliveries, the GP practice list), each with its own keys (NHS numbers, hospital numbers, the council's person ids with the NHS number partly blank), grain and clock (a return to Sunday midnight in patients a day, discharge times, start dates as dates); referrals re-sent after a ward move or a declined offer; ward transfers inside one stay; patients' home neighbourhoods mapped to the county's districts and the neighbouring county.
Layer 2: the analytical pitfall is decomposing a delayed stay between concurrent dependencies: the days a package frees are the care wait's excess over the other waits that hold the patient, built per patient across the brokerage and the equipment service, while the delay on record and the care wait from optimisation each credit the hospital whose other waits are longest.
