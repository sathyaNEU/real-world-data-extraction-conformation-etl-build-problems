## Why it survives the solver

Against `pilot_lessons.md`, the decisive step is FC01's held form, and none of the moves the pilot solver made without effort reaches it. No shipped sentence describes it: the programme rules name the meter set as the moment a rebate is earned and say unset slots lapse on 31 December, which the solver executes with Year 1's lag and lands on rung 2, and nothing says the crews queue, gives their capacity, or sets the programme beside anything they have set. No schema-visible join hands it over: the solver reads Year 1's meter-set dates, sees every one inside three weeks, and has no reason to compute a ceiling from 2025's meter exchanges, a different order type in a different year. No reproduction check refutes the stop rung: Year 1's book reproduces under any crew capacity above Year 1's weekly volume, and no closed week ever brought programme meter orders near a crew's spare capacity, because Year 1 sold 412 heat pumps across six rebuilt-feeder neighbourhoods. No stated definition carries it: the capacity is a recovered constant, the ceiling Lakes' and Uplands' crews held through the 2025 campaign while their standing work stayed flat. Replaying at the finest grain, the solver's starting method, still lands on rung 2, because it replays each forward meter set at the closed lag and never re-times it to its place in a queue that only the programme's own volume creates, as FC01's solvers replayed every charge from its plug-in time and never re-timed the hand-offs; a solver who does think of capacity at the annual grain is told every crew has room, and the installers' true belief that their crews can fit every rebated install points the capacity question where the answer is an honest all-clear. The residual risk is a solver that runs a weekly capacity check on the co-ops' own crews as a habit, so the design stage keeps out of the pack every sentence that would prompt one (no voice on the 2025 backlog, no filed order-of-work line) and the stopping rule records what a landed round means.
