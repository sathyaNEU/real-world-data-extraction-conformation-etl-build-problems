5. Literal fixing sentence (4.3): "A service area meets the adequacy standard for the 2027 network year when the weekly treatment slots of the Sites in scope in that area are at or above 1.15 times the area's projected 2027 weekly treatment demand." with "An area's projected 2027 weekly treatment demand is its treatments a week in the 2026 contract year multiplied by the area's projected growth factor in the Plan's enrolment projection for 2027." and "a week is one fifty-second of a contract year" and "taking the treatments the Sites in scope delivered". Literal result: for each area, the in-scope (2027 contract intent) sites' calendar-2026 delivered treatments divided by 52, times that area's growth factor, times 1.15, is the bar; the area's slots are the sum over its in-scope sites of positions x roster sessions per week x (1 for haemodialysis, 2 for infusion). Slots = positions x sessions x per-session yield (4.2); "positions" is filed as simultaneously operable positions.

6. What decides the main answer: mostly reading and following the 4.2 definition (positions operable simultaneously, not the attested or licensed station count) and executing 4.3/4.4. The residual analytical content is estimating a site's simultaneous positions from timed encounter intervals across the two layouts; the filing does not say how peak concurrency is to be taken (maximum, a percentile, a window). That is the part that is analysis; the "do not use stations" part is handed over by the document. I judge this borderline between a data-semantics/definition read and an analytical estimate, leaning toward the definition doing a good share of the work.

## Three hard items

A. Clause 4.4 new sentences.
- "Capacity and adequacy figures are reported in whole treatment slots. The figure an area is required to hold is rounded once, at the area level, up to the next whole treatment slot, because it is a minimum." Files a rounding convention only (what is rounded, where, which direction). It does not say how any figure is computed and points to no method.
- "A treatment is counted in the period of the contract year in which it was delivered, whether it was delivered in network or out of network." Files period attribution (service date, not adjudication quarter) and population (OON included). It is a WHAT convention. Side effect: it steers the out-of-network asks to service_date rather than adjudication_quarter, and tells the reader the run-out file matters; that nudges toward a reading they would otherwise have to find, though it is a convention and not a method. Neither sentence points toward concurrency.
- Also in 4.4 (not new): "Encounters recorded without a stop time or a recorded length are not delivered treatments." Files the treatment definition. It mentions stop time and length, which only matter if timed intervals are used, a mild pointer toward the interval columns. It does not address status values (cancelled, no show) at all.

B. Clause 4.2 vs station counts. Yes, the pack makes it plain. Quotes: "Deliverable treatment positions means the treatment positions a Site is able to operate simultaneously." and "A Site's licensed station count, where used, is the count on the Site's own facility licence row at its service address, and not a count held at a corporate holder." The first separates positions from stations by definition; the second treats the station count as a different, optional quantity ("where used"). Column names (attested_stations, licensed_stations) are distinct from "positions". Nothing states that the station count overstates or understates positions, and nothing states a ratio.

C. Invitation to sweep for concurrency. Not by any column name, sheet or file name; no field says concurrent, overlap, peak or capacity. But three things together invite it: the phrase "able to operate simultaneously" in 4.2; the 4.4 sentence about stop time and recorded length; and the transmittal's timestamp_convention ("service_start and service_stop in site local clock" versus "service_start with offset from coordinated universal time"), which matters only if clock times are compared or aligned. The data shape (start plus stop in one table, start plus minutes in the other) makes interval work possible and the staffing roster gives sessions, not positions. Counting or summing encounters is still fully possible without any of this, and the pack does not tell a reader which is wanted beyond the 4.2 definition. Net: a careful reader is invited by the definition sentence, not by anything structural; the invitation is moderate.
