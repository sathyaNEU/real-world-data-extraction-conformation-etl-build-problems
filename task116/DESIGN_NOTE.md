## ENTITY, UNIT OF VALUE, DECISION

Tallybridge Maths sells a maths practice platform to state schools in England, one agreement per school, priced per
pupil on roll (price list 2026-27: primary 4.60, secondary 5.30, a 850 minimum per school, ex VAT). In 2026 it piloted
the Trust Licence: one agreement with a multi-academy trust covering every open academy in it at 4.05 a pupil, which
replaces the subscriptions of academies already on an agreement. Two trust partnership managers run one spring round;
each can take the licence to five trusts, so the round is ten trusts, for licences from 1 September 2027.

Two quantities both read as the size of a trust's opportunity: the recurring revenue a licence would add if signed
(the trust's open academies priced at the licence rate, less the subscriptions it replaces), and what the round can
actually win there. They part because a trust-wide licence is only ever signed by a trust whose central office buys
for its academies, and the trusts with the most room to grow are mostly the ones whose academies each buy for
themselves: their room exists because their purchasing is fragmented.

Decision: exactly ten trusts for the spring 2027 round, ranked, and the round's planned new recurring revenue for
2027-28 in whole pounds, where each trust's planned value is its new recurring revenue times the licence's closed
sign rate (the plan's convention, filed), rounded to the pound, and the round is the sum.
