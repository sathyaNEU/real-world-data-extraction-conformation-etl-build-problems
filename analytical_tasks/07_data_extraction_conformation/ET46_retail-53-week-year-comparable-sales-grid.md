## 1. The real-world project

A beverage distributor's FP&A team reports **comparable sales growth** by category and fiscal quarter on the retail 4-5-4
calendar it adopted from its largest customers. Fiscal 2023 (Feb 2023 – early Feb 2024) had **53 weeks**. The analyst
compared fiscal-2023 quarter totals with fiscal-2022 quarters and included every store that bought in either year. Q4 growth
for every category looked excellent; the CFO asked why the board deck disagreed with the weekly flash reports.

## 2. The business decision (one deterministic recommendation)

**Which product category gets the incremental marketing fund — the category with the highest fiscal-2023 Q4 comparable
growth — and what is its comp?** The full category × quarter grid supports the decision.

Rules (comp-sales standard):

* Calendar: NRF 4-5-4; fiscal 2023 = 53 weeks (Q4 has 14 weeks). Comparisons are **shifted**: each fiscal-2023 week is
  compared with the fiscal-2022 week that is 52 weeks earlier (same weekdays); the 53rd week of fiscal 2023 is **excluded**
  from comps (reported separately).
* Data: Iowa Liquor Sales invoice lines (sales by store to retailers — treated as the distributor's sales to customer stores);
  measure = `Sale (Dollars)`; date = invoice date.
* Comparable store: purchased in **every** fiscal month of both fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2023 through the comparison period,
  by `Store Number` (not name).
* Category: by `Category` code mapped to the six reporting categories in the folder; never by `Category Name` (names were
  revised over time).
* Comp growth = Σ comparable stores' sales in fiscal-2023 weeks (shifted, excluding week 53) ÷ Σ same stores' sales in the
  matched fiscal-2022 weeks − 1.

## 3. Why this gets overlooked in real projects

* Fiscal calendars are generated by a helper table; 53-week years occur every 5–6 years, so the edge case is untested.
* "Same quarter last year" by fiscal labels compares 14 weeks to 13 in Q4.
* Calendar shift: because fiscal 2022 started a week earlier relative to the calendar, unshifted comparisons misalign holiday
  weeks (Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year).
* Store openings/closures dominate total growth; comp definitions exist precisely to remove them.
* Category names in the source changed spelling and grouping over the years; codes are stable.

## 5. Deterministic solution path

1. Build the 4-5-4 calendar for fiscal 2022–2023 (week starts Sunday; fiscal year ends on the Saturday nearest 31 January);
   flag week 53.
2. Assign invoices to fiscal weeks; map categories by code.
3. Determine comparable stores (monthly purchase presence in both years).
4. Pair weeks with a 52-week shift; drop week 53; compute comps by category × quarter.
5. Choose the Q4 maximum; report grid and the unadjusted alternative.

## 6. The traps

**Trap A — 53rd week included.** Q4 growth inflated by ~1/13 for every category; ranking may still change via different
week-53 mixes.

**Trap B — unshifted calendar.** Holiday weeks misaligned; categories with holiday peaks (spirits gift packs) swing.

**Trap C — all stores.** New-store growth leaks in; the leader changes.

**Trap D — names not codes.** Splits/merges categories.

## 7. Why the data is honest

The invoice data are the State's official records; the 53-week year is a calendar fact; comp-store rules are standard
retail practice. Nothing is planted.
