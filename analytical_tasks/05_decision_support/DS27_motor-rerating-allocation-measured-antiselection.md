## 1. Situation

A motor third-party liability insurer has regulatory approval for an €18.0 million increase on its renewing book, measured on the in-force
book at the filing date. Each rating area moves by its indicated change, capped at +12%, and the pricing committee's charter sends the
increase to the areas where it adds most to next year's underwriting result, each area re-rated in full or not at all. The chief actuary
holds that the most under-priced areas come first. Fourteen of the 44 rating areas run above the 68% target loss ratio. The committee
meets on the 14th.

## 2. Gate G: why this is legal

* **Litmus.** Every figure is correct: the regional experience exhibit, the claims and policy files, the indicated changes and the
  rate-change log. No stakeholder read is overturned: the under-priced areas really are under-priced. The difficulty is that the result an
  increase buys depends on how the book reacts to it, and the book's reaction differs by how a policy was sold.
* **Flags.** surface_read_dependency: no · stumping_family: analytical_non_defect · sole_data_defect: no.
* **Deletion test.** Delete the chief actuary's view and the regulator's reading. The natural allocation still re-rates the most
  under-priced areas first, and still books every euro of increase as a euro of result.
* **Instrument repair.** Give the insurer perfect claims data and unlimited credibility. The indicated changes become exact, and the
  aggregator-heavy areas still give the increase back, because the customers who leave have not left yet.
* **Lens swap.** The naive read prices today's book. The answer prices next year's renewing book, a different population once the
  increase has changed who stays.

## 3. The driving force

A strong solver builds credibility-weighted loss ratios at the rating manual's grain, ranks the areas by how far they sit above target,
and spends the €18.0M from the top. Under a static book every euro of increase is a euro of result, so every allocation that uses the cap
looks equally good, and the deficiency order settles it. The book is not static. In the nine re-ratings the rate-change log records, the
policies sold through comparison sites lapsed at 1.8 extra points per 1% of increase, and the ones that left had claimed at 0.55 of the
frequency of the ones that stayed. Agent-sold policies lapsed at 0.3 points per 1%, and their leavers looked like their stayers. The
change log reads as an audit trail of dates and percentages; the effect appears only when each change is aligned with the renewal and
claims records around it. The two most under-priced areas sell 64% and 58% through aggregators, so a +12% there loses premium and keeps
the claims.

## 5. Why the decisive rung survives the opponent

1. **Written nowhere.** The charter says where the increase should do most. The pricing manual's only behavioural line is a uniform
   planning elasticity. No document says leavers differ from stayers, or that the response depends on how a policy was sold.
2. **Corpus blind for a computable reason.** *In every one of the 13 overlap weeks no rating area's rate moved, so the overlap holds no
   price change and is silent on lapse and leaver frequency.* It certifies the incident count and the area coding exactly.
3. **No arithmetic symptom.** Premium, exposure, claims and the approved increase reconcile under every allocation, and the change log
   ties to the filed rate tables.
4. **Not a row predicate.** The response is a difference in lapse between re-rated and unchanged areas at the first renewal after each
   change, by channel, plus the prior-year claim frequency of the leavers against the stayers. It is then transported to each area's
   channel mix, which comes through a join from area to policy.
5. **The enumeration is arithmetic.** Which areas add most is computed from measured responses. No column carries a gain or an elasticity.
6. **No cutover date.** The nine past changes are measurement instances spread over four years. The forward effect is a change in who
   renews, and no series in the decision set steps.
7. **Survives deletion.** No wrong number exists to delete. Without any voice, the static book still makes every euro look like a euro.

## 6. The calibration corpus

* **Form.** The parallel run: 13 weeks in which the legacy system (one row per claimant, region only) and the new system (one row per
  incident, rating area coded) both recorded every new claim.
* **What it certifies.** Rungs 1 and 2. The 4,812 legacy claimant rows collapse to the new system's 4,236 incidents on the incident key,
  4,236 of 4,236. Legacy postcodes mapped through the rating manual's postcode list reproduce the new system's rating area on 4,236 of
  4,236.
* **What it is blind to.** The response to price (above).
* **Twin pair.** R11-U and R16-U are identical on every exhibit and rating-manual column: €20.0M premium, 41,000 car-years, credibility
  weight 0.91, credibility-weighted loss ratio 84%, a capped +12% change. Re-rated, R11-U adds €1.65M and R16-U €0.77M (2.1×). They sell
  18% and 52% through aggregators, a split visible only from the policy file and priced only by the channel response.
* **Resemblance points at the decoy.** By region, density band and deficiency, R07-U most resembles the fourth past re-rating, an urban
  area whose result improved by 0.86 of its increase. That area was 9% aggregator-sold.

## 7. Pins, voices and the licensed wrong basis

* **Filed pins.** The approval letter: €18.0M on the renewing book, measured on the in-force book at the filing date; no area above +12%.
  The rating manual's class list: 44 rating areas, region × density band. The pricing manual: indicated change is the credibility-weighted
  loss ratio over 68%, less one. The committee charter: the increase goes where it adds most to next year's underwriting result, each area
  re-rated in full or not at all. One sentence each.
* **Empirical pins.** Lapse slopes and leaver frequencies by channel, from the nine re-ratings.
* **Voices.** The chief actuary: "Our most under-priced areas have been carried for three years; they come first." The distribution
  director: "Aggregator churn is a volume question for my team, not a pricing one." The pricing manager: "Credibility is the whole game in
  the thin areas."
* **Licensed wrong basis.** The approval letter records that the regulator's rate review reads each area's contribution as its static
  increase and will report the filing on that basis.

## 8. Determinism by construction

* **Response form.** The nine changes span +6% to +12%, and lapse rises linearly in each (aggregator slopes 1.7 to 1.9, agent 0.25 to
  0.35), so linear and log forms give the same projection to €0.1M. Unchanged areas lapse at 9% with leavers like stayers, so the baseline
  cancels.
* **Measurement window.** Lapse is read at the first renewal after each change, and every one of the nine has a full renewal year behind
  it. Leaver frequency uses the prior policy year, closed and developed with the manual's factors.
* **Knapsack.** The charter's whole-area rule makes it a selection under the cap. The seven areas use €17.12M; no swap of one or two areas
  adds more, and the next-best set adds €11.3M.
* **Channel.** Every policy carries one sales channel for its life; no channel changes mid-term.

## 10. The ask layer

* **Ask A (device-carried, decoupled).** For each of the 22 regions, last year's glass-claim count and average cost per job. *Device:* the
  glass network invoices one line per job and issues credit notes for cancelled jobs that reference the original job ID in a later month,
  as its billing schedule documents. Counting invoice lines overstates jobs in every region and the cost per job in eleven. Glass is
  first-party cover and never enters the liability filing.
* **Ask B (device-carried).** For each of the 22 regions, complaints per 1,000 policies last year. *Device:* the complaints log holds one
  row per contact, grouped under a case reference the complaints guide documents. Counting contacts overstates complaints by about 1.6×,
  unevenly by region.
* **Ask C (validity).** For each of the 14 under-priced areas, the projected gain under the static book and under the channel response.
* **Decoupling.** Clearing the channel response changes no figure in asks A or B. Glass invoices and complaint contacts touch no premium,
  claim, renewal or channel record.
