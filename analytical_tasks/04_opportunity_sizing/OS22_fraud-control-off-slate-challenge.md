## 1. Situation

A card issuer must replace its e-commerce fraud rules by 1 February: the card scheme's monitoring programme puts its e-commerce fraud at
0.40% of sales against an exit line of 0.12%. The model team's memo offers four controls on the new model's score: decline at 0.70, 0.80
or 0.90, or refer to manual review at 0.65. The fraud strategy standard sets four conditions a control must meet. The holdout scores
transactions now 45 to 60 days old, with fraud labelled as reported so far. Finance's cost schedule prices declines, referrals and
challenges, the change log records the rules engine's past changes, and the authentication server logs every challenge it issued last
year. The fraud committee meets on the 12th. The model team favours decline at 0.90.

## 2. Gate G: why this is legal

* **Litmus.** Every figure is correct: the scores, the labels reported so far, the cost schedule, the change log, the authentication log
  and the standard. Decline at 0.90 really does have the cleanest alerts. No stakeholder read is overturned. The difficulty is that no
  listed control is admissible, and the one that is lies off the list.
* **Flags.** surface_read_dependency: no · stumping_family: analytical_non_defect · sole_data_defect: no.
* **Deletion test.** Delete the model team's preference and its precision ranking. The money-weighted search over the list still adopts
  decline at 0.80 on the holdout, or referral at 0.65 at maturity.
* **Instrument repair.** The suspect file is the holdout, whose fraud labels are 45 to 60 days old and so incomplete. Repair it at every
  depth: label each transaction at 180 days, and replace chargeback labels with confirmed fraud outcomes. Rung 0 still ranks decline at
  0.90 first on precision; rung 1 then finds no listed control passing and falls back to referral at 0.65, as rung 2 does; the
  answer is unchanged and still needs the grid search. The authentication and change logs are complete and need no repair.
* **Lens swap.** The naive read ranks the listed controls. The answer searches the controls the standard admits, a different set.

## 3. The driving force

A strong solver drops precision for money: fraud prevented less finance's costs of $40 a declined good transaction, $3.00 a referral plus
$0.50 for each good customer referred, and $6.06 a challenged good transaction. On the holdout as labelled, decline at 0.80 is the only
listed control that clears every condition. But the labels are 45 to 60 days old, and the change log's re-measured changes show low-score
fraud is reported slowly: 50% of the 0.55 band's fraud is in by day 45, against 85% above 0.90. At maturity decline at 0.80 stops 61% of
fraud against the 70% the exit line needs, and no listed control passes; the best-valued, referral at 0.65, overruns the review team by 13%
and stops only 59%. The standard admits challenges, and the authentication log shows 85% of challenged fraud failing. A challenge at 0.55
stops 72.2% of fraud while challenging 1.90% of good transactions against a 2.0% ceiling, and every other control on the grid fails a
condition.

* **Position table.** The challenge ranks 5th on rung 0 (precision 0.06). Valued on rung 1's basis it would rank 2nd (1.66× behind decline
  at 0.80) and on rung 2's 4th; it leads only rung 3, as the one control that passes.
* **Discriminator dominance.** Referral at 0.65 carries a 1.55× value advantage into rung 3 ($9.42M against $6.09M). The conditions admit
  none of it (370,200 referrals against capacity for 328,500, and 59.4% of fraud stopped against 70%) and all of the challenge, which clears
  its binding conditions with 5% (friction) and 3% (prevention) to spare, so no value lead survives the decisive test.
* **Partial correction priced (L3).** Every half-applied construction names a wrong control, at least 1.15× clear of its runner-up and of
  the answer on its own basis. A solver who searches the whole grid at maturity but ranks by value without the conditions names referral at
  0.60 ($11.05M, 1.15× referral at 0.55 and 1.81× the challenge at 0.55). One who searches the grid with the conditions on the labels as
  reported names challenge at 0.60 ($7.04M on that basis, 1.37× decline at 0.80 and 2.27× the challenge at 0.55); grossing the labels up

  challenged fraud passes challenges up to 0.60 and names challenge at 0.60 ($11.60M against $8.39M for the challenge at 0.55, 1.38×).
* **Grid.** Labels (as reported, at maturity) × controls (the list, the whole grid) × conditions (ignored, applied) gives 8 cells: referral

## 5. Why the decisive rung survives the opponent

1. **Written nowhere.** The standard lists the actions and the conditions. No document says the model team's list is incomplete, or that
   a challenge is the control that passes.
2. **Corpus blind for a computable reason.** *In every logged change the action was a decline or a referral, because the rules engine
   cannot issue a challenge; challenges come from the authentication server, whose log is a separate file.* The change log certifies
   money-weighting and the lag table, 9 of 9 changes, and never prices a challenge.
3. **No arithmetic symptom.** Prevented fraud, declines, referrals and challenges reconcile to the holdout under every reading, and every
   listed control's figures are computed without error.
4. **Not a row predicate.** The answer needs every action at every grid threshold valued at maturity, through each band's lag, and tested
   against four conditions on referrals, declines, challenges and residual fraud.
5. **The enumeration is arithmetic.** No column marks a control as admissible; 33 controls are evaluated and one passes.
6. **No cutover date.** The score distribution and the lags are stable across the holdout, and no series steps.
7. **Survives deletion.** Removing the model team's preference leaves the money-weighted list search adopting decline at 0.80.

## 6. The calibration corpus

* **Form.** The change log: nine changes to the rules engine's decline and referral rules over three years, each with alerts, declines
  and prevented fraud at 45 days and at 180 days, and every touched transaction re-scored by the new model.
* **What it certifies.** Money-weighted net savings at finance's costs reproduce all nine changes' 180-day results; precision and recall
  rank them wrongly 6 times in 9. The ratio of 45-day to 180-day fraud by score band gives the lag table (50% in the 0.55 band, rising to
  85% above 0.90).
* **What it is blind to.** Challenges (above).
* **Twin pair.** Changes C3 and C6 are identical on every change-log column at 45 days: 1,200 alerts a day, the same declines, $0.40M of
  fraud prevented. C3's fraud scored above 0.85 and C6's in the 0.50 band, so at 180 days they prevented $0.47M and $1.00M, 2.1× apart. Only
  the lag by score band separates them.
* **Resemblance points at the decoy.** The listed declines most resemble change C2, the decline rule that met its fraud target on its
  45-day measurement.

## 7. Pins, voices and the licensed wrong basis

* **Filed pins.** The fraud strategy standard: a control on the model score may decline, refer to manual review or challenge with strong
  customer authentication, at a threshold on the 0.05 grid; it is adopted only if referrals stay within capacity (900 a day), declined
  good transactions stay at or under 0.20%, challenged good transactions at or under 2.0%, and residual fraud at or under the scheme's
  0.12% of sales; among controls that pass, the highest net savings is adopted. The scheme notice: the current rules must be replaced.
  Finance's cost schedule: $40 per good transaction declined, $3.00 per referral and $0.50 per good transaction referred, $0.06 per
  challenge and $6.00 per good transaction challenged.
* **Empirical pins.** Scores, from the holdout. Lags, from the change log. Challenge outcomes, from the authentication log: 85% of
  challenged fraud fails, and 15% of challenged good customers abandon.
* **Voices.** The model lead: "Precision is what the reviewers feel. Decline at 0.90 has the cleanest alerts." The head of payments:
  "Every option on that list saves us money; take the one that saves most."
* **Licensed wrong basis.** The standard records that the scheme's monitoring reviewer reads the model team's precision ranking and will
  present it at the review.

## 8. Determinism by construction

* **Grid.** Thresholds sit on the 0.05 grid from 0.40 to 0.90, three actions, 33 controls.
* **Clearance.** The challenge at 0.55 clears its ceilings by 5% (friction) and 3% (prevention); every other control misses its binding
  condition by at least 5% (challenge at 0.60 stops 66.3% of fraud; decline at 0.80 at maturity 61%). On the labels as reported, decline at
  0.80 clears by 3% and every other listed control misses by at least 13%.
* **Lags.** Each band's 45-to-180-day ratio is the same in every logged change within one point, and every holdout transaction is 45 to 60
  days old, inside one lag column.
* **Challenges.** The authentication log covers every challenge issued on e-commerce last year, and its outcome rates are the same in
  every month within one point.
* **Rounding.** The challenge's net savings are $6.092M, which rounds to $6.1M.

## 9. Prompt sketch and deliverables

> The scheme wants our new e-commerce fraud control live by 1 February, and the fraud committee adopts one on the 12th. I need the
> control we adopt and the net savings a year it brings, to the nearest $0.1M. Our model lead's view is that decline at 0.90 is the
> cleanest option. Give me the recommendation in a sentence for the committee, with `control_options.xlsx`, a chart
> `controls_against_conditions.png`, and a one-page `committee_memo.docx`.

* `control_options.xlsx` — every control on the grid with its four condition values and net savings as labelled and at maturity, the
  lag build, the review-queue sheet (ask A) and the retry sheet (ask B).
* `controls_against_conditions.png` — a script-rendered panel per action (decline, refer, challenge): net savings by threshold as bars,
  each condition's limit drawn as a line, failing thresholds greyed, the listed controls outlined, and the adopted control highlighted.
* `committee_memo.docx` — the adopted control, its savings, and why each listed control fails.

## 10. The ask layer

* **Ask A (device-carried, decoupled).** For each of the six review shifts, last month's median minutes from referral to decision and the
  share over 30 minutes. *Device:* a referral reassigned between reviewers is logged as a new case carrying `reassigned_from`, and the
  operations guide times a referral from its first queue entry. Timing reassigned cases from their own entry understates the two night
  shifts.
* **Ask B (device-carried).** For each of the ten largest e-commerce merchants, last quarter's authorisation approval rate. *Device:* a
  transaction the merchant retries after a soft decline carries `retry_of`, and the authorisation guide counts the original attempt once.
  Counting retries as attempts lowers the rate at the three merchants that retry most.
* **Ask C (validity).** Each listed control and the answer on the four rung bases, the answer's four condition values, and the logged
  changes reproduced (of 9) by money-weighted and by count-based measures.
* **Decoupling.** Adopting any other control changes no figure in asks A or B. Review-queue logs and authorisation retries touch neither
  the holdout's labels, the change log nor the authentication log.

## 11. Rubric arithmetic

6 shifts × 2 (ask A) + 10 merchants (ask B) + 5 controls × 4 bases, 4 condition values and 2 reproduction counts (ask C) + the adopted
control, its savings, the best listed control and its breach + 5 named chart parts + 3 files ≈ 60 criteria.
