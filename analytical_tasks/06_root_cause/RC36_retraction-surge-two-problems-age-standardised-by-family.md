## 1. Situation

Corbel Scientific retracted 2,064 papers this year against 410 last year. The integrity board adopts one account of the surge on the 20th, and
the account decides which remediation programmes the portfolio funds: a portfolio-wide re-review, controls on guest-edited special issues, or
reviews of named journal families. Next year's close-out will check whether each problem the account names produced its stated share of the
excess. The board chair believes standards have collapsed across the portfolio. The head of integrity points to nine mass retractions of
guest-edited special issues.

## 2. Gate G: why this is legal

* **Litmus.** Every reported number is correct: the retraction records, notice dates, publication counts by journal and year, and last
  year's close-out. The chair and the head of integrity are each partly right. No figure is overturned. The difficulty is what a failure
  is and how exposure shapes a period count.
* **Flags.** surface_read_dependency: no · stumping_family: analytical_non_defect · sole_data_defect: no.
* **Deletion test.** Delete both views. Paper counts still say collapse, decisions still say concentrated batches, and family period counts
  still point at chemistry.
* **Instrument repair.** No file is suspect. Notices, decisions, journal families and publication counts are complete and current. Repair
  them anyway at every depth. Rung 0 still reads one portfolio-wide problem, rung 1 the nine mass retractions and rung 2 special issues and
  chemistry. The age-specific baseline on current exposure is a counterfactual built from earlier cohorts, which no row records, so the
  decisive construction is still needed.
* **Lens swap.** The naive readings count this year's notices. The answer sets each family's notices against what its earlier cohorts'
  rates predict for papers of the same age, a different population of comparisons.

## 3. The driving force

A strong solver counts decisions, not papers, because the policy counts failures by decision. That turns a fivefold surge into a rise of
1.5×, of which the special-issue programme carries 59%. It splits the rest by journal family and finds chemistry's regular retractions up
by 44 decisions. That is a clean two-problem account, special issues plus chemistry, and it is wrong about the second problem. Retractions
arrive years after publication, mostly at ages two to four. Chemistry published more regular papers in those years, so its jump is exposure
at unchanged rates. Materials science published 40% fewer regular papers in those years, because its editors were moved onto special issues.
Its period count rose only 26 while its age-specific retraction rates nearly doubled. Set against prior rates on current exposure, materials
science carries 56% of the excess failures and the special-issue programme 40%.

* **Structure shape.** Each rung names a different account, of one problem, one concentrated problem, then two problems with the wrong second
  locus. The answer keeps two problems and moves the second, and the special-issue share falls from 59% of the increase to 40% of the excess.
* **Partial correction priced (L3).** A solver who age-standardises the portfolio as a whole finds rates up 43% and adopts rung 0's
  portfolio-wide account. One who standardises by family but counts papers puts special issues at 94% of the excess, which is rung 1's
  single-problem account. Neither half reaches the answer.
* **Grid.** Grain (papers or decisions) × split (portfolio or family) × basis (period or age-standardised) = 8 cells. Only decisions by
  family, age-standardised, name materials science. Every other cell names a portfolio-wide problem, the batches alone, or special issues
  with chemistry.
* **The deciding comparison.** Family by family, observed decisions against expected decisions (materials 186 against 96, chemistry 134
  against 126). The account rests on this comparison, and the memo has to state it.

## 5. Why the decisive rung survives the opponent

1. **Written nowhere.** The policy defines a failure as a decision, and no document says retractions lag publication or that exposure moved
   between families.
2. **Corpus blind for a computable reason.** *In every closed period each family's regular publication volume held within 3% of its five-year
   average, so period counts and age-standardised excesses coincided.* The close-out certifies the event rule and the family split, and it
   cannot show exposure masking.
3. **No arithmetic symptom.** Notices, papers, events and family totals reconcile, and the event-adjusted rate is correct as computed.
4. **Not a row predicate.** The comparison needs each family's papers by publication year, prior cohorts' retraction rates by age, and this
   year's exposure by age. The retractions are then compared with the expected count family by family.
5. **The enumeration is arithmetic.** No record says "excess". Expected counts are built from cohorts.
6. **No cutover date.** The editors' move unfolded over two years of publishing, and retractions arrive by age, not on a date.
7. **Survives deletion.** No wrong number exists to delete.

## 6. The calibration corpus

* **Form.** Last year's integrity close-out: the board's adopted account ("one problem: a chemistry guest-editor batch"), the event list
  behind it, and the outcome of the guest-editor bar it funded.
* **What it certifies.** The event rule (notices within a journal grouped when no gap exceeds seven days, ten papers or more being a mass
  event) reproduces last year's event list exactly. The family split reproduces its family counts.
* **What it is blind to.** Exposure masking (property 2).
* **Twin pair.** The Journal of Applied Polymers (materials) and Inorganic Letters (chemistry) both rose from 31 to 38 regular retractions,
  with identical five-year paper totals and no special issues. Their excess over prior rates is 19 and 8 (2.4×), because only the polymers
  journal's papers at ages two to four fell by a third. Only the cohort comparison reproduces both.
* **Resemblance points at the decoy.** This year's family pattern resembles last year's, a jump in one family's period count, which the
  close-out confirmed as that family's problem.

## 7. Pins, voices and the licensed wrong basis

* **Filed pins.** The integrity policy: "The board counts integrity failures by decision; a notice retracting many papers is one failure."
  The board's terms: "An adopted account is checked at the next close-out against the excess failures each named problem produced."
* **Empirical pins.** The event rule comes from last year's event list. Prior rates come from the three preceding cohorts.
* **Voices.** Board chair: "Five times the retractions means our standards have collapsed." Head of integrity: "It's nine paper-mill
  batches in guest issues; stop the guest issues and it stops." Materials editor-in-chief: "Our regular submissions are as clean as they
  have ever been."
* **Licensed wrong basis.** The policy records that the publishers' association benchmarks members on decisions per paper published in the
  prior five years, and will compare the board's account with that rate.

## 8. Determinism by construction

* **Event grouping.** Every mass event's notices fall within three days. No singleton sits within ten days of another in its journal. Any
  gap rule from four to ten days and any size floor from three to ten give the same events.
* **Age bands.** Whole years since publication. Monthly bands change no family's excess by more than 2.
* **Reference rates.** The three cohorts before the editors' move. Two or four cohorts give the same two problems and shares within 2
  points.
* **Maturity.** Ages are counted only where a cohort has completed the age, so this year's cohort enters no rate.
* **Shares and the noise rule.** Shares are of the summed positive excess (162 decisions). The close-out counts a family's excess as a problem
  only beyond its own prior cohorts' spread. Chemistry's +8 sits inside its ±11, while materials' +90 and special issues' +64 sit far
  outside.

* `excess_by_family.png` — for each family, observed against expected decisions, with exposure by age as an inset. Mass events are shown as

* `board_account.pdf` — the adopted account, its remediations and the deciding comparison.

## 10. The ask layer

* **Ask A (device-carried, decoupled).** For each of the 12 journal families, last year's median days from first submission to acceptance.
  *Device:* a revised submission gets a new manuscript ID that carries its parent's, as the submission system's dictionary states. Timing
  from the revised ID understates by about five weeks in the three families that request most revisions. Editorial times never enter the
  retraction account.
* **Ask B (device-carried).** For each family, corrections and expressions of concern issued this year. *Device:* an amended notice is
  re-issued under the same DOI with a version field. Counting notices instead of distinct DOIs overstates corrections by about a seventh in
  four families. The main call counts retractions only.
* **Ask C (validity).** For each of last year's 14 closed-out events, the notices and papers your rule groups, beside the close-out's list.
* **Decoupling.** Clearing the cohort comparison changes no figure in asks A or B. Ask C runs on a year of stable exposure.
