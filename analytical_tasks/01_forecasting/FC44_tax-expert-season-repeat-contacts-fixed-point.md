## 1. Situation

A consumer tax-software company runs a live tax-expert service for its premium tiers. Expert contacts peak in the deadline weeks of
April. Attrition has left 150 returning experts, against 300 last season. The training centre can run four classes of 60 before the
deadline; any further cover comes from an outsourcer at contracted rates. The workforce standard plans capacity for every contact the
expert queue will receive in the deadline weeks, repeat contacts included, and routes first contacts to available experts in proportion to
scheduled hours. The pack holds five seasons of the old ticketing system's contacts, last season's CRM records, the returns forecast, the
routing policy's class list and the convenience table of support contacts per return. The hiring committee meets on 2 November.

## 2. Gate G: why this is legal

* **Litmus.** Every figure is correct: the returns forecast, the old system's contacts, the CRM's links, the class list and the
  convenience table. No stakeholder's reading of their own numbers is overturned; contacts per return really have been steady. The
  difficulty is that the steady number was produced by a floor of mostly returning experts, and next season's floor is mostly hires.
* **Flags.** surface_read_dependency: no · stumping_family: analytical_non_defect · sole_data_defect: no.
* **Deletion test.** Delete the head of operations' view and every voice. Expert contacts per return, carried forward, still reproduce
  every past deadline week and still fit under the training cap with 25 seats to spare.
* **Instrument repair.** Imagine a ticketing system that had linked every repeat to its handler for five seasons. The repeat rates would be
  measured better, but next season's repeat volume would still depend on next season's hires, which the plan itself chooses.
* **Lens swap.** The naive read and the answer weigh different populations: contacts generated by a floor of returning experts, against
  contacts generated by a floor of first-season and outsourced experts.

## 3. The driving force

A strong solver forecasts April's expert contacts from the returns forecast, first through the convenience table of support contacts per
return, then through the routing policy's class list, which sends amended returns, state errors and notice letters to experts and
leaves refund status to general support. It removes the old system's transfer-backs, converts contacts to experts and subtracts the
returning 150: 215 hires, inside the training centre's 240. Every step is correct. But the contacts carried forward include repeats, and a
repeat comes back at a rate set by who handled the first contact. The CRM, run alongside the old system for last season's six deadline
weeks, links every repeat to its first contact: 5% for returning experts, 30% for first-season ones, 40% for outsourced ones. Last
season's floor was 300 returning and 60 first-season experts, so contacts per return embed a 9.2% repeat share. Next season's floor is
mostly hires, so the hires generate part of their own load. Each expert nets one minus their own repeat rate against the season's first
contacts: the balance needs 270 hires, the training centre takes 240, and the remaining load needs 35 outsourced experts at their 40%.

* **Figure shape.** The answer is the extreme cell: total seasonal cover of 275 against 150, 230 and 215 on rungs 0 to 2, and the only
  construction that contracts any outsourced expert at the right level. One-pass builds never reach the training cap, so they never ask
  the outsourcer for anyone.
* **Partial correction priced (L3).** A solver who recomputes the repeat share once at the new mix and stops lands at 240 / 23 (outsourced
  −34%). One who solves the balance but nets the outsourced experts at the first-season rate lands at 240 / 30 (−14%). One who solves the
  balance and ignores the training cap plans 270 hires and no outsourcing, a plan the training centre cannot run.
* **Grid.** Segment (convenience table, class list) × transfer-backs (counted, removed) × repeats (carried share, balance) = 8 cells. Only
  the class list with transfer-backs removed and the balance reaches 240 / 35; with transfer-backs counted the balance asks for 57
  outsourced (+63%), and with the convenience table it needs 186 hires and none outsourced. The nearest wrong cell is the seasonal-rate
  netting at 30, 14% short.

## 5. Why the decisive rung survives the opponent

1. **Written nowhere.** The standard says repeats are included and first contacts are routed by scheduled hours; no document gives a
   repeat rate, ties repeats to the first handler, or says the load depends on the plan.
2. **Corpus blind to the mix.** *In every closed season returning experts handled at least 80% of first contacts, because the seasonal
   intake never exceeded a sixth of the deadline floor; so the repeat share held at 9–10% and expert contacts per return carried forward
   reproduced every deadline week within 2%.* The old system's five seasons certify the carry exactly where it cannot fail.
3. **No arithmetic symptom.** Contacts reconcile across both systems in the overlap, the class list partitions every contact reason, and
   every rung's plan is internally consistent.
4. **Not a row predicate.** Repeats are linked to their first contact and its handler in the CRM, rates come from groups of linked pairs,
   and the season's need comes from a balance in which the hires appear on both sides.
5. **The enumeration is arithmetic.** No column says how much load a hire creates; it is computed from linked pairs and solved.
6. **No cutover date.** The CRM's start is dated but steps no contact series; the old system's counts run unbroken through it.
7. **Survives deletion.** No wrong number exists to delete.

## 6. The calibration corpus

* **Form.** The six deadline weeks of last season in both systems: every expert contact in the old system, and the same contacts in the
  CRM with each repeat linked to its first contact and that contact's handler.
* **What it certifies.** The class list's partition, the transfer-back rule and the carried contacts per return, which reproduce the old
  system's deadline weeks for five seasons.
* **What it pins.** Repeat rates by first handler (5%, 30%, 40%), steady in all six weeks and at both sites.
* **Twin pair.** In the deadline week the Ogden and Fresno sites handled identical first contacts with the same headcount and the same
  handle times. Fresno's floor was 60% first-season experts after a late class, Ogden's 20%; their repeat contacts were 2.0× apart. Contacts
  per return and every site-level rate predict them equal; only repeats by first handler reproduce both.
* **Resemblance points at the decoy.** By returns forecast and contact-reason mix, next season most resembles last season, whose deadline
  weeks the carried contacts per return reproduced within 2%.

## 7. Pins, voices and the licensed wrong basis

* **Filed pins.** The workforce standard: plan capacity for every contact the expert queue will receive in the deadline weeks, repeat
  contacts included; route first contacts to available experts in proportion to scheduled hours; round every staffing figure up to the
  next whole expert. The routing policy's class list. The training centre's four classes of 60.
* **Empirical pins.** Repeat rates by first handler, from the overlap. Expert first contacts per return by contact reason, from five
  seasons.
* **Voices.** The head of support operations: "Contacts per return have been the steadiest number we have for five seasons." The training
  lead: "Four classes of 60 is everything the centre can take, and we've never come close." The finance partner: "Outsourced experts cost
  twice as much a contact; keep it in-house if we possibly can."
* **Licensed wrong basis.** The workforce standard records that the outsourcer prices its standby capacity on the company's contacts-per-
  return forecast and will quote on that basis.

## 8. Determinism by construction

* **Balance.** The balance is linear in every group's headcount, so iteration to convergence and the closed form give the same 240 / 34.5,
  rounded up to 35 under the standard.
* **Repeat chains.** A repeat can itself be repeated at its handler's rate; the CRM shows every chain ending within three links, and the
  balance counts chains whole, so counting by link or by chain agrees.
* **Routing share.** First contacts follow scheduled hours under the standard, so each group's share of the floor is its share of
  headcount in the deadline weeks; every class is productive before them.
* **Season.** The deadline weeks are the six that end on filing day under the standard, and the returns forecast is filed by week.
* **Rounding.** The figures sit clear of whole-number edges: 34.5 outsourced, 269.6 uncapped hires, 214.6 on the carried share.

## 9. Prompt sketch and deliverables

> Attrition has left us 150 returning tax experts for next season, half last year's number, and the hiring committee needs a plan on the
> 2nd. Our head of support operations says contacts per return have been the steadiest number we have. Tell me how many seasonal experts
> to hire and how many outsourced experts to contract, as whole numbers, in one sentence for the committee, and send
> `season_staffing.xlsx` with the build and the sheets below, a chart `deadline_balance.svg`, and a one-page `hiring_memo.pdf`.

* `season_staffing.xlsx` — the expert-contact forecast, the balance by group, the queue sheet (ask A) and the tier sheet (ask B).
* `deadline_balance.svg` — stacked bars for the deadline week showing first contacts and repeats by handler group under the carried share
  and under the balance, with the 240-seat training cap drawn across the hires, the outsourced block marked, and the committed plan
  labelled.
* `hiring_memo.pdf` — the committed hires and outsourced experts, and why last season's contacts per return understate the load.

## 10. The ask layer

* **Ask A (device-carried, decoupled).** For each of last season's six deadline weeks, the general-support queue's average speed of answer
  and abandonment rate. *Device:* a caller who accepts a callback offer leaves the queue and re-enters it as a new queue entry, with the
  link in the callback table, as the telephony guide documents; timing each entry separately understates waits for callback customers.
  The general queue enters no part of the expert plan.
* **Ask B (device-carried).** For each of the four product tiers and each of February, March and April last season, the share of returns
  filed with at least one chat. *Device:* a customer who upgrades tier mid-season shows the new tier in the customer table, with the dated
  history in the subscription-changes table, as the billing data guide documents; reading the current tier assigns upgraders' earlier
  chats to the wrong tier. Chats by tier enter no part of the expert plan.
* **Ask C (validity).** Hires and outsourced experts under each of the four rung constructions, and the repeat share each implies for the
  deadline weeks.
* **Decoupling.** Clearing the balance changes no figure in asks A or B.

## 11. Rubric arithmetic

6 weeks × 2 measures (ask A) + 4 tiers × 3 months (ask B) + 4 constructions × 2 (ask C) + the committed hires and outsourced experts, the
uncapped need and the four class sizes + 5 named chart parts + 3 files ≈ 47 criteria.

## 12. World-building constraints

* Returning experts 150 (300 last season, with 60 first-season); training cap 240 in four classes of 60.
* Repeat rates by first handler: returning 5%, first-season 30%, outsourced 40%; last season's carried repeat share 9.2%.
* Deadline-week first contacts equal 331.2 experts' capacity. Carried build: 364.6 experts (214.6 hires); with transfer-backs 379.2
  (229.2); convenience table 300 (150).
* Balance: 150 × 0.95 + 240 × 0.70 + 0.60 × outsourced = 331.2, so outsourced 34.5; uncapped hires 269.6. Partials: one recomputation 23;
  seasonal-rate netting 30; transfer-backs counted 57; convenience table 186 hires and none outsourced.
* The twin sites are identical in first contacts, headcount and handle times; first-season shares 60% and 20%; repeats 2.0× apart.
* Callback re-entries and tier histories touch no expert contact, repeat link or headcount in the plan.
