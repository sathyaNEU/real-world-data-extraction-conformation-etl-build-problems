## 1. Situation

A bank's economics desk publishes a nowcast of the week's national unadjusted initial claims every Thursday at 7:00, ninety minutes
before the Labor Department's release. The research memo files the house method: weekly views of 30 unemployment-related encyclopedia
pages, chosen each January from 220 candidates by correlation over prior years, regressed in 52-week log differences on claims and refit
each quarter. The nowcast is the base week's claims times the fitted ratio. The model-risk standard admits a method for client use only
if, run on the data available at each publication, it reproduces every one of the desk's 52 published nowcasts to the claim. This week
is the week after Labor Day.

## 2. Gate G: why this is legal

* **Litmus.** Every figure is correct: the claims history, the pageview files, the page-move log, the published nowcasts, the state
  claims detail, the auto team's down-week schedule and the handbook. No one's claim about their own numbers is overturned. The difficulty
  is a part of this week's claims that the model's signal cannot see and its year-over-year form has always cancelled.
* **Flags.** surface_read_dependency: no · stumping_family: analytical_non_defect · sole_data_defect: no.
* **Deletion test.** Delete both voices and the trading desk's consensus basis. The house method still reproduces 52 of 52 published
  nowcasts and still says 223,000.
* **Instrument repair.** Suspect: the pageview files, keyed by title where the house method reads pages. Re-key every view to its page
  through the move log. Rung 0 then returns rung 1's 200,000 and rung 2 still 222,800. The pages still record people, so the employer-filed
  block is still needed for 252,000.
* **Lens swap.** The naive read and the answer differ in population: claims filed by people whose searching the pages record, against a
  total that also holds claims employers file for idled workers.

## 3. The driving force

A strong solver implements the house method, finds through the page-move log the 14 candidate pages that were renamed, anchors each
base week to the same holiday position as the published nowcasts do, and reproduces all 52 of them to the claim. It then checks the
weeks that ought to be hard. Every retooling and holiday-shutdown week in the record passed within the method's usual error, so a
careful analyst concludes that plant shutdowns are handled. They were handled by arithmetic, not by the signal. Those claims were filed
by employers, they generated no searching, and they recurred in the same weeks every year, so the 52-week ratio cancelled them. This
week four assembly plants idle for a parts shortage, for the first time. They sit in the two states where the employer files the initial
claim for every idled hourly worker, so 29,000 claims will be in Thursday's figure, invisible to the pages and with no counterpart in
last year's base week. The block is built from the auto team's down-week schedule (kept for the bank's vehicle-production forecast),
joined to the plant list's states and to the handbook's employer-filing states, at one claim per idled worker in the first week of a
spell, which every past state batch confirms.

* **Figure shape.** Every correction moves the figure up (−29%, −21%, −11.5%), and the answer is the largest cell of the grid, so a
  solver who stops anywhere short under-forecasts.
* **Partial correction priced (L3).** A solver who suspects the schedule matters and adds year-over-year idled headcount to the
  regression gets a coefficient of 0.08 with a standard error of 0.9, because in the training weeks idled headcount never differed from
  its base week by more than 400. It adds 2,300 claims: 225,000 (−10.6%). A solver who removes employer-filed batches from history and
  refits on claimant-filed claims, but adds nothing for this week, lands back on 222,800 (−11.5%), because the base week held none.
* **Grid.** Page key (title, identity) × base week (52-week lag, holiday anchor) × this week's block (omitted, added) gives 8 cells:
  178,000 / 207,000 (title, lag), 198,200 / 227,200 (title, anchor), 200,000 / 229,000 (identity, lag), and 222,800 / the answer. The
  nearest wrong cell is the block without the holiday anchor, 229,000 (−9.0%), which needs the decisive insight and misses a construction
  the published record pins.
* **Licensed basis, priced.** The Street's seasonally adjusted consensus (213,000) times the week's published seasonal factor (1.061)
  gives 226,000, −10.2%.

## 5. Why the decisive rung survives the opponent

1. **Written nowhere.** The memo describes attention and the ratio form. The handbook lists the states that accept employer-filed claims
   as background to its definitions. No document says employer-filed claims leave no page view, that the ratio cancels only recurring
   ones, or that the auto schedule bears on claims.
2. **Corpus blind for a computable reason.** *In every one of the 52 published weeks, employer-filed claims matched those in the anchored
   base week within 400, because every employer filing in the window was a recurring retooling or holiday shutdown at the same plants in
   the same week.* The house method reproduces 52 of 52, and its errors in the nine shutdown weeks are no larger than in the rest.
3. **No arithmetic symptom.** The release is not out. Pageviews tie to the dumps, claims to the release history, and every published
   nowcast reproduces.
4. **Not a row predicate.** The block is a sum over first-week down spells, reached through schedule → plant → state → handbook, and
   netted against the same plants' spells in the anchored base week. It is a match across two years and three files, not a filter on any
   claims row.
5. **The enumeration is arithmetic.** Which plants' idlings are new this week, and how many claims they bring, is computed. No column says
   "unmatched".
6. **No cutover date.** The idling falls in the nowcast week itself. No closed series steps, and the published record never saw an
   unmatched block.
7. **Survives deletion.** No wrong number exists to delete. Without the voices, rung 2 is where a careful build stops.

## 6. The calibration corpus

* **Form.** The published workbook: the desk's 52 Thursday nowcasts, each with the realised advance figure and the year's selected pages
  by the titles they carried at selection.
* **What it certifies.** The house construction: page identity through the move log and the holiday-anchored base week reproduce 52 of
  52 to the claim. A 52-week lag reproduces 46, and pages keyed by current title 31. A back-tester is confirmed at rung 2.
* **What it is blind to.** Unmatched employer-filed claims (above). The state claims detail shows the two employer-filing states'
  batches only in retooling and holiday weeks, each equal to the schedule's idled hourly workers at that state's plants, to the worker.
* **Twin pair.** States S-04 and S-11 are identical on every column the claims file carries: covered employment, their claims in last
  year's post-Labor Day week (11,900 each) and every week of this year to date. This week S-04's claims will be 2.1× S-11's (about 23,800
  against 11,100), because two of the idled plants (12,700 workers) are in S-04. The attention ratio gives both one figure; only the
  employer-filed block separates them.
* **Free training instance (O3).** In the back-test window, before the desk began publishing, a one-week supplier strike idled a parts
  plant in S-09: 3,900 employer-filed claims with no base-week counterpart. The method missed that week by 3,700, inside its ±6,500 band,
  so the pattern is visible and harmless there.
* **Resemblance points at the decoy.** This week's attention profile most resembles last year's post-Labor Day week, which the house
  method nowcast within 1,100 claims.

## 7. Pins, voices and the licensed wrong basis

* **Filed pins.** The research memo: 30 pages chosen each January from the 220 candidates by correlation over prior years, OLS in
  52-week log differences, quarterly refit, nowcast equal to the base week's claims times the fitted ratio. The model-risk standard: a
  method is usable only if it reproduces every published nowcast to the claim from the data available at each publication. The handbook:
  initial claims count every claim filed, whether by the claimant or by an employer on the claimant's behalf.
* **Empirical pins.** Page identity, from the move log. The base-week rule, from the published workbook. One employer-filed claim per
  idled hourly worker in the first week of a spell, from the state claims detail against the schedule.
* **Voices.** The head of research: "Attention has caught every turn in claims since we built this." The desk's data scientist: "It
  reproduces every number we've ever published. The model is done."
* **Licensed wrong basis.** The client-publication policy records that the trading desk benchmarks the nowcast against the Street's
  seasonally adjusted consensus converted with the week's published seasonal factor, and will quote that figure beside the desk's.

## 8. Determinism by construction

* **Base week.** The holiday anchor is pinned by the published record, and this week's anchored base is last year's post-Labor Day week
  at 240,000 claims; the 52-week lag would take the following week, at 215,500.
* **Two routes.** Adding the block to the house nowcast, or refitting on claimant-filed claims and adding the block, agree to the
  thousand: the two fits' ratios for this week differ by less than 0.0005.
* **Spells.** Every idled plant's first down day is this Monday, no plant is in a continuing spell, and no idled plant was down in either
  candidate base week, so first-week and netting conventions cannot move the block.
* **States.** Both states hosting the idled plants are on the handbook's employer-filing list, and no plant down this week sits in any
  other state.
* **Vintages.** Pageview counts are final when published, and every refit uses claims at least four weeks old, which are fully revised,
  so today's files equal the data available at each publication.
* **Rounding.** 222,770 + 29,000 = 251,770, reported as 252,000.

## 9. Prompt sketch and deliverables

> Our nowcast of this week's unadjusted initial claims goes to clients at 7:00 on Thursday, before the release. Our head of research
> says the attention model has never missed a turn. Give me the figure to the nearest thousand claims, and send `claims_nowcast.xlsx`, a
> chart `nowcast_build.png`, and a one-page `client_note.pdf` that commits to it.

* `claims_nowcast.xlsx` — the nowcast build, the continued-claims sheet (ask A) and the revision sheet (ask B).
* `nowcast_build.png` — weekly NSA claims for the last 60 weeks as a line, the published nowcasts as points, this week's bar stacked as
  the attention nowcast and the employer-filed block, the four rung figures as labelled ticks, and plant down spells shaded.
* `client_note.pdf` — the committed figure, the size of the employer-filed block, and the consensus basis the trading desk will quote.

## 10. The ask layer

* **Ask A (device-carried, decoupled).** For each of the ten largest states, last quarter's average weekly continued claims and the ratio
  of continued to initial claims. *Device:* each release row carries initial claims for its reference week and continued claims for the
  week before, as the release's technical note says. Pairing them as one week misstates the ratio in the four states whose claims turned
  during the quarter.
* **Ask B (device-carried).** For each of the last 13 published weeks, the nowcast's error against the advance figure and against the
  revised figure. *Device:* the release history keeps each week's advance and revised figures as separate rows keyed by reference week
  and release date. Taking the latest row for both readings makes the two errors identical in every week.
* **Ask C (validity).** The nowcast under each of the four rung constructions, with each one's count of reproduced published nowcasts.
* **Decoupling.** Removing the employer-filed block changes no figure in asks A or B.

## 11. Rubric arithmetic

10 states × 2 (ask A) + 13 weeks × 2 (ask B) + 4 constructions × 2 (ask C) + the committed figure, the block and the base-week claims
+ 5 named chart parts + 3 files ≈ 65 criteria.
