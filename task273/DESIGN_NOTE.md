## Default path

1. Read the board's request: a main is critical when a burst on it would leave more than 500 properties (rating units) without water while it is shut off for repair, with the network as it stood on 31 December 2026 and otherwise run as normal.
2. Build the network from the GIS: mains between nodes, the valve nodes from the valve layer, the two plants and the reservoir as supply points; attach each rating unit to the main its service connection taps.
3. Take the field's criticality, refined to valves: shut each main off at the nearest valves the GIS draws around it, count the properties inside, and add every property the closure leaves with no open path to a plant or the reservoir.
4. Register the mains above 500 and sum their length.

Ends on: about 93 mains and 15.1 km (prototype, seed 5); the link-removal criticality without valves (each main removed alone) files about 50 mains and 11.9 km.

## Three rules

Task type: Urban & Regional Science, `network-analysis`: the criticality of a network's links as the demand their failure leaves unreachable; it serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis (the distribution of single-burst outages across the district's mains, cut at the board's 500 properties).
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each main's consequence of failure, the properties without water while a burst is shut off: the area inside the nearest valves the crews can close plus every area that closing it leaves with no open path to a plant or the reservoir; water utilities estimate it by valve-segment analysis on the GIS, and that estimate holds only where every drawn valve operates.
Swap: with hospital wards for mains and patients for properties the call reads "which wards would leave more than 500 patients without care while shut for repair", and it loses its valves, its segments and its supply paths, so nothing composes the area a failure takes out; the question only works on a network whose failures are isolated at devices.
Layer 1: valve and main identifiers differ between the GIS and the maintenance system (the system's crosswalk, valves renumbered at the 2024 integration); the exercising result codes changed at the 2024 upgrade (four-letter codes before, an operable flag and a reason after); one rating unit per flat behind a shared connection; burst jobs split across make-safe, repair and reinstatement work orders; work order times in New Zealand local time across daylight saving; lengths in metres in the GIS and kilometres in reports; each is how the system named holds its records, none placed to be caught.
Layer 2: `field_method`: the unit a failure removes is composed from the network (the valve-bounded area through the valves that operate, and what it cuts off), not read from a row; link removal or the drawn segments against the closable-valve shut-off flips the call.
Decisive step: compose each burst's shut-off area through the valves the crews can actually close (the latest programme visit deciding which will not), then count its properties and those it leaves with no open path to supply.
Not semantics: every field, code, unit and definition read correctly (the valve layer, the programme's old and new codes, the crosswalk, rating units against connections) leaves the field's criticality on the drawn valves at about 93 mains; knowing that a valve was found seized says nothing about which properties a burst two mains away cuts off, and only composing the shut-off area through the network says that.
Not rule application: no filed rule or procedure says how a burst is shut off or what it cuts off; the request files only the outcome (more than 500 properties without water while shut off for repair) and the network's date. Enumerated family on the prototype (seed 5): the main's own properties 0 mains; the main removed alone with its unreachable demand 50 (11.9 km); the drawn segment's own properties 0; the drawn segment with its unreachable demand 93 (15.1 km); the closable segment's own properties 0; the closable segment with its unreachable demand 173 (20.6 km); spread 0 to 173 mains. Standing family (what the record leaves defensible: a valve found inoperable at its latest visit does not close, every other drawn valve does, normally closed valves stay shut, the two plants and the reservoir supply): one construction, spread 0. Loudness of the ruling-out: no filed clause does it; the programme's latest visits (a maintenance record kept to run the programme) and the network's own connectivity do, and the best one-column cut (the drawn-segment outage re-thresholded) reaches a Jaccard of 0.54 against the golden.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every field correctly and applies the request's definition takes the field's criticality on the drawn valves (each main's segment as the GIS draws it, its properties plus those left with no open path to a plant or the reservoir) and files about 93 mains and 15 km, or by link removal about 50 mains and 12 km, where the golden is about 173 mains and 21 km; executed on the generator's clean truth in `checks.py` (ANALYST) at stage 3.
Estimand: filed at stage 2 in the board's request: a main is on the 2027 register when a burst on it would leave more than 500 rating units without water while it is shut off for repair, with the network as it stood on 31 December 2026 and the rest of it run as normal; with the register's length in kilometres and the rating units at least one register main's burst would leave without water.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: no earlier register, criticality score, consultant's list or memo ranks the mains; the seized valves are the programme's own findings spread over the district (about one in six, more in the old cast-iron town), and which of them matter follows from where they sit.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure gives a main's burst consequence, so the method cannot be recovered by reproducing one; the burst jobs carry no count of the properties affected.
Lesson: what a failure takes out of a network is set by the devices that can actually isolate it, which a maintenance record holds and the asset drawing does not, so criticality read off the drawn network misses exactly the places where the record says a device will not close; no build on file composes a failure's footprint.
