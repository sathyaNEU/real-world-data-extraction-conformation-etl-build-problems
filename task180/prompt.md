One field team, eight patches, and from January to March it works only one of them. Placing it is my call as revenue protection manager, and I take it to Monday's planning meeting on data pulled on 7 December. Judith on the field side wants the team back in Lochaber, where referrals have confirmed best. What I need is the patch the team works next quarter and the number of referrals it starts the quarter with.

That decision goes on slides as `field_team_q1_2027.pptx`. The first slide names the patch, its referral count, the runner-up and how many referrals separate them, over a bar chart of referrals for all eight patches, largest first, with the chosen patch highlighted, the runner-up labelled and the decision stated in the title. The second slide is for Judith. For every patch over the last three winters (October to March), give the visits where the officer got in, the diversions confirmed and the confirmation rate as a percentage to one decimal place, and beside them the most visits the team could make in that patch between 1 January and 31 March.

The desk loads the same per-patch figures from `patch_screen_q1_2027.csv`, one row per patch. Counts are whole numbers.
