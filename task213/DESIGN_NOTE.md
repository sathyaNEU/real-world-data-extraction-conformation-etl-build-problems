# task213 design note: Calabash Exchange's Q3 2026 rating-ring sweep

Seed AD36 (wave 8, Anomaly Detection & Diagnostics), read for its header and Situation only: a peer-to-peer
trading platform whose users rate each other after escrow settles, a quarterly sweep for coordinated rating rings
that suspends the accounts and expunges every rating they gave, and the count it expunges, committed to the nearest
hundred in the 1 December integrity notice. The world, the decision and the quantity are the seed's; the pitfall was
re-found from the data under the 10/10 rules. None of the seed's own devices carries the call: no second ring
definition reproduces a published share (no validated decoy), the playbook's holdback is a filed fact the count passes
through on every route alike, and no document carries the head of trust's prior or any figure arguing for a count.

## Default path

The trust-and-safety field's own ring detection, run correctly on the shipped records (figures at the shipped seed):

1. Read the moderation playbook: a ring is two or more accounts run together that trade with one another so that the
   accounts can rate one another, accounts being run together when one person or group controls them; the sweep
   suspends every account in a ring and expunges every rating each gave in the quarter, a rating once in its latest
   version and in the quarter it was first given; no account loses more than half (rounded down) of the ratings it
   received as the seller, suspended accounts included, the rest held for manual review; a customer's account at a
   Calabash Point is the customer's own.
2. Load the three monthly escrow exports (one renames `released_at` to `settled_at` from August), the ratings service's
   versions, the identity service's device links and the account register; resolve escrow handles to accounts through
   the register and its handle changes; conform the two fingerprint schemes.
3. Build the linkage graph every ring pipeline starts from: accounts joined through the phones and tablets they sign in
   on, the Calabash Point tablets set aside as playbook 2.3 says, keeping the linked groups whose members traded with
   and rated one another.
4. Confirm each group in the rating graph: mutual +10s on small orders, sellers rated almost only by the group,
   sign-ins switching between the group's accounts. They are genuine rings, run from a few shared phones.
5. Suspend every account in those groups, expunge every rating they gave in the quarter, apply the holdback seller by
   seller and total.

Ends on: 2,400 (2,357 before rounding: the rings that share phones)
Golden: 5,900 (5,920 before rounding: every ring, the three run from one phone per account included)

## Stump sentence

A competent analyst links accounts through the phones they sign in on, sets the agent-shop tablets aside, confirms the
linked groups as rings in the rating graph, applies the holdback and files 2,400 ratings, never following each buyer's
purchased USDT out of the escrow into the wallet ledger, where the purchases of the rings run from one phone per account
return within the hour to an account that tops up the very seller they were bought from.

## Three rules

- Task type: banking-credit-financial-risk / fraud-aml-detection, which serves Anomaly Detection in the taxonomy; the
  anomaly is the quarter's coordinated rating activity, diagnosed account by account as ring or not.
- Domain test: the deciding quantity is the ratings given by accounts one person or group controls, established the way
  fraud work establishes common control on a trading platform, by the economic substance of the accounts' trades
  (whether what a buyer pays for stays with the buyer), with the platform's identifier links as the field's first
  screen; with hospital or school nouns there are no escrowed coins, no wallet and no rating ring.
- Swap: "how many ratings will the sweep expunge" with patients and reviews of a clinic in place of traders and orders
  loses the escrow, the coins a seller must release to earn a rating and the wallet they come back through, so the
  question stops parsing; with a goods marketplace it still parses but the decisive evidence (a buyer's purchase passed
  to whoever tops up the seller) has no counterpart in a parcel.
- Layer 1: escrow exports keyed by handle (renames logged in the register's handle changes) and the wallet ledger keyed
  by wallet number (closed accounts' numbers released and later reissued), both resolved through the account register;
  escrow and ratings in UTC, the wallet ledger in Lagos wall-clock time without an offset, the device links in epoch
  milliseconds; wallet amounts in integer micro-USDT against escrow amounts in USDT; the ratings service writing a row
  per version; two fingerprint schemes after the July app release; the escrow export split by month with a renamed
  column from August. Each is written by the system that holds it; nothing is placed to be caught and none of it moves
  the call (SF11 to SF13, SF16, asserted).
- Layer 2: estimand_identification, the right construction of the population: whether accounts are run together is
  read from where each buyer's purchases go across the escrow and the wallet, because the identifier links the field
  starts from mark shared use (agent shops, households, cafes) and miss the rings that run one phone per account.
- Decisive step: following each buyer's purchased USDT from the escrow into the wallet ledger and asking whether it
  reaches, within the quarter, an account that tops up the very seller it was bought from, then taking every account on
  such a loop as one ring's.
- Not semantics: every field, code and definition read correctly (handles, wallet numbers, versions, clocks, units,
  fingerprints, the playbook's terms) still leaves the linkage graph on the phone-sharing rings alone, because the loop
  is a relation among three accounts' rows in two ledgers that no field, code or flag records.
- Not rule application: no filed rule names a loop, a wallet or a transfer; executing the playbook (its ring definition,
  the holdback, the Calabash Point clause) on the platform's identifier links files the default path. The enumerated
  family (shared phones with or without the shop tablets, dense mutual +10 cores, wash sets in the escrow, small-ticket
  buyer heuristics, forwarders, fan-in collecting accounts, transfer-funded sellers, d2 returns to counterparties, d3
  funders' sources, cycle searches other than the cross-ledger 3-cycle, the fairness algorithm) spreads from 0 to about
  21 times the golden over 20 redraws; the standing family (every construction that closes the loop, 67 of them) files
  the golden in 20 of 20, a standing spread of zero on the set reached only by the step itself, and the ruling-out is
  quiet: the data, not a sentence.
- Analyst test: a strong analyst who cleans every file and reads every rule links accounts through shared phones, sets
  the shop tablets aside, confirms the linked groups in the rating graph and files 2,400 ratings, the default path's
  Ends on, never reading where each buyer's coins went next.
- Estimand: the moderation playbook (the trust and safety team's filed standard) fixes the population (every account in
  a ring, a ring being two or more accounts one person or group controls that trade with one another so that they can
  rate one another, in the ring for the whole quarter), the outcome (the ratings those accounts gave in the quarter
  swept, each once in its latest version), the window (the calendar quarter in UTC by the time a rating was first
  given), the unit (ratings) and the holdback (half of each account's received-as-seller ratings, rounded down, every
  account included, the rest to manual review).
- No decoy: nothing is planted; the agent shops are the platform's real programme, the phone-sharing rings are genuine
  rings the default path rightly finds, the transparency report's prevalence is a true published figure that measures
  every inauthentic rating, the prior notices are true counts for their own quarters, and no memo, draft, briefing or
  stakeholder figure argues for any count.
- No certified replay: no certified or published figure reproduces under one method; the earlier sweeps' counts are
  totals for other quarters with no records shipped behind them, so nothing is recovered by replaying them.
- Lesson: on a platform where shared devices are ordinary, accounts run together are shown by where the value they
  trade ends up across every ledger the platform keeps, not by the identifiers they share; no build on file teaches
  reading common control from a value loop across two ledgers.

## Survivor anatomy

- Actor: ring membership has no column in any file; the deciding property is a relation among three accounts' rows in
  two ledgers (a purchase in the escrow, the buyer's wallet transfer within the hour, and that recipient's transfer to
  the seller), which no export stores.
- Link: the escrow knows parties by handle and the wallet ledger by wallet number; the loop pairs a purchase with its
  buyer's later transfers and that recipient with the purchase's seller, through the register's two keys and two clocks;
  no key pairs a purchase with a transfer and no sentence invites the comparison.
- Reflex: identity linkage through shared phones with the agent tablets set aside, confirmed in the rating graph, sits at
  rung 0 and files 2,357 (2,400), a correct but partial set; the AML reflexes (forwarders, fan-in collectors, round trips
  to counterparties) sit at rung 2 and overcount.
- Back-test: the textbook route validates: every phone-linked group it keeps is a genuine ring (mutual +10s on small
  orders, sellers rated almost only by the group, sign-ins switching between its accounts), so the linkage's precision
  checks out and nothing in the pack refutes it.
- Second use: the wallet ledger carries ask 2 (users' withdrawals reconciled to the custodian) and ask 3 (merchant-group
  cashback), the escrow exports carry ask 1, the register carries every key and the device links carry the default
  path; delete the loop from the design and every file stays in the pack.
- Shock: the ring accounts read as ordinary small-ticket buyers who pass coins on within the hour, like circle members,
  corridor customers and payers, and their sellers as ordinary merchants with honest buyers too; no ring is an extreme
  on any single statistic (the top hundredth by small-ticket share files 12.6 times the golden).
- Pins: the playbook files the WHAT (control, the ratings given in the quarter swept, the latest version, the first-given
  quarter, the holdback for every account) in its own genre; no sentence names money, wallets, transfers, loops, forward
  windows or the escrow-to-wallet relation, and deleting any one of them moves nothing the step rides on.

controls that trades with another so that they can rate one another, and leave how control is shown to the records;
