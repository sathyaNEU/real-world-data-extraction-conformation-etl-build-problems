## Default path

1. Conform the two vendors' detection files: Quillback's standard cubic feet of whole gas an hour to kilograms of methane an hour with the system's gas analysis, both vendors' times to Eastern, each detection to the register's site and that site's gathering system on the detection date.
2. Join each detection to the work order the crew raised for it: when the crew arrived, what it found, and when it repaired the source.
3. Book each detection as a release: its measured rate over the days from halfway between the site's last clean flight and the detecting flight to the crew's repair; a detection the crew found already stopped ("no emission found on arrival") is dropped as not confirmed.
4. Add each system's routine-source inventory for the qualification year from Pennwick's inventory workbook.
5. Divide by the methane in each system's sales gas over the qualification year (the state production reports by well and month, rolled to pads and systems, times the methane share in the system's gas analyses) and nominate the systems at or below 0.10 per cent.

Ends on: five systems nominated, Garner Run, Owens Ridge, Pursley Creek, Sycamore Flat and Bluelick (fixed at build).
Golden: three systems nominated, Garner Run, Owens Ridge and Sycamore Flat (fixed at build).

## Stump sentence

The default path nominates five systems, Pursley Creek and Bluelick among them, because step 3 books each detection as a release lasting from halfway since the site's last clean flight to the crew's repair and drops the detections the crew found already stopped; on the wet-gas systems most large releases are tank flashes and blowdowns that end within hours, before any crew arrives, so that booking keeps only the stuck valves a crew fixed and misses most of their large-release methane, while each site's mean measured rate over its monthly flights times the year's hours puts Pursley Creek and Bluelick above the 0.10 per cent cap (figures fixed at build).

## Three rules

Task type: Physical & Environmental Sciences / emissions-inventory, serving the ETL objective (two survey vendors' detections, the flight logs, the site register, the routine-source inventory, the state production reports and the gas analyses conformed into the supply agreement's measurement-informed inventory annex).
Domain test: each system's methane intensity is a measurement-informed inventory, whose large-release part the field builds from repeated overflights as each source's mean measured rate times its persistence (the share of overflights that found it emitting), added to the routine-source factor inventory and divided by the methane sold; with the persistence estimate replaced by detections booked as releases of assumed length, Pursley Creek and Bluelick fall under the cap and the nomination grows from three systems to five.
Swap: put a hospital in ("which wards can the trust nominate, by infections a patient-day from monthly prevalence rounds") and the routine-source factor inventory, the releases only an aircraft above a detection floor sees, and intensity against the methane sold have no counterpart; the call no longer reads.
Layer 1: two survey vendors across the year in their own units, clocks and site keys (Merganser kilograms of methane an hour against site ids in UTC, Quillback standard cubic feet of whole gas an hour against coordinates in Eastern time); flight logs in which some sites were not flown in some months (airspace, weather, pads brought on line mid-year); a site register in which pads moved between gathering systems on an effective date; state production reports by well and month with amended filings; quarterly gas analyses by system; maintenance work orders with free-text findings in local time; the routine-source inventory workbook with multi-row headers (inventory at stage 3).
Layer 2: field_method. A scheduled overflight samples a site's emission at one moment, so a site's yearly mass from intermittent large releases is its mean measured rate across the flights that covered it (zero where flown clean) times the year's hours; detections booked as releases with durations assumed from the flight schedule and the work orders understate the short self-ending releases and overstate the long repaired ones.
Decisive step: estimating each system's large-release methane over the qualification year as each site's mean measured methane rate over the overflights that covered it, a clean flight counting zero, times the hours in the year, summed over the system's sites (each source's rate times its persistence), rather than as detected releases with assumed durations; nothing filed names persistence, a duration convention or the time-average.
Not semantics: every field read right (a detection is the rate a flight measured at a site at its time, a flight log says which sites a flight covered, a work order says when the crew fixed a source or found it already stopped) still leaves the yearly mass of releases nobody saw start or stop to be estimated; the analyst who reads all of it and books detections as releases nominates five systems.
Not rule application: the annex files what is measured (methane from every source at a system's sites over the qualification year, large releases from the overflights and the rest from the routine inventory, over the methane in sales gas) and the 0.10 per cent cap; no filed rule says how a detection becomes tonnes, so executing every rule leaves the annualisation open, and the event route executes every rule and files the wrong set.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who conforms both vendors' detections, joins each to its work order, books each as a release from halfway since the site's last clean flight to the repair, drops the ones the crew found already stopped, adds the routine inventory and divides by the methane sold nominates five systems, Pursley Creek and Bluelick among them, whose measured intensity is above the cap (fixed at build).
Estimand: the supply agreement's MRV annex (Schedule C): a system's methane intensity is the methane emitted from all sources at its sites from 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026, large releases above the survey's detection floor from the overflights and every other source from the routine-source inventory, divided by the methane in the gas the system delivered to sales over the same months; a system qualifies at or below 0.10 per cent.
No decoy: no document, memo, figure or stakeholder carries the wrong set or a duration convention; the work orders are the maintenance system's ordinary records; no regulator's leak-duration convention ships.
No certified replay: nothing certified or published is reproduced; the vendors report detections, never annual figures; the routine inventory answers its own question (routine sources only) and ties under every handling.
Lesson: a survey that visits every site on a fixed schedule samples time, so an intermittent source's yearly mass is its rate while seen times the share of visits that see it, and booking each sighting as a release with an assumed start misstates short self-ending releases in one direction and long repaired ones in the other; no build on file teaches it (task180, task229 and task230 are about what a detector misses, task123 about apportioning a measured total by a measured profile).
