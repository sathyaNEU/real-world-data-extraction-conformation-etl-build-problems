Since 24 August, 283 of our members' cards have been used for counterfeit fraud, and on Thursday 8 October I take a reissue request to the board risk committee. Our compromise policy replaces every open card that a compromised terminal read by contact while it was compromised, unless the card has been replaced since. Nicholas traced the first cases to the pumps at the Ferrier Creek plaza on the 416. The committee approves a number of cards. That number is what I need.

The committee will read the request as `card_reissue_request.pdf`, and page one gives the number of cards to reissue and says where they were compromised: merchant, town and terminal ID, with the first and last date a card later used in counterfeit fraud was read there. Page two gives what the wave has cost so far, for debit and credit cards separately: gross loss, recovered and net, in Canadian dollars to the cent. Page three gives, for debit and credit separately, the counterfeit cases we refunded within our ten-business-day promise and the longest any refunded member waited, in business days.

So the fraud desk can check it, the same figures go in `common_point_trace.xlsx`, one sheet per page of the request.
