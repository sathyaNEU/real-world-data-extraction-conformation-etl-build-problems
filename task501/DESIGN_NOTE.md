## Default path

The pipeline a competent network analyst builds from the question, the standard and the files' grain, with the
field's own method in it (the discolouration conditioning test on trunk flows built by mass balance from the district
meters through the register's arrangement):

1. Read the network operations standard's planned-change clause (a main the change would carry at a higher hourly flow
   than the highest hourly flow it carried in the twelve months to the end of the month before the outage plan is
   prepared is flushed first; flows under the change are judged on the districts' recorded hourly inflows for the same
   dates a year earlier) and the outage plan (TM107 out of service 15 March to 15 September 2027, TM115 shut, the
   IC05 and IC03 boundary valves opened; plan dated 16 November 2026, so the reference year is November 2025 to
   October 2026).
2. Conform the district meters (hourly means on UTC, re-sent rows dropped, blank hours left out, the two meter changes
   joined on the DMA) and place each district at its inlet junction from the register.
3. Build each trunk main's hourly flow over the reference year as the sum of the inflows of the districts downstream of
   it, through the register's normal valve positions (interconnectors shut), and take each main's highest hour.
4. Replay 15 March to 15 September 2026 through the plan's valve positions and take each main's highest hour: six mains
   carry more than in normal running in every hour (Eastgate T05, Coldharbour Road T13, North Road T08, Highfield T09,
   and the interconnectors X02 and X04).
5. Flush every main whose works peak passes its reference-year peak: all six (T05 at 2.06 times its normal peak, the
   others higher still, X02 and X04 from nothing).

Ends on: flush six mains (T05, T08, T09, T13, X02 and X04)
Golden: flush two mains (T13 and X04)

The other natural routes land on six as well (any main whose flow rises; any main above its typical daily peak); a
solver who rebuilds only the year's burst (leaving the June valve job, a planned job, as normal running) lands on five
(T08, T09, T13, X02 and X04), and one who rebuilds the planned jobs and leaves the burst lands on three (T05, T13, X04).

## Three rules

Task type: asset-condition-maintenance (energy-systems-utilities), the utility task type that serves Forecasting & Predictive Modeling: whether a planned 2027 outage's flows will disturb the material conditioned onto each trunk main's wall, forecast from the year that conditioned it.
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each trunk main's highest hourly flow under the relining arrangement against the highest hourly flow it carried in the reference year, worked the way distribution engineers work discolouration risk (a main's wall layers are conditioned to the greatest shear it has carried, and trunk flows that no meter records are built by mass balance from the district meters through the network as valved).
Swap: put a hospital's nouns in ("which corridors must be deep-cleaned before a ward closure re-routes patients through them") and the task stops making sense: nothing in a corridor is conditioned by the most traffic it has carried, so a corridor that once took more people is not thereby safe, and no corridor's traffic has to be rebuilt from a door log through a building plan.
Layer 1: the district meters' historian export on UTC hours with re-sent rows (one district-day), download gaps left blank, one-hour dropouts the historian filled and flagged S, and two inlet meter changes under new serials (historian); the control room's valve log on local time across both 2026 clock changes, valves by number, 2,190 distribution-valve rows outside the trunk register and exercising rows that change nothing (control room); the plan's arrangement stated as valve settings (planning); the works outlet meter whose balance shows the July burst's loss (telemetry); fifteen-minute inlet turbidity with cleaning windows, fouling drift, air-bubble spikes and communication losses (water quality telemetry); districts coded in the meters and named in the plan; on the asks, the job sheets' date layout changing at the new year and re-sent sheets (contractor), and the loggers' local clock with the autumn hour read twice (business customers); each written by its own system, none placed to be caught, each asserted neutral on the call (R2 lines in checks.py).
Layer 2: field_method, the discolouration conditioning test done on the network as operated: the trunk flows that set each main's reference have to be rebuilt through the year's actual valve arrangements, which the register's arrangement on paper hides, and the district turbidity monitors confirm the operated flows.
Decisive step: recognising that a main's reference-year peak is the flow it actually carried and rebuilding those flows through the temporary arrangements the valve operations log records (the Brook Street relining, the June valve job and the July burst on the Mill Road main), so the Eastgate, North Road and Highfield mains and the Highfield-Sallowby interconnector already carried more than the relining will send them.
Not semantics: every field, code, clock and definition read correctly (the meters' UTC hours, the valve numbers, the standard's reference and window, the plan's valve settings) still leaves the easy route on six mains, because no file holds a trunk main's flow; it has to be modelled through the network, and the arrangement on paper is a modelling assumption the year's operations contradict.
Not rule application: the standard files the reference (the highest hourly flow carried in the reference year) and the plan files the arrangement, and executing both on trunk flows built through the register's normal arrangement flushes six mains; no filed rule or procedure says how a main's past flows are built. The enumerated family (any rise, above the typical daily peak, above the normal-arrangement peak, the burst rebuilt but planned jobs left as normal, planned jobs rebuilt but the burst left, every logged arrangement rebuilt) spans six, six, six, five, three and two mains; the standing family the data leave defensible (every logged arrangement under either clock reading, a switch hour taken at its start, end or majority, blank meter hours left out or filled, daily means for hourly) prints two every time (asserted SF2, SF8, SF9); the ruling-out is quiet, no clause names an arrangement, and it takes the valve log composed through the register, and the turbidity monitors' record to confirm it.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who conforms the meters, reads the standard and the plan correctly and builds each main's reference-year flows from the district meters through the register's normal valve positions files flush six mains (T05, T08, T09, T13, X02 and X04); counting every main the relining raises also gives six, and rebuilding only the burst gives five; only the flows rebuilt through every arrangement the valve log records leave T13 and X04 (executed in checks.py on the shipped rows and on the generator's clean truth, R3a and R3b).
Estimand: the network operations standard's planned-change clause files the population (every trunk main, the register's interconnectors included, a planned change loads), the outcome (a main is flushed when the change would carry it at a higher hourly flow than the highest hourly flow it carried in the twelve months to the end of the month before the outage plan is prepared), the grain (hourly flow) and the demand basis (the districts' recorded hourly inflows for the same dates a year earlier); the outage plan files the arrangement, the works window (15 March to 15 September 2027) and its own date (16 November 2026); no sentence names an operated arrangement, the valve log or a past job.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: no flushing proposal, risk assessment, hydraulic model output, list of affected mains or earlier assessment ships, no memo or stakeholder argues for any set of mains, and the condition assessment (a declared distractor) grades mains on structural condition, a different question; the valve log, the meters and the monitors are what their systems write.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure is reproduced to recover a method; there is no published trunk flow, no model output and no prior assessment of the relining; the reference is filed and every flow is the analyst's own build from the district meters.
Lesson: a model learns that a quantity defined by what a network actually did has to be rebuilt through what it actually did (the year's temporary arrangements in the operations log, composed through the asset register) rather than through the arrangement on paper, and that a main conditioned by an earlier emergency or planned job is already safe for the flows it carried then; no build on file rebuilds an asset's history through an operations log the standing arrangement hides.

## Survivor anatomy

Actor: the deciding property is each trunk main's highest operated flow, and no file has a column for it: the district meters record districts, the register records the network on paper, the valve log records valve operations by number; a main's operated flow is a relation between the districts downstream of it hour by hour, through the arrangement the valve log implies, that nothing stores.
Link: the signal is read through a link no key offers: valve operations by valve number, mapped through the valve register to mains and composed into the arrangement in force each hour, then the district inflows routed through it; the field's method (the standard's test on the register's arrangement) never asks for the log, and the log's rows name valves, not mains or districts, with 36 trunk rows that change an arrangement among 2,282.
Reflex: the field's method for the deciding quantity, the conditioning test on trunk flows built by mass balance from the district meters through the register's normal arrangement, sits at rung 0 and files flush six mains (T05, T08, T09, T13, X02 and X04), the default path's Ends on.
Back-test: the habitual control validates the textbook route: the works' outlet meter equals the sum of the district meters within 3 per cent in every hour but the burst's (asserted C1), under any arrangement. The turbidity record is the golden's own validation (lesson 12): the operated flows explain 18 of its 21 event days and the normal arrangement none, and the normal arrangement predicts July turbidity below North Road and Highfield that never came (asserted V1 to V3); it reads only once a solver models each main's running peak against the monitors, which the field's method does not ask for.
Second use: every file the decisive step reads ships for its own reason: the district meters (the call's spine), the register (the network, its valves and districts), the valve log (the control room's record of every valve worked in the year, 98 per cent of it distribution repairs and exercising), the turbidity monitors (the network's water quality record), the plan and the standard; delete the decisive step and every one of them stays in the pack.
Shock: the movement the step recovers reads as ordinary: a handful of valve jobs and one planned relining in a year of normal running; the district inflows never step, spike or end with an arrangement. On every shipped statistic (works peak, normal peak, relative rise, diameter, year laid, length) the pair is no top or bottom pair (asserted L6); on the absolute rise in peak flow it sits inside a top group of three within 3 l/s of one another (T05 118.0, T13 120.6, X04 120.6 l/s, the three mains that take the Ferrars and Mill Bridge districts), so no cut on that statistic picks the pair except one inside those 3 l/s, and nothing in the question invites a cut at all.
Pins: the WHAT is filed in the company's network operations standard and the outage plan (the reference, its window, the grain, the demand basis, the arrangement); no filed sentence names an operated arrangement, the valve log or a past job (asserted P3: the plan's earlier mention of phase 1's completion date was cut at stage 3); delete any sentence and the call is either unchanged or undefined, never handed over.
