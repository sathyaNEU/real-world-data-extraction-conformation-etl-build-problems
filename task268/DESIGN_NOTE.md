## Stump sentence

The default path names Fennick General Hospital, 61 per cent by Kaplan-Meier, because it treats every move beyond the registry's death linkage as ordinary censoring: on Gullhaven's retirement coast the patients who move north are almost all in NYHA class IV at their last visit, so Kaplan-Meier credits them with the survival of those who stayed, while the movers whose deaths the exchange still reports are the healthier ones; only carrying each lost patient's survival from the class recorded at the move (or weighting the patients still followed by the inverse of their chance of staying, given their class) shows Gullhaven Hospital's two-year survival, 58 per cent, as the lowest.

## Three rules

Task type: survival-time-to-event-analysis (Biomedical & Clinical Research), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis: each hospital's distribution of time from heart failure diagnosis to death, read at two years, under censoring that depends on the patient's recorded clinical state.
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each hospital's two-year survival from heart failure diagnosis, a time-to-event estimate whose losses to follow-up are driven by the patient's recorded NYHA class (frail retirees moving back to family beyond the registry's death linkage), the field's informative censoring, handled by carrying each lost patient's survival from the clinical state recorded at the loss; replacing it with the generic product-limit estimate moves the call from Gullhaven Hospital to Fennick General Hospital.
Swap: "the hospital whose patients' two-year survival is lowest once the patients the registry lost are carried from their heart failure class at the move" in a field without a clinical state that drives both the loss and the outcome (megawatt-hours for patients, a meter whose reading does not decide when it is removed) has nothing for the losses to follow, so the blind spot of the product-limit estimate, and the task, stop making sense.
Layer 1: the registry's visit export writes the NYHA class four ways across the 2024 EHR migration and links patients through a medical record number crosswalk; two campuses share one hospital code until a 2024 split; deaths come from the Florida certificate linkage (a workbook) and a quarterly exchange file in its own pipe-delimited layout; follow-up end reasons are coded text with destination states; diagnosis dates sit beside first clinic dates; each is how the system's registry and the state's records keep them, nothing placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimation_fit, the survival estimator under informative censoring: Kaplan-Meier with the moves as ordinary censoring against survival carried from the class at the loss (inverse probability of censoring weights on the class at each visit, or a projection through the class changes and class-specific death rates of the patients still followed); the choice flips the call.
Decisive step: estimating each hospital's two-year survival with the patients lost to a move beyond the linkage carried from their NYHA class at the move (equivalently, the patients still followed weighted by the inverse of their probability of staying in reach, given their class), rather than treating the moves as uninformative censoring.
Not semantics: every field read correctly (deaths from both linkage files, every end of follow-up with its reason, the classes harmonised, the record numbers linked) still leaves Kaplan-Meier on Fennick General Hospital, because the error is treating losses that follow the patients' worsening as uninformative, an estimation choice no field, code or definition settles.
Not rule application: the charter defines what is measured (every patient newly diagnosed in 2022 and 2023, alive or not two years after diagnosis, the patients the registry no longer follows included) and no rule or procedure says how survival is estimated when follow-up ends early; executing every stated rule with the product-limit estimate names Fennick General Hospital.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every field and the charter correctly runs Kaplan-Meier for each hospital with follow-up ending at each recorded move beyond the death linkage and at Wrenhaven's re-consent withdrawals, and names Fennick General Hospital at 61 per cent (Gullhaven 62); Kaplan-Meier credits Gullhaven's movers with the survival of those who stayed, though 581 of its 612 were in class III or IV at the visit where they reported the move, and carried from that class Gullhaven Hospital's two-year survival is 58 per cent, the lowest.
Estimand: each hospital's share of its patients newly diagnosed with heart failure in 2022 and 2023 alive two years after their diagnosis date, every such patient counted whether or not the registry still follows them, compared across the five hospitals, the lowest hosting the 2027 programme; filed in the programme charter (section 3) and the registry manual (the cohort and its follow-up).
No decoy: nothing is planted; the moves are the registry's own record of patients relocating, the exchange file is the state's records exchange doing what it does, and no memo, draft or stakeholder figure argues for a hospital.
No certified replay: no published or certified figure reproduces the method; the registry publishes no survival by hospital and nothing is recovered by matching published figures.
Lesson: when the reason follow-up ends is tied to how sick the patient has become, the product-limit estimate credits the lost with the survival of the patients still followed, so each lost patient has to be carried from the clinical state recorded at the loss; no build on file teaches informative censoring (task265 left truncation, task266 competing endings, task134 v1 competing exits).
