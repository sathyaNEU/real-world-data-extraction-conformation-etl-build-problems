## Default path

1. Read the stock recovery plan: each season the committee closes the ground whose haddock stock in the latest year has fallen furthest below its 2019 to 2021 average.
2. Take the members' logbooks for the six grounds, 2019 to 2026: paper trip forms to 2022 (round weight, hours fished, rectangle and sub-square) and electronic tow records from 2023 (gutted weight converted to live weight, haul start and end times to hours, cell codes to the grid).
3. Each ground's catch per hour towed in each year, standardised by year, cell, vessel and month (the field's commercial index), or as the plain ratio of catch to hours.
4. Each ground's 2026 rate against its 2019 to 2021 mean; the largest fall closes.

## Three rules

Task type: missing-data-censoring-measurement-error (General Statistics & Data Science), which serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis: each ground's stock is an area mean that the logbooks cover only where the fleet chose to tow, cells missing not at random, against a probability sample of the whole area.
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each ground's haddock stock over its whole stock area in 2026 against its 2019 to 2021 base, a population mean over an area that the field estimates from a probability sample, while the catch records' sampling locations follow the quantity measured (preferential sampling); replacing the probability-sample estimator with the generic summary of the records (catch per unit of effort, raw or standardised) moves the call from Halmorie Deep to Brettach Bank.
Swap: "the ground whose haddock stock has fallen furthest, read from fixed stations over the whole stock area rather than from the boats' catch per hour" in a field whose collectors do not follow what they measure (meter readers reading every meter on a route, megawatt-hours for haddock) has no record whose locations track the quantity, so the catch rate's blind spot, and the task, stop making sense.
Layer 1: the logbooks change format in 2023 (paper trip forms, one line per trip and rectangle in round weight and hours fished; electronic tow records with haul start and end in UTC and gutted weight), cells written as rectangle plus sub-square on paper and as one code on the e-logbook, a vessel re-registered in 2024 under new port letters, survey stations moved within their stratum after net damage and one missed for weather in 2020; each is how the organisation's and the authority's systems keep the records, nothing placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimation_fit, the estimator of each ground's stock: the survey's design-based stratified mean over the stock area against the fleet's catch per unit of effort, whose sampling locations follow the stock; the choice flips the call.
Decisive step: estimating each ground's stock from the probability sample that covers its whole stock area, each depth stratum weighted by its area, rather than from the catch rates of boats that concentrate where the stock still is, because a stock that shrinks retreats into its best ground and the fleet follows it there.
Not semantics: every field read correctly (catch put on live weight, effort on hours, cells on the grid, the survey's strata and their areas) still leaves the easy route on Brettach Bank, because the error is estimating the stock from records whose locations the fleet chose by the catches it expected, an estimation choice no field, code or definition settles.
Not rule application: the plan defines what is measured (the haddock on each ground's stock area in 2026 against its 2019 to 2021 average, the ground with the largest fall closing) and no rule or procedure says which records estimate a ground's stock or how; executing every stated rule on the members' catch rates closes Brettach Bank.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every logbook field and the plan correctly standardises the members' catch per hour towed by year, cell, vessel and month and closes Brettach Bank, whose rate fell about 40 per cent while Halmorie Deep's fell about 20; the survey's stratified mean over each ground's stock area puts Halmorie Deep's fall at about 55 per cent and closes it.
Estimand: the haddock on each ground's stock area (the plan's grid, Annex A) in 2026 against the average of 2019, 2020 and 2021, compared across the six grounds, the ground whose stock has fallen furthest closing for the 2027 season; filed in the stock recovery plan (stock_recovery_plan_2022.pdf).
No decoy: nothing is planted; the logbooks are the members' own records of where and how they fished, the survey is the organisation's gear and bycatch survey run for its own purpose, and no memo, draft or stakeholder figure argues for a ground.
No certified replay: no published or certified figure reproduces the method; the organisation publishes no stock index and nothing is recovered by matching published figures.
Lesson: an area mean read from records whose locations are chosen by the quantity measured is biased toward where the quantity remains, so when the quantity contracts into part of the area the records hold it up while it falls, and only a probability sample of the whole area estimates it; no build on file teaches preferential sampling (task199 normalises event counts by the watchers' filing volume, task462 corrects detections for size, task264 weights members by their visit frequency).
