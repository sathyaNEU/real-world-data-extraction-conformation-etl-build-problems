## Three rules

Task type: asset-condition-maintenance (Energy Systems & Utilities), tagged in the taxonomy for Opportunity Sizing & Decision Support: the out-of-warranty replacement demand an asset base throws off in 2027, forecast from each asset's condition (its pack's age since its last fitting, its life curve, its warranty clock), sizing a refurbishment line's fixed capacity across five platforms.
Domain test: The deciding quantity is each platform's 2027 out-of-warranty pack failures in the Netherlands, the asset-condition analyst's installed-base forecast built from each pack's own condition; for a vehicle fleet the installed base keeps moving through used imports that the register records only as they arrive, so the 2027 base is the register plus the arrival stream its own registration dates show, and replacing it with the register's own sum moves two to three lots off the Mira city car.
Swap: "the slots each platform needs once the used imports the register shows arriving, nine years old and past their pack warranty, are carried into 2027" put into a hospital's infusion pumps or a utility's distribution transformers loses its mechanism: no hospital takes in used pumps from abroad at a steady rate past their warranty, no transformer fleet grows by imported second-hand units, and no asset register carries both an asset's first service anywhere and its arrival date here.
Layer 1: the RDW open-data extract with Dutch column names, YYYYMMDD dates and two registration dates per car; trade names that vary by variant and spelling; a replacement log merged from the OEMs' EU-wide dealer systems and Voltwerk's own, with the country and the cover under which each pack was fitted; life curves published as cumulative shares by whole year of pack age, one OEM column per platform and Voltwerk's own refurbished-pack curve; nothing is placed to be caught.
Layer 2: estimand_identification: the population whose packs fall due in 2027 is every car in use in the Netherlands in 2027, which the register on 30 June 2026 holds only in part; the used imports still to arrive are projected from the register's own arrival history (their rate and their age and pack state at arrival) and carried at their conditional chance of failing outside cover after they arrive.
Decisive step: carrying the used imports the register shows arriving every month into the 2027 forecast, each at the pack it arrives with and its chance of failing in 2027 outside warranty once it is here, rather than taking the register on 30 June 2026 as the population whose packs fall due in 2027.
Not semantics: every field reads as what it is (first admission, first Dutch registration, each replacement's date, country, cover and pack), the warranty terms are read correctly from first admission, and the register is complete for its own date; no field, code or definition says that cars will arrive after the snapshot, and the analyst who reads all of it correctly still sums over the register.
Not rule application: the tooling plan files the WHAT (the out-of-warranty replacements falling due on each platform in the Netherlands in calendar 2027, a car in use taken to stay in use, the curves read by straight lines) and says nothing about which cars to forecast; the enumerated family on the Mira's slots runs from 1,100 (the register alone) through 1,200 (either half of the arrival stream) to 1,250, three lots, the standing family (every construction that carries the full stream, replayed from the last 6, 12 or 24 months at each import's pack at arrival) agrees on all five platforms at a worst remainder margin of 0.21 lots, and nothing filed rules the register-only route out: only the register's own arrival dates do, which no sort or group-by of the register reaches.
Analyst test: A strong analyst who reads every file correctly forecasts 2027 from the 30 June 2026 register alone (each car's current pack from the log, its conditional chance of failing outside cover from the curves) and tools Volta 1,150, Kestrel 300, Plover 350, Mira 1,100, Tern 100 against the golden Volta 1,050, Kestrel 250, Plover 350, Mira 1,250, Tern 100 (prototype seed; checks.py will run both on the clean world and on the pack).
Estimand: each platform's out-of-warranty traction-battery replacements falling due in the Netherlands in calendar 2027 (a pack failing while its car is in use in the Netherlands, outside the OEM's eight years from first admission or Voltwerk's two years from a refurbished fit), with the 3,000 slots split in lots of 50 in proportion by largest remainder (`voltwerk_tooling_plan_2027.md` sections 2 and 3).
No decoy: nothing is planted: the register is the RDW's as it stood on 30 June 2026, the log the recorded replacements, the curves the OEMs' and Voltwerk's own; no memo, draft or figure argues for a split, no file ranks the platforms, and no stakeholder figure is set up to be overturned.
No certified replay: no certified or published figure sits in the pack; no file states a forecast, a past split or a past year's replacement count to be reproduced, and the method is not recovered from any figure.
Lesson: an installed-base forecast is a forecast of the base too; when the roll it is summed over is a snapshot of a base that keeps taking in old units, the units still to arrive are part of the population, and the roll's own arrival dates are what show them; no build on file forecasts a population's units still to arrive from the roll's own arrival history.

## Tried and rejected

- Pack 1 of the prototype (2026-10-24, seed 3061) hid each imported car's pack history before its Dutch registration (the log started at the Dutch registration), so a solver could not know an import's pack state and both probes repaired it differently (sonnet calibrated the imports down by 0.74, opus modelled the abroad replacements in expectation): a fork, not the stump. The log is now the OEMs' EU-wide record plus Voltwerk's own, and a car whose pack failed outside warranty abroad is scrapped there and never imported.
- The prototype's truth carried a scrappage and export rate that no file shows; the forecast now takes a car in use to stay in use through 2027, filed in the tooling plan.
- The first cut's imports arrived about six years old, inside the eight-year warranty, and moved the call by three per cent and one lot; the Mira city car is now the platform kept alive by old imports from abroad, as old cheap EVs are.
- A refurbished pack failing inside Voltwerk's two years was logged as an OEM warranty fit in the prototype (99 rows): fixed, a Voltwerk-warranty failure takes another refurbished pack under Voltwerk's cover.
