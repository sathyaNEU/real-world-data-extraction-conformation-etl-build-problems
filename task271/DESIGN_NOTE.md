## Literal reader

Executed word for word, the criteria file each group's risk as the chance that a woman who has inherited a variant in the group is diagnosed with a first breast cancer before 70 if she lives to 70, measured on the probands' mothers, sisters and daughters, living or dead, the probands and half-sisters left out; the terms define a carrier as a person who has inherited the family's variant. The records give every one of those 9,179 women's histories and the genotypes of the 3,170 who were tested; for the other 6,009 the literal estimand still counts the ones who carry the variant, which no field records. Each of them carries it with probability one half, by inheritance, and that is the one piece of evidence on them: the kin-cohort reads the carriers' risk as twice the relatives' risk less the population's, and the Mendelian likelihood reads each untested woman's history under both genotypes at even odds. Kaplan-Meier on the relatives whose report shows the variant estimates the same quantity only if testing were unrelated to history, which the records refute: among the living relatives of the late groups 43 to 80 percent of those with breast cancer were tested against 13 to 17 percent of those without. The family closes on the two PALB2 groups (Ladder).
