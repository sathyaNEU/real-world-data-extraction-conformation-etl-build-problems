# task144: Grachtwerk's Sluis spotlight for November 2026 (architecture 1)

Private, never ships. A new build (wave 3), numbered from seed FC15. The seed (a developer platform's newsletter
spotlight for one new repository, picked by its expected external stars in its first 90 days public with the
spotlight's effect included) was read at its header and Situation only. Its world, decision and quantity are kept.
Its mechanism (the spotlight's extra stars going only to projects readers can install in one step) is a withheld rule
recovered from a pilot and is not kept. The pitfall is re-found from how a code-hosting platform's stars come to
exist: every star is shown in the starrer's followers' home feeds, and followers star from the feed card at a rate the
repository sets.

**World.** Grachtwerk is a code-hosting platform based in Amsterdam with developer accounts worldwide. Its home feed

button on the card. Sluis is Grachtwerk's monthly developer newsletter, sent on the first Tuesday of the month to

measure the spotlight); in October 2026 the editorial team ended the lottery, and the featuring memo now sends the

## Default path

1. Read the featuring memo: the spotlight goes to the shortlisted repository with the most expected external stars in
   its first 90 days public, the spotlight's effect included; an external star is an account's first star of the
   repository, from an account that is neither a bot nor a member of the owning organisation at the time.
2. Count each of the seven repositories' external stars by day since it went public from the star events.
3. Forecast each one's organic external stars to its 90th day from its trajectory, with the lottery's runners-up
   (shortlisted, listed, not spotlighted) as the reference at the same age.
4. Take the spotlight's lift from the lottery: each issue's spotlighted repository against its six runners-up (drawn by
   lot), the average gain in 90-day external stars, scaled to the November issue's reads.
5. Add the average lift to each repository's forecast and spotlight the largest.

## Tried and rejected

- The seed's mechanism (the spotlight's extra stars only for projects installable in one step, recovered from the
  pilot): a withheld rule with a pilot that recovers it, banned as the decisive step (10/10 rule 3).
- The spotlight's in-window gain as adoption pulled forward from after day 90 (a generalised Bass time shift, the
  gain being the repository's own stars in the days just past its 90th): rejected at the draw because the gain is a
  smooth function of each repository's visible trajectory, so pilot analogs or a regression on pre-spotlight pace reach
  it, and its carrier (the repository's own adoption rate at the window's end) sits next to task136's own-pace carrier.
- A carrier search over the lottery for the spotlight's lift (the basis on which past spotlights agree: per star, per
  visitor, per recent pace): rejected as task136's architecture (an intervention's increment carried on the basis
  past runs agree on).
- A filed organic projection as the baseline, leaving only the spotlight's increment to build: rejected as task136's
  template (a filed base forecast plus an intervention's increment transferred to each candidate).
- A bounded audience (a plugin's host-framework developers saturating its stars): rejected because at realistic
  penetration (under a fifth of an ecosystem in 90 days) saturation moves a 90-day total by a few per cent only, and
  the pool's definition is a fork.
- Delayed stars from recent visitors still deciding: rejected as task134's driver (a surge filling a pool not yet in
  its conversion window) and task77 v1's stock in flight.
- Stars from accounts the platform later closes, nowcast by account age: rejected because it reads as fake stars
  caught, a planted defect in a reviewer's eyes.
- Point-in-time features against attributes exported as a current snapshot: rejected as data semantics (the answer
  flips on reading that a column is a snapshot) and a generic leakage lesson that fails the swap test.
