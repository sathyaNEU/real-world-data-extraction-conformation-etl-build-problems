## Three rules

Task type: General Statistics & Data Science, `missing-data-censoring-measurement-error`: a share over time estimated from measurements whose missingness depends on a state recorded in another file; it serves Descriptive & Distribution Analysis (each hub's share of peak time below the standard and its distribution across the hubs against the line).
Domain test: the deciding quantity is each hub's share of weekday peak time below 80 per cent of plan, a missing-data estimate in which the completed tests are missing at random only given the node's utilisation in the quarter-hour; the field conditions on that auxiliary record (each household's tests re-weighted to its node's full peak load distribution, or the load at which its connection drops under 80 per cent located and the node's peak quarter-hours above it counted), where the share of tests is the complete-case estimate and the hour weighting conditions on the wrong variable.
Swap: with a fixed sensor reading every quarter-hour in place of the panel unit (a water meter, a ward's call-bell log) the sentence still parses but nothing is missing and the share of readings is the answer; with the share of completed tests in place of the missing-data estimate, the generic summary any field would compute, the call changes. The task is the missingness mechanism, so swapping either the probe or the estimate empties it.
Layer 1: Tollard's utilisation filing stamps intervals in UTC and the panel's export in local time with its offset, across the 1 November clock change; plan changes are effective-dated in the subscription history, where two legacy plans were migrated in April 2025; a failed measurement unit is replaced under a new unit id at the same address after a gap of one to four days; households join the panel after the window opens or withdraw before it closes, and three households moved during the window and had their units removed; nodes split before the window carry the parent node in the address table up to the split and the child after it (the window's own splits fall on nodes serving no panel household); the panel's collector re-uploads a unit's buffered batch after an outage, so some tests arrive twice; the panel export gives throughput in kbit/s and the plan catalogue advertised speeds in Mbit/s. Each is written by the system that holds it; none is placed to be caught, and each is asserted at stage 3 (R2 LAYER1 and the SF rows in checks.py) to leave the call where it is when mishandled.
Layer 2: `estimation_fit`, own:missing at random given an auxiliary utilisation record: the share of completed tests, overall, by hour or by night, describes a lighter load than the peak holds; conditioning on the node's utilisation flips the call.
Decisive step: condition each household's weekday peak tests on its node's utilisation in the tested quarter-hour and carry that to every quarter-hour of the peak, either by re-weighting the tests to the node's full peak load distribution or by locating the load at which the household's connection falls under 80 per cent of plan and counting the node's peak quarter-hours above it.
Not semantics: the methods note's skip rule, read correctly, says when a test is skipped, not whether skipping is related to speed; every field, code, unit and definition read correctly (throughput, plan speeds, the peak's hours, the hub of each node, the plan in force) leaves the share of tests, overall or hour by hour, on the default set, and the relation between skipping and congestion appears only when the node loads at the completed tests are set against the node's full peak record, which no field states.
Not rule application: the Schedule files the estimand (every interval of every weekday evening peak in the window, every panel household served from the hub counted equally, the plan in force on the day) and nothing on estimating the speed in the intervals no test reached. The enumerated family (the share of tests; weighted by hour; weighted by night; every test not run counted a failure; the constructions conditioned on the node's load) names four distinct designated sets on the build (Carrow Mills and Dunfield; those two and Greyhaven; all eight; the golden four); the standing family, the constructions conditioned on the node's utilisation (the load above which the connection falls under the standard, per plan; 20-bin and 10-bin re-weighting with rates non-decreasing in load; an isotonic speed curve per plan), names one set, the first three at the same whole per cent on every hub and within 0.4 points of each other, every member 2.03 points or more from the line. What rules the others out is the comparison of the node loads in the tested intervals with the node's full weekday peak record (EVIDENCE in `checks.py`: lighter in all eight hubs, Greyhaven 65.3 against 68.0 per cent), which no filed clause makes and no group-by on the test file performs.
Analyst test: a strong analyst who reads every field correctly, keeps the weekday 7 to 11 pm tests of the window and takes each hub's share of those tests below 80 per cent of the household's plan speed (or weights each evening hour equally) files two hubs, Carrow Mills and Dunfield, because the units skip a test while the household's own line is busy and household use rises on the same nights the hub's nodes congest; executed on the generator's clean truth in `checks.py` (R3a ANALYST), where the decisive step alone files the golden four (R3b DECISIVE).
Estimand: Schedule 4 (Network Performance) of the franchise agreement, 3.1 and 3.2 with the definitions in 1.2 to 1.8 (franchise_network_performance_schedule.pdf): a hub is designated when, over the 2026 measurement window (14 September to 13 November), the connections of the panel households served from it delivered less than 80 per cent of the advertised download speed of the plan in force for more than 20 per cent of the weekday evening peak, 7 pm to 11 pm local time Monday to Friday, taken over every quarter-hour of the peak for every such household, each household counted equally.
No decoy: nothing is planted to be caught: no memo, earlier estimate, provider report or draft states a hub's share or ranks the hubs; the tests, the unit log, the register, the utilisation filing and the plan catalogue are the panel's and Tollard's own records, and the 2025 close-out summary answers another question (last autumn's designations and the capacity Tollard added).
No certified replay: no certified or published figure carries a hub's 2026 share, so no method is recovered by reproducing one.
Lesson: when a probe is silenced by activity that rises with the load that degrades the outcome, the completed probes describe a lighter load than the window holds, and the share of the window is estimated by conditioning on the load's own record rather than on the clock. task273 architecture 1 taught the closed-loop form, where the outcome itself silences the probe; here the probe is silenced by a correlate, and the clock correction the field reaches for first is the one that fails.

## Literal reader

Executed word for word, Schedule 3.1 and 3.2 file each hub's share of every weekday evening peak interval of the window, for every panel household served from it, in which the household's connection delivered under 80 per cent of the advertised download speed of the plan in force. The panel's download test measures the delivered speed in the intervals a unit ran a test in; the literal estimand still asks for every interval, and the units ran no test in an hour while the household's own line stayed busy. The records refute the tests as a sample of the intervals: in every hub the tested intervals sat at a lighter node load than the hub's peak intervals as a whole (EVIDENCE in `checks.py`), while a connection's speed in an interval follows its node's load there.
